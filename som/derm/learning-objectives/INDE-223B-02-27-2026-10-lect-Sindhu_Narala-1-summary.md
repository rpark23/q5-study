## Learning Objectives

1. Recognize the cutaneous clinical presentation of skin conditions such as **acanthosis nigricans**, **granuloma annulare**, and **erythema nodosum**, which may be presenting signs of internal disease
2. Recognize the cutaneous manifestations of **endocrine disorders**
3. Recognize cutaneous findings associated with various **GI disorders**
4. Recognize cutaneous findings associated with **chronic kidney disease**
5. Recognize cutaneous findings associated with **sarcoidosis** and identify the main organ systems affected by this multisystem disease
6. Recognize the cutaneous presentation of **neutrophilic disorders** such as Sweet syndrome and pyoderma gangrenosum and describe the systemic diseases that may be associated
7. Recognize the cutaneous clinical presentation of the most common **paraneoplastic skin diseases** and list their associated malignancies
8. Recognize physiologic, cutaneous changes in **pregnancy**
9. Recognize the clinical presentation of **hidradenitis suppurativa** and understand medical co-morbidities that disproportionately affect this patient population

## Objective 1 & 2: Cutaneous Manifestations of Endocrine Disorders

### Thyroid Disease

| Finding | Details |
|---|---|
| **Vitiligo** | Autoimmune depigmentation; associated with thyroid dysfunction in 15% |
| **Pretibial myxedema** | Excess mucin deposition; deep red/brown infiltrated plaques on shins |
| **Thyroid acropachy** | Swelling of distal digits with overgrown nail plates; clubbing |
| **Urticaria/dermatographism** | Skin writing; hive-like response to pressure |
| **Pruritus** | Generalized itch |
| **Hyperpigmentation** | Localized or generalized |
| **Hair changes** | Fine, thin, diffuse alopecia; increased alopecia areata |
| **Nail changes** | Onycholysis, koilonychia |

### Vitiligo

- **Morphology:** Well-demarcated **depigmented** macules and patches (complete depigmentation due to loss of melanocytes)
- **Etiology:** Unknown; possibly immune destruction of melanocytes via autoantibodies
- **Distribution:** Favors periorificial, genital, acral, and joint areas (prone to repetitive trauma)
- **Re-pigmentation** occurs in a **perifollicular pattern**
- Affects all Fitzpatrick skin types; Fitzpatrick types IV-VI report greater impact on daily life (VALIANT Study)
- **Workup:** Check serum TSH, anti-TPO, anti-thyroglobulin to screen for autoimmune thyroid disease

### Cutaneous Manifestations of Diabetes Mellitus

| Condition | Key Features |
|---|---|
| **Diabetic dermopathy** | Brown macules/patches on shins; most common (50% of pts); possibly trauma-related; may correlate with microangiopathic disease |
| **Necrobiosis lipoidica diabeticorum** | Yellow/red/orange atrophic plaques +/- ulceration on shins; associated with DM in 11-65% |
| **Bullous diabeticorum** | Sudden-onset painless tense bullae on normal skin, usually acral (legs) |
| **Granuloma annulare** | Annular plaques/papules; usually benign, sometimes associated with DM |
| **Acanthosis nigricans** | Velvety hyperpigmented plaques; associated with hyperinsulinemia/insulin resistance |

### Granuloma Annulare

- **Morphology:** Annular plaques or papules
- **Distribution:** Hands and arms most common
- Usually benign, localized, and **self-limited** (sometimes generalized)
- Occurs in otherwise healthy individuals; etiology unknown
- **Associations:**
  - Usually none
  - Sometimes **diabetes mellitus**
  - Rarely paraneoplastic: solid organ tumors, Hodgkin disease, Non-Hodgkin lymphoma

### Acanthosis Nigricans

- **Morphology:** Symmetric, hyperpigmented, **velvety plaques**
- **Distribution:** Most commonly axillae, groin, posterior neck
- Pathogenesis: Possible stimulation of growth factors in keratinocytes leading to epidermal hyperplasia
- Most commonly **benign**: related to **hyperinsulinemia/insulin resistance/diabetes**
- Common in Native Americans and Americans of African descent (25-fold increased risk)
- **Atypical distribution** (palmar/"tripe palms," mucosal) → consider internal malignancy, most commonly **GI adenocarcinoma**
- Can also be paraneoplastic (see Objective 7)

## Objective 3: Cutaneous Findings in GI Disorders

### Liver Disease

- **Pathophysiology:** Liver dysfunction → impaired estrogen metabolism → increased vessel dilation

| Finding | Description |
|---|---|
| **Spider angiomas** | Central arteriole with radiating small vessels |
| **Telangiectasias** | Dilated superficial blood vessels |
| **Palmar erythema** | Erythema of the palms |
| **Dilated abdominal wall veins** | Caput medusae |
| **Terry's nails** | White nail bed |
| **Sparse hair** | Axillary, pubic, pectoral |
| **Gynecomastia** | From estrogen excess |
| **Jaundice** | Bilirubin deposition |

### Dermatitis Herpetiformis

- **Morphology:** Pruritic papules, vesicles, and bullae on elbows, knees, lower mid back, nape of neck
- Often only erosions visible because it is so itchy
- **Immunofluorescence:** IgA at dermal papillae tips
- **Associated with:** Celiac disease
- Covered in detail in the Autoimmune Bullous lecture

### Erythema Nodosum

- **Pathophysiology:** Inflammation of subcutaneous fat (panniculitis)
- **Morphology:** Tender erythematous nodules, usually on **anterior shins**
- Can present with associated arthralgias
- **Associations:** Sarcoidosis, Crohn's disease, streptococcal infections, TB, coccidioidomycosis, histoplasmosis, drugs

### Pyoderma Gangrenosum

- **Neutrophilic disorder** (neutrophil dysfunction)
- **Morphology:** Small tender papule or pustule → rapidly evolves (24-48 hrs) into **painful ulceration** with **violaceous, undermined borders**; heals with **cribriform scarring**
- **Pathergy:** Lesions can occur at sites of trauma
- **Systemic illness in 50-70%:**
  - IBD: Ulcerative colitis, Crohn's disease
  - Hematologic diseases (e.g., leukemia)
  - Connective tissue disease, rheumatoid arthritis

## Objective 4: Cutaneous Findings in Chronic Kidney Disease

### Uremic Pruritus

- Also called **chronic kidney disease-associated pruritus**
- **Prevalence:** 10-90% of patients with CKD/ESRD; more common in ESRD on dialysis
- Nearly 1/5 patients are very much or extremely bothered
- Significant impact on quality of life: poor sleep, depression, increased risk of secondary infection
- **Pathophysiology:** Unclear; complex interplay of cutaneous biology, nervous system, immune system, neurotransmitters, opioids
- **Clinical features** (secondary to scratching):
  - Angulated linear erosions ("outside job")
  - **Prurigo nodules** (thickened lichenified papules)
  - Upper back, scalp, legs (areas patients can reach)
  - Worse at night
- **Differential:** Prurigo nodularis vs. lichen planus

### Calciphylaxis

- Most commonly in patients with **ESRD on dialysis**; non-uremic cases make up 15-20%
- **Mortality:** 40-80% at one year (due to secondary infection and sepsis from open wounds)
- **Pathophysiology:** Unclear; calcification of small blood vessels → occlusion → ischemia → tissue death
- **Risk factors:** Female gender, obesity, age >60, hyperphosphatemia, DM, hyperparathyroidism, calcium/vitamin D exposure, coumadin, iron overload, low albumin
- **Clinical presentation:**
  - Early: Painful erythema/induration or painful "bruise" (pain out of proportion to exam)
  - Progresses to **retiform purpura** (branching, non-blanching purple patches with jagged edges)
  - **Skin ulcers and black eschars**
  - Central distribution: abdomen, buttocks, thighs, breasts
  - Peripheral: hands, feet, penis
- **Treatment:** Multidisciplinary approach
  - Optimize dialysis
  - Review potential triggers (coumadin, iron)
  - Sodium thiosulfate IV
  - Wound care, pain control, monitor for infection

## Objective 5: Cutaneous Findings in Sarcoidosis

### Overview of Sarcoidosis

- **Multisystem granulomatous disease** affecting skin, lungs, joints, lymph nodes, eyes, heart, liver, kidneys
- In US: F > M; higher prevalence in skin of color
- **Pathogenesis:** Genetic susceptibility + triggering antigen → T-cell and macrophage activation (hyperactive cell-mediated immunity) → granuloma formation
- Income, education, health insurance, and occupational/environmental exposures all contribute to disease variance

### Cutaneous Manifestations (25% of patients)

- **Morphology:** Red-brown to violaceous papules and plaques
- **Distribution:** Symmetric; favors nose (alae), perioral, periocular, neck, upper trunk, extremities
- Predilection for **sites of trauma** (including within tattoos)
- 25% of patients with cutaneous sarcoidosis have NO systemic disease

#### Lupus Pernio
- A specific form of cutaneous sarcoidosis
- Indurated papules on nose, lips, cheeks, ears → can result in scarring
- **Note:** Despite the name, has nothing to do with lupus

### Systemic Manifestations

| System | Finding |
|---|---|
| Pulmonary (90%) | SOB, non-productive cough, chest pain; hilar and paratracheal lymphadenopathy |
| Eyes | Uveitis |
| Liver | Hepatomegaly |
| Heart | Cardiomegaly, arrhythmias, CHF |
| Nervous system | Neuropathies (e.g., Bell's Palsy) |
| Musculoskeletal | Arthritis, weakness, myopathy |
| Kidneys | Nephrolithiasis, hypercalciuria |
| Labs | Hypercalcemia, lymphopenia, eosinophilia |

### Work-up

- **Skin biopsy:** Non-caseating granulomas with sparse/absent lymphocytic inflammation ("naked granulomas") composed of epithelioid histiocytes
- Chest X-ray and high-resolution chest CT

## Objective 6: Neutrophilic Disorders

### Sweet Syndrome (Acute Febrile Neutrophilic Dermatosis)

- **Morphology:** Abrupt tender, **red-purple edematous**, sometimes vesiculating papules and plaques ("juicy" appearance)
- **Distribution:** Face, neck, upper extremities
- **Pathergy:** Lesions may occur at sites of skin trauma (e.g., IV insertion sites)
- **Reactive inflammatory disorder** associated with:
  - Hematologic malignancies (**AML most commonly**, in 20%)
  - Medications (G-CSF)
  - IBD, infections
- **Systemic symptoms:** Fever, headaches, myalgias, arthralgias
- **Extracutaneous:** Ocular (most common), pulmonary, GI tract, CNS, kidney, heart
- **Labs:** Neutrophilia

### Pyoderma Gangrenosum

(See also Objective 3 above)

- Key distinguishing features: violaceous undermined border, pathergy, painful ulceration
- Associated systemic diseases: IBD, hematologic malignancy, connective tissue disease

## Objective 7: Paraneoplastic Skin Diseases

### Overview

Certain skin conditions can be the presenting sign of an underlying malignancy.

| Condition | Associated Malignancy |
|---|---|
| **Sweet Syndrome** | AML (most common), other hematologic malignancies |
| **Acanthosis nigricans** (atypical/mucosal/"tripe palms") | GI adenocarcinoma (most common) |
| **Erythema gyratum repens** | Lung, esophageal, breast cancer (80%); TB, idiopathic (20%) |
| **Granuloma annulare** (generalized) | Solid organ tumors, Hodgkin disease, Non-Hodgkin lymphoma |

### Acanthosis Nigricans (Paraneoplastic Form)

- Atypical distributions raise concern: **palmar (tripe palms)** and **mucosal** involvement
- Most commonly associated with **GI adenocarcinoma**

### Erythema Gyratum Repens

- Rare **figurate erythema** (raised erythematous ring-shaped lesions)
- Classic morphology: multiple annular/polycyclic erythematous plaques with trailing scale; **"wood grain, ring within rings" pattern**
- Advances rapidly (up to 1 cm/day); can see vesicles/bullae; typically spares hands/feet
- **80% paraneoplastic** (lung, esophageal, breast cancer)
- Pathogenesis: Immune response to underlying malignancy or infection

## Objective 8: Physiologic Cutaneous Changes in Pregnancy

| Finding | Description |
|---|---|
| **Striae** | Stretch marks on abdomen |
| **Pyogenic granuloma** | Pedunculated, friable vascular papule |
| **Postpartum telogen effluvium** | Diffuse thinning of the scalp after delivery |
| **Linear nigra** | Linear hyperpigmented patch on abdomen |
| **Melasma** | "Moth eaten" hyperpigmented patches in sun-exposed areas (cheeks, forehead, chin, upper lip) |

### Melasma

- **Pathophysiology:** Worsened by increased **estrogen** states (pregnancy, OCP use, gender confirmation therapy for transfeminine patients) and UV exposure
- Also known as "mask of pregnancy" (Chloasma)
- Benign but significant cosmetic distress
- **Treatment:** Diligent sun protection (sunscreen, protective clothing, sun avoidance); bleaching creams

## Objective 9: Hidradenitis Suppurativa (HS)

### Overview

- Chronic inflammatory condition characterized by **recurrent painful abscesses, nodules, and draining sinus tracts** in intertriginous areas
- Women 3x more affected than men; starts after puberty (peaks in 20-40s)
- Incidence ~1%; family history in 30-40%
- African Americans disproportionately affected in the US
- **Delay in diagnosis of 7-10 years**

### Pathogenesis

- **NOT** a disorder of apocrine gland dysfunction (previously thought)
- Disorder of the **folliculopilosebaceous unit**
- Mechanism: Follicular hyperkeratosis/plugging → dilatation → follicular rupture → inflammatory response → chronic inflammation with architectural tissue changes
- Contributing factors: Smoking, obesity, hormones, heat exposure (increased sweating → friction → follicular plugging)

### Diagnosis

- **Clinical diagnosis** (no biopsy required unless in question)
- Diagnostic criteria:
  1. **>2 flares in 6 months**
  2. Flares located in **skin folds** (intertriginous areas: axillae, inguinal, buttocks)
  3. Flares comprised of inflammatory nodules, abscesses, sinus tracts/tunnels

### Skin Lesions Seen in HS

| Lesion | Description |
|---|---|
| **Nodules** | Deep-seated, tender, round-topped |
| **Abscesses** | Fluctuant, tender, pus-filled collections |
| **Sinus tracts/tunnels** | Hollow tubes under the skin, often interconnected |
| **Double-headed comedones** | Two comedones side-by-side ("tombstone comedones"); represent old, burnt-out lesions |
| **Rope-like/hypertrophic scarring** | Raised firm scars; can cause contractures in the axilla |
| **Atrophic scarring** | Indented hyperpigmented patches |

Lesions are accompanied by pain, itch, drainage, and malodor.

### Quality of Life Impact

- **Pain:** >90% reported pain in the past week
- **Mental health:** 1 in 4 with depression, 1 in 5 with anxiety; >2-fold higher suicide rate; 2-fold higher substance use disorders; sexual dysfunction in 50-60%
- **Work:** Higher unemployment, lower income; 15% disabled or unable to work

### Comorbidities (Annual Screening Recommended)

| Comorbidity | Prevalence in HS |
|---|---|
| Obesity | 75% |
| Metabolic Syndrome | 50% |
| Diabetes | 30% |
| IBD (risk 9-fold higher) | Significant |
| PCOS | 9% |
| Tobacco use | 70-75% |
| Cardiovascular disease | Elevated |
| Spondylarthritis | 3.7% |
| Down Syndrome | Increased (screen HS pts) |
| Anxiety/Depression/Suicidality | Elevated |

## Summary Take-Home Points

- **Vitiligo** = depigmentation from loss of melanocytes; screen for autoimmune thyroid disease (TSH, anti-TPO)
- **Acanthosis nigricans** = velvety hyperpigmented plaques in axillae/neck → insulin resistance; atypical/mucosal distribution → GI malignancy
- **Pyoderma gangrenosum** = painful ulcer with violaceous undermined border + pathergy; associated with IBD and hematologic malignancy
- **Calciphylaxis** = extremely painful retiform purpura/ulceration in ESRD patients; 40-80% one-year mortality
- **Sarcoidosis** = non-caseating ("naked") granulomas in skin; 90% have pulmonary involvement
- **Sweet Syndrome** = juicy red-purple edematous plaques with fever + pathergy; associated with AML
- **Erythema gyratum repens** = wood grain "ring within ring" pattern; 80% paraneoplastic
- **Melasma** = moth-eaten facial hyperpigmentation worsened by estrogen and UV; treat with strict sun protection
- **Hidradenitis suppurativa** = recurrent abscesses/sinus tracts in skin folds; clinical diagnosis; screen annually for metabolic comorbidities
