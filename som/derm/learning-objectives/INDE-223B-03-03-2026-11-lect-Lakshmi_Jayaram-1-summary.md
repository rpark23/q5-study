## Objective 1: Define Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE)

SLE is defined by two key features:

1. A **systemic inflammatory disease** directed against:
   - Skin and joints
   - Serosal surfaces
   - CNS
   - Kidney
   - Blood (CBC abnormalities)

2. With **specific immunologic abnormalities**:
   - **ANA**, **anti-dsDNA**, **anti-Sm**, low **C3/C4**, **antiphospholipid antibodies**
   - Note: ESR and CRP are **not** specific immunologic abnormalities

### Pathogenesis

1. Self cell is misrecognized as a foreign antigen
2. T cells stimulate B cells to produce **autoantibodies**
3. Autoantibodies and antigens form **immune complexes**
4. Immune complexes attack organs

### Organs Targeted

| Organ | Manifestation |
|---|---|
| Skin | Malar rash, discoid rash, subacute cutaneous, oral ulcers |
| Joints | Tender, painful, swollen |
| Serosal surfaces | Pleuro-pericarditis |
| Systemic | Fever |
| Kidney | Glomerulonephritis |
| Brain | Cerebritis |
| Bone marrow | Low platelets, low WBC, hemolytic anemia |

### Incidence and Demographics

- Peak incidence in women aged **20–49**
- Incidence in females is significantly higher than males
- Prevalence is **5–15x more common in women** than men
- Prevalence is **4x higher** in African Americans than Whites
- Outcomes and prevalence worse in African Americans, APIs, Hispanics, and Asians than Whites

## Objective 2: Understand Features of SLE Through History and Physical Examination

> Key point: Diagnosis of rheumatic diseases is based on **clinical elements**. Labs are supportive but not diagnostic. A positive ANA + positive dsDNA **does not diagnose SLE** unless there is at least one distinctive clinical feature.

### Most Common Presenting Manifestations

| Manifestation | % at Presentation |
|---|---|
| Malar or Discoid Rash | 59% |
| Inflammatory Arthritis | 57% |
| Pleuro-pericarditis | 40% |
| Fever | 34% |
| Alopecia | 30% |
| Oral Ulcers | 22% |
| Nephritis | 13% |
| Hematologic (thrombocytopenia, leukopenia, anemia) | 10% |

### History: Questions to Ask

- Distinctive rash? Alopecia? Frequent mouth ulcers?
- Painful, tender, swollen joints?
- Unexplained chronic fever?
- Pleuritic chest pain or pleuro-pericarditis?
- Unexplained proteinuria/hematuria or increased creatinine?
- Leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, hemolytic anemia?

### Physical Exam: Organ-by-Organ

| System | Exam Findings |
|---|---|
| Skin | Alopecia, malar rash, discoid rash, SCLE, oral ulcers |
| Joints | Tender, swollen hands/wrists/knees (MCPs, PIPs, wrists) |
| Serosal | Pleural/pericardial rub, splinting |
| Kidney | Edema |
| CNS | Neuro exam abnormalities |
| Bone Marrow | Petechia, splenomegaly |
| Systemic | Fever (T > 37 degrees) |

### Articular Manifestations

- Painful, tender, and **swollen** joints
- Peripheral, **small joints** (MCPs, PIPs, wrists)
- **Non-erosive** (does not destroy bone, unlike RA)

### Other Clinical Domains

- **Serositis:** Pleuritis, pericarditis, rarely peritonitis
- **Neurologic:** Delirium, psychosis, seizures, and many other central and peripheral manifestations
- **Renal and Hematologic:** Discussed under Objective 4

## Objective 3: Identify Dermatologic Manifestations of SLE

Five key dermatologic manifestations:

### 1. Malar Rash ("Butterfly Rash")
- Erythema and scaling over **nose and cheeks**
- **Spares nasolabial folds** (key distinguishing feature from rosacea)
- Red/purple, fixed, raised
- **No pain or itch**
- Note: "Malar" refers only to location; does not specify cause. The lupus malar rash is fixed and spares the nasolabial folds.

### 2. Discoid Rash (Discoid Lupus Erythematosus, DLE / Chronic Cutaneous Lupus Erythematosus)
- Red **patches** with scaling, **follicular plugging**, and **scarring**
- Hypo- or hyperpigmented scarring papules and plaques
- Affects scalp, face (bridge of nose, lips, ears)
- Photosensitive eruption
- **Scarring alopecia** can result
- **20%** of SLE patients have these lesions
- **5–10%** of isolated DLE patients progress to SLE (workup needed to exclude SLE)
- **Histopathology:** Interface dermatitis with lymphocytic attack at the dermal-epidermal junction (DEJ), follicular plugging (keratin plug), vacuolar degeneration of basal cells, thickened basement membrane, dense perivascular inflammatory infiltrate, periadnexal inflammatory infiltrate, increased dermal mucin

### 3. Non-Scarring Alopecia
- Hair follicle remains **intact** and capable of regrowing hair
- Hair shaft falls out or thins
- Potentially **reversible** (follicle structure preserved)
- Included as a classification criterion

### 4. Subacute Cutaneous Lupus Erythematosus (SCLE)
- **Photosensitive eruption** (UVB > UVA)
- **Annular scaly plaques** with central clearing on sun-exposed skin
- Commonly **drug-induced** (HCTZ, terbinafine, CCBs, ACE inhibitors, NSAIDs — 35% of cases)
- **Anti-(SSA)Ro positive** in 70%
- 10–15% go on to develop systemic internal disease
- Skin biopsy suggestive but not specific for lupus

### 5. Oral Ulceration
- Located on palate, lips, gingiva, buccal mucosa
- Characteristically **painless**
- Must occur **frequently** (at least once a month) to count as a diagnostic criterion

### Summary of Dermatologic Manifestations
1. Non-scarring alopecia
2. Malar rash
3. Discoid rash
4. Subacute cutaneous lupus erythematosus (SCLE)
5. Frequent oral ulcers

> Note: **Photosensitivity** is often present in lupus but is **not** a specific diagnostic criterion.

### Acute Cutaneous Lupus
- Localized (malar area) or generalized (widespread maculopapular rash on chest, arms, upper back)
- Transient: hours to days; photosensitive
- Non-scarring
- Associated with systemic transition to nephritis and serositis risk

## Objective 4: Understand Lab Tests and Diagnostic Criteria for SLE

### Anti-Nuclear Antibody (ANA)

- **What it is:** Antibodies to cell nucleus components
- **Positive threshold:** ≥ 1:80 titer
- **Titer or pattern important?** No
- **Sensitivity for SLE:** ~100% (a negative ANA rules out SLE per EULAR/ACR 2019)
- **Specificity for SLE:** 20–50% (many false positives)
- **Key message:** Low clinical probability + positive ANA = likely **false positive**
- You **cannot diagnose SLE on an ANA alone**

### ANA Interpretation

| Result | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Negative | SLE ruled out (cannot enter the diagnostic algorithm) |
| Positive | PPV depends on pre-test probability; if ACR/EULAR score ≥ 10 = SLE; if < 10, look for other causes |

### Other Causes of a Positive ANA
1. **Other rheumatic diseases** (most common: RA, Sjogren's; also myositis, scleroderma, vasculitis)
2. **Organ-specific autoimmune disease** (Hashimoto's, autoimmune hepatitis, thrombocytopenia, hemolytic anemia, MS, sarcoidosis)
3. **Drugs** (hydralazine, ACE inhibitors, INH, anti-epileptics, proton pump inhibitors, beta blockers — anti-histone often positive)
4. **Infection** (EBV, CMV, Strep, endocarditis)
5. **No explanation** (family history, aging)

### Other Immunologic Criteria (Order Only If ANA Positive)

- **Anti-Smith (Sm):** Sensitivity 30% (10–40%), Specificity 94% (84–99%)
- **Anti-dsDNA:** Sensitivity 56% (29–78%), Specificity 93% (83–98%) — very specific, may be absent without nephritis
- **C3, C4** (low complement)
- **Antiphospholipid antibodies (APL):**
  - Beta-2 Glycoprotein IgG > 40 units
  - Lupus anticoagulant
  - Anti-cardiolipin antibodies IgG > 40 GPL

> Both anti-Sm and anti-dsDNA can be present in other autoimmune rheumatic diseases (vasculitis, myositis).

### ANA Staining Patterns (for reference)

| Pattern | Associated Antibodies | Disease |
|---|---|---|
| Homogeneous | Anti-dsDNA, anti-histone | SLE, drug-induced lupus |
| Speckled | Anti-U1 RNP, Sm, SSA (Ro), SSB (La) | Sjogren's |
| Nucleolar | Anti-fibrillarin, RNA polymerase I/III | Scleroderma, myositis |
| Anticentromere | Anti-centromere | Scleroderma, MCTD |

### UA and CBC: Essential Tests

- **UA and CBC are the only clinical domain labs not detectable on history and physical exam**
- Nephritis (proteinuria > 500 mg, hematuria, cellular casts, elevated creatinine) and hematologic abnormalities (thrombocytopenia, leukopenia, autoimmune hemolytic anemia) can only be identified via lab
- Cannot make or rule out a diagnosis of SLE without a UA and CBC

### ACR/EULAR Classification Criteria (2019)

**Entry criterion:** Positive ANA at ≥ 1:80 (cannot enter algorithm without this)

**Rules:**
1. ≥ 10 points = SLE diagnosis
2. ANA must be positive at ≥ 1:80
3. Criterion not counted if there is an alternative explanation
4. Each criterion counted only once
5. Must have points from at least one clinical domain
6. Only the highest criterion in a single domain counts

**Clinical Domains:**

| Domain | Criterion | Points |
|---|---|---|
| Constitutional | Fever | 2 |
| Cutaneous | Nonscarring alopecia | 2 |
| Cutaneous | Oral ulcers | 2 |
| Cutaneous | Subacute cutaneous or discoid lupus | 4 |
| Cutaneous | Acute cutaneous lupus | 6 |
| Arthritis | Synovitis in ≥ 2 joints, tenderness in ≥ 2 joints, ≥ 30 min morning stiffness | 6 |
| Neurologic | Delirium | 2 |
| Neurologic | Psychosis | 3 |
| Neurologic | Seizure | 5 |
| Serositis | Pleural or pericardial effusion | 5 |
| Serositis | Acute pericarditis | 6 |
| Hematologic | Leukopenia | 3 |
| Hematologic | Thrombocytopenia | 4 |
| Hematologic | Autoimmune hemolysis | 4 |
| Renal | Proteinuria > 0.5g/24 hr | 4 |
| Renal | Class II or V lupus nephritis | 8 |
| Renal | Class III or IV lupus nephritis | 10 |

**Immunologic Domains:**

| Domain | Criterion | Points |
|---|---|---|
| Antiphospholipid | Anticardiolipin IgG > 40 GPL or anti-β2GP1 IgG > 40 or lupus anticoagulant | 2 |
| Complement | Low C3 or low C4 | 3 |
| Complement | Low C3 and low C4 | 4 |
| Highly specific antibodies | Anti-dsDNA | 6 |
| Highly specific antibodies | Anti-Smith | 6 |

## Objective 5: Describe Common Treatment Regimens for SLE

### Keys to Treatment: Five Steps

1. **First and most important question:** Whether to use systemic corticosteroids and at what dose
2. Must assess **which organ system(s) are involved**
3. **Low dose steroids** might be indicated if severe or refractory: mucocutaneous, articular, constitutional
4. **High dose steroids** are likely indicated: renal, neuropsychiatric, pleuropericarditis
5. **Maintenance hydroxychloroquine (Plaquenil)** for all patients
6. Monitor for **drug toxicity**

### Steroid-Sparing Immunosuppressives

| Drug | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Hydroxychloroquine (Plaquenil)** | Least immunosuppressive; maintenance for ALL lupus patients; reduces flares |
| **Mycophenolate (Cellcept / Myfortic)** | Oral; commonly used especially for renal disease |
| **Azathioprine (Imuran)** | Oral; commonly used |
| **Methotrexate** | Oral |
| **Belimumab (Benlysta)** | IV or subcutaneous; inhibits B lymphocyte activating enzyme (anti-BAFF) |
| **Cyclophosphamide (Cytoxan)** | IV or oral; choice for inducing remission in acute nephritis or cerebritis |
| **Rituximab** | IV; anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody; data mixed |
| **Anifrolumab (Saphnelo)** | IV; inhibits IFNAR receptor; dampens type I interferon signature |

### Six Indications for Steroid-Sparing Immunosuppressives

1. **Hydroxychloroquine** — maintenance for **all patients**
2. Any organ involvement refractory to therapy or where steroids cannot be tapered
3. Severe neuropsychiatric disease (coma, cognitive impairment, seizures, psychosis, delirium, cerebritis)
4. Glomerulonephritis
5. Acute pleuro-pericarditis
6. Hemolytic anemia, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia

### SLE Treatment Matrix

**Mucocutaneous, Articular, Constitutional:**

| System | Step 1 | Step 2 | Step 3 | Step 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mucocutaneous | Topical steroids, HCQ | Dapsone, chloroquine, prednisone 5–15 mg | MMF, methotrexate, azathioprine | Belimumab |
| Articular | NSAID, HCQ | Steroids (low), MMF, azathioprine, methotrexate | Belimumab | Cyclophosphamide, rituximab |
| Constitutional | HCQ | Prednisone 5–10 mg | Belimumab | Rituximab |

**Serositis, Nephritis, NeuroPsych:**

| System | Step 1 | Step 2 | Step 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Serositis (pleuritis/pericarditis) | NSAID, HCQ | 0.5–1 mg/kg prednisone | Mycophenolate, methotrexate, azathioprine |
| Nephritis | ACE, prednisone 10–20 mg | 1 mg/kg prednisone, mycophenolate, azathioprine | Pulse medrol, cyclophosphamide, rituximab |
| NeuroPsych | SSRI, migraine tx | Prednisone 1 mg/kg | Pulse Medrol, cyclophosphamide, rituximab |

> If nephritis is acute with a rapidly downward trend, usually start at Step 2 or 3.

### Treatment Toxicity Summary

| Side Effect | Monitoring | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| GI (nausea, diarrhea, dyspepsia) | — | Common with many agents |
| Mucocutaneous (rash, stomatitis) | — | — |
| Fatigue | — | Especially MTX |
| Cytopenia | CBC (MTX, AZA, CYC, Cellcept) | Especially CYC, MTX, AZA |
| **Teratogenicity** | Birth control | Especially **Cellcept, MTX** — stop 3–4 months before conception |
| Retinal toxicity | Retinal exam yearly (HCQ) | Dose must be ≤ 5 mg/kg; adjust for GFR < 30 cc/min |
| Hepatotoxicity | Liver enzymes (MTX) | Avoid in liver disease or GFR < 30 cc/min |
| TPMT enzyme deficiency | TPMT enzyme before AZA | Inactivates toxic metabolites |
| Hemorrhagic cystitis, infertility | Fluid intake (CYC) | CBC routinely; avoid in pregnancy |
| Hepatitis B reactivation | HBSAg prior (Rituximab) | — |

### Drug Reference Table

| Drug | Class/Mechanism | Key Toxicities | Key Cautions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Methotrexate (MTX) | Anti-metabolite; inhibits dihydrofolate reductase | Nausea, fatigue, stomatitis, rash, cytopenia, hepatitis, infection, interstitial lung disease | Avoid in GFR < 30, liver disease, **pregnancy** |
| Azathioprine (AZA) | Anti-metabolite; inhibits purine synthesis | Cytopenia, hepatitis, infection, nausea, diarrhea, lymphoma | Check **TPMT enzyme** before starting; allopurinol increases levels |
| Cyclophosphamide (CYC) | Cytotoxic; interpolates DNA → cell apoptosis | Cytopenia, infertility, infection, carcinogenic, zoster, hemorrhagic cystitis | CBC routinely; increase fluid intake; **avoid in pregnancy** |
| Rituximab (RTX) | Monoclonal Ab; anti-CD20 → reduces B cells | Infection, low IgG, infusion reaction, Hep B reactivation, PML (rare) | Check HBSAg prior |
| Hydroxychloroquine (HCQ) | Antimalarial; blocks dendritic cell TLR receptor | **Retinal** toxicity | Dose ≤ 5 mg/kg; yearly eye exam if > 5 years of use |
| Corticosteroids (Prednisone, Medrol, etc.) | Reduce lymphocyte activation, B & T cells | Osteopenia, infection, AVN, cataracts, mood, insomnia, DM, HTN, CHF, myopathy | — |
| Mycophenolate (MMF/Cellcept) | Anti-metabolite; inhibits inosine-5'-monophosphate dehydrogenase → reduces T & B cells | Infection, diarrhea, leukopenia | **Avoid in pregnancy** |
| Belimumab (Benlysta) | Monoclonal Ab; anti-BAFF receptor → reduces B cell activity | Hypersensitivity | Avoid live vaccines |

## Objective 6: Describe Typical Outcomes of Treatment for SLE

### Survival

- Overall survival rates: **>90%**
- Survival is **lower** in patients with renal disease compared to those without
- Factors that worsen survival:
  - Older age
  - Race (African American)
  - Organ damage (renal, CNS)
  - Higher steroid dose
  - More time in active disease
  - Recurrent infection

### Long-Term Outcomes

- **Remission ≥ 5 years:** 25–45%
- **Low Disease Activity State (LLDAS):** 48%
- Outcome **worse** with: older age, major organ damage (renal, CNS), recurrent infection, higher steroid dose, chronic disease activity

### Outcome Definitions
- **LLDAS** = Lupus Low Disease Activity State
- **CROT** = Clinical Remission On Treatment
- **DORIS** = Definition Of Remission In SLE

### Social Determinants of Health

- **Gender:** Prevalence 5–15x more common in women
- **Race/Ethnicity:**
  - Prevalence 4x higher in African Americans than Whites
  - Outcomes worse in African Americans, APIs, Hispanics, and Asians
  - Outcomes worse in lower SES
- **Environment:**
  - Sun exposure and heat increase risk of flare
  - More common in urban than rural areas
- **Unconscious bias and "ableism"** — affect gender, ethnicity, culture
- **Health literacy** affects medication adherence; nonadherent patients tend to be younger, more likely Black, lower income, with more hurried physician communication

## Reference Summary Slides

### Summary: Diagnosis

**History and Physical:**
- Alopecia
- Fever
- Pleuritic chest pain/rub
- **Distinctive mucocutaneous** findings
- Synovitis
- Delirium, psychosis, seizures

**Lab and Imaging:**
- CBC — low WBC, low platelets, autoimmune hemolytic anemia (AHA)
- Antiphospholipid antibodies (beta-2 glycoprotein, anticardiolipin, lupus anticoagulant)
- UA — proteinuria, nephritis
- dsDNA, anti-Sm, C3/C4
- Echo/CXR

### Summary: Treatment

1. **Decide** whether to use corticosteroids and at what dose
2. If cerebritis, nephritis, pleuropericarditis, severe thrombocytopenia, hemolytic anemia, or neutropenia → **0.5–1 mg/kg prednisone** or equivalent, OR **1 gram IV Medrol**
3. Attain remission/control and **add a steroid-sparing immunosuppressive**
4. If mucocutaneous or articular symptoms are steroid-dependent or refractory, add a steroid-sparing immunosuppressive
5. **Maintenance hydroxychloroquine (Plaquenil) — all patients**
