## SCLERODERMA (Systemic Sclerosis / SSc)

---

### Objective 1: Define Scleroderma and Distinguish Subtypes

**Definition:** A **fibrosing disease** affecting skin, arteries, lungs, and the gastrointestinal tract.

- The underlying mechanism is **fibrosis** — increased fibroblasts and collagen deposition in the dermis, submucosa, and endothelium
- Sclerosis occurs in three locations: **cutaneous**, **vascular**, and **GI**

#### Subtypes

| Category | Subtypes |
|---|---|
| Most common | Limited, Diffuse |
| Less common (variants of Limited) | Sine Scleroderma, Overlap Syndrome (with SLE, Myositis, or MCTD) |

**MCTD** = Mixed Connective Tissue Disease

#### Limited vs. Diffuse — Key Distinctions

| Feature | Limited SSc | Diffuse SSc |
|---|---|---|
| Skin distribution | Distal extremities (distal to MCPs), face, neck ("acral") | Trunk + proximal extremities |
| Progression | Slow | Rapid, early visceral involvement |
| Autoantibody | **Anti-centromere (ACA)** | **Anti-Scl-70** (Anti-DNA Topoisomerase I) |
| Classic variant | **CREST** (Calcinosis, Raynaud's, Esophageal dysmotility, Sclerodactyly, Telangiectasias) | — |
| Sine scleroderma variant | Lung, pulmonary artery involvement; no cutaneous features | — |

- **Diffuse** involves the trunk and extends beyond the forearms; **Limited** spares the trunk
- Variants are sometimes difficult to distinguish; ultimately the **degree and location of organ involvement** directs treatment
- Both subtypes can have major internal organ involvement

#### Less Common Subtypes
- **Sine Scleroderma:** No cutaneous features; internal organ disease present
- **Overlap Syndrome:** Co-existing SLE, myositis, or vasculitis

### Objective 2: Recognize Cutaneous Manifestations of Scleroderma

#### 1. Skin Sclerosis (Sclerodactyly)
- **Skin sclerosis** proximal to MCP (metacarpophalangeal) and MTP (metatarsophalangeal) joints
- Leads to **indurated, taut, shiny skin**; loss of skin creases
- Morphology: digital pitting scars, substance loss of finger pad
- Face: "beak-like" appearance, no wrinkles, reduced oral aperture (pursed mouth)

#### 2. Raynaud's Phenomenon
- Present in **>95% of patients** — hesitate to diagnose scleroderma without it
- **Three phases:**
  - Blue (cyanotic)
  - White (ischemic) — must be present to call it true Raynaud's
  - Red (re-perfusion)
- May only see two phases; the **white/ischemic phase** is required
- Only blue = "acral cyanosis" (not true Raynaud's)
- More serious Raynaud's is present year-round
- Complications: **digital ulcerations**, **calcinosis cutis** (calcium deposits under skin)

#### 3. Other Cutaneous Features
- **Diffuse hyperpigmentation and depigmentation** ("salt-and-pepper" pattern)
- **Telangiectasias** (dilated superficial blood vessels), especially on lips, palms, nail folds
- **Abnormal nail fold capillaries** (visible on capillaroscopy; enlarged, disorganized loops)

#### Cutaneous Summary by Subtype

| Feature | Limited | Diffuse | Sine Scleroderma | Overlap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Raynaud's | Present | Present | Present | Present |
| Puffy Digits | Present | Present | **Absent** | Present |
| Sclerosis Location | Spares trunk | Involves trunk | **Absent** | Usually spares trunk |
| Sclerosis Progression | Slow | Rapid | **Absent** | Slow to rapid |
| Telangiectasias | Present | Sometimes | **Absent** | Sometimes |
| Abnormal Nail Capillaries | Present | Present | **Absent** | Present |

### Objective 3: Detect Internal Organ Involvement in Scleroderma

**Pulmonary disease is the leading cause of mortality.**

#### Most Common Organ Involvement
- **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD)**
- **Pulmonary Artery Hypertension (PAH)**
- **Esophageal and Intestinal Dysmotility**
- **Pericardial and Myocardial Disease**

#### Organ Involvement by Subtype

| Organ | Limited | Diffuse | Sine Scleroderma | Overlap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ILD | Later onset | Early onset + fibrosis | Variable | Variable |
| Pulmonary HTN | Late onset | Early onset | Variable | Variable |
| Renal | Rare | Uncommon, but **renal crisis** possible | Uncommon | Depends on co-existing disease |
| Cardiac | Late onset | Early onset | Variable | Depends on co-existing disease |
| GI | Esophageal | Esophageal + intestinal | Esophageal + intestinal | Esophageal |

#### Scleroderma Renal Crisis
- Characterized by rapidly rising creatinine, hypertension, microangiopathic hemolytic anemia, thrombocytopenia
- Most associated with **Diffuse SSc** and **Anti-RNA Polymerase III** antibody
- Treatment: **ACE inhibitors**

### Objective 4: Employ Criteria to Diagnose Scleroderma

#### 2013 ACR/EULAR Classification Criteria for Systemic Sclerosis

> A total score **≥ 9** classifies a patient as having **definite systemic sclerosis**.
> Take the **highest score** within each category.

| Item | Sub-items | Score |
|---|---|---|
| Skin thickening of fingers of both hands extending proximal to MCPs | — | 9 |
| Skin thickening of fingers (count highest only) | Puffy fingers | 2 |
| | Whole finger, distal to MCP | 4 |
| Fingertip lesions (count highest only) | Digital tip ulcers | 2 |
| | Pitting scars | 3 |
| Telangiectasia | — | 2 |
| Abnormal nailfold capillaries | — | 2 |
| Pulmonary arterial hypertension and/or ILD | — | 2 |
| Raynaud's phenomenon | — | 3 |
| Scleroderma-related antibodies (anti-centromere, anti-topoisomerase I [anti-Scl-70], anti-RNA polymerase III) | — | 3 |

**Note:** Criteria are **not applicable** to patients with skin thickening sparing the fingers, or to disorders better explaining their findings (e.g., nephrogenic sclerosing fibrosis, scleredema diabeticorum, scleromyxedema, lichen sclerosis, graft-versus-host disease).

### Objective 5: Describe Treatment of Scleroderma

**Before treating, identify the extent and severity of disease.**

#### Assess Disease Severity

| Signs and Symptoms | Consider | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| SOB, edema, wet rales, S3, JVD | Cardiomyopathy, Myocarditis, Pericarditis, CAD | ECG, CK/Troponin, Echocardiogram |
| SOB without cardiac signs | Pulmonary HTN or ILD | Echo; if abnormal → Right Heart Cath; CT chest + PFTs |
| Rising creatinine + elevated BP | Renal Crisis | Rule out other causes of acute renal failure |
| Diffuse skin edema/thickening | Diffuse Scleroderma | Dermatology consult |
| Red/white/blue digits | Raynaud's | Examination |
| Dysphagia/heartburn/reflux | GI: GERD, gastric or intestinal motility disorder, GAVE | EGD/Motility studies |

#### Treatment by Organ System

| Organ System | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **ILD** | Cyclophosphamide OR Tocilizumab OR Mycophenolate OR Azathioprine |
| **Pulmonary Hypertension** | Calcium Channel Blockers; **Ambrisentan (+) sildenafil**; Prostacyclin Pathway Agonists; Endothelial Receptor Antagonists |
| **Scleroderma Renal Crisis** | **ACE inhibitors** |
| **Raynaud's** | CCB: Amlodipine, Nifedipine; Sildenafil |

### Objective 6: Understand How Social Determinants Influence the Course of Scleroderma

#### Gender
- Prevalence **3–8x more common in women** than men
- **Women:** Earlier onset, more often limited subtype; increased risk of PVD and PAH; longer delay in diagnosis
- **Men:** More often diffuse subtype; higher rates of ILD and renal crisis

#### Race/Ethnicity/Age
- **African Americans:** Earlier disease onset, more severe disease
- Prevalence: European, North and South American > East Asian patients
- Greatest prevalence (47 in 100,000): indigenous peoples in Canada
- Outcomes worse in lower SES (socioeconomic status)
- Increased organ involvement and disease acceleration in older patients

#### Environment
- Warmer climate: less severe Raynaud's
- Subtropical climate region: **5.4x higher risk** of developing digital ulcers (DUs) vs. tropical climate

## SJÖGREN'S SYNDROME

---

### Objective 7: Define Sjögren's Syndrome

**Definition:** An **autoimmune disorder** that causes **destruction of salivary and lacrimal glands**, leading to dry eyes and dry mouth.

#### Key Features
1. Decreased lacrimal and salivary gland function → **dry eyes (xerophthalmia)** and **dry mouth (xerostomia)**
2. Can be **primary** or **secondary** to other rheumatic diseases
3. Two subsets:
   - **Exocrine glandular only** — 90% of patients
   - **Extraglandular** — 10% of patients

#### Classification
- **Primary Sjögren's:** Affects 1–4% of the population; associated with **SSA** and **SSB antibodies**; no other connective tissue disease (CTD)
- **Secondary Sjögren's:** Seen in **15–20%** of patients living with other autoimmune processes (e.g., RA, SLE, scleroderma)

---

### Objective 8: Employ Criteria to Diagnose Sjögren's Syndrome

#### Diagnostic Requirements — Must Have ALL THREE:

1. **Objective evidence of dry eyes or mouth:**
   - Eye: Schirmer's test OR ocular staining
   - Mouth: decreased salivary flow (stimulated or unstimulated) OR US/MRI showing glandular parenchymal abnormalities
2. **Serologic or histopathologic features:**
   - Positive **SSA (Ro) autoantibodies** OR
   - Salivary gland biopsy with **focal lymphocytic infiltration**
3. **Alternative causes for dry eye or mouth ruled out**

#### ACR/EULAR Classification Criteria for Primary Sjögren's Syndrome

> A score **≥ 4** classifies a patient as having primary Sjögren's (when inclusion criteria are met and exclusion criteria are absent).

| Item | Score |
|---|---|
| Labial salivary gland with focal lymphocytic sialadenitis and focus score ≥ 1 foci/4mm² | 3 |
| Anti-SS-A/Ro positive | 3 |
| Ocular Staining Score ≥ 5 (or van Bijsterveld score ≥ 4) in at least one eye | 1 |
| Schirmer's test ≤ 5 mm/5 minutes in at least one eye | 1 |
| Unstimulated whole saliva flow rate ≤ 0.1 ml/minute | 1 |

**Exclusion criteria:** Prior head and neck radiation, active HCV infection, AIDS, sarcoidosis, amyloidosis, graft-versus-host disease, IgG4-related disease

---

### Objective 9: Identify Extra-Glandular Disease Features of Sjögren's Syndrome

Only **5–10%** of patients develop extraglandular disease.

| Most Common | Less Common |
|---|---|
| Fatigue, arthralgia | Hypergammaglobulinemia |
| Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD) | Purpura / cutaneous vasculitis |
| Interstitial Nephritis | **Increased risk of Lymphoma** |
| Peripheral Neuropathy | Cranial Neuropathies |
| | CNS involvement |

---

### Objective 10: Understand Treatment Strategies for Sjögren's Syndrome

#### Treatment of Dry Eyes and Mouth (Glandular Disease)

**For the mouth:**
- Stimulate salivary flow: sugar-free gum/candy, oral inserts, dry fruits, malic acid tablets, artificial saliva
- Dental hygiene
- Treat oral candidiasis
- **Pilocarpine or cevimeline** (acetylcholine analogues — stimulate secretion)

**For the eyes:**
- Artificial tears
- Punctal plugs
- Restasis drops (cyclosporine eye drops)

**For either:** **Hydroxychloroquine**

#### Treatment of Extraglandular Disease

Depends on organ involved and severity:

| Drug | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Methotrexate** | Steroid-sparing agent for severe neuropathy |
| **Rituximab** | Used in ILD and severe extraglandular disease |
| **IVIG** | Severe extraglandular manifestations |
| **Hydroxychloroquine** | Note: equivalent to placebo in some studies |

## Summary & Take Home Points

### Scleroderma
1. Four subtypes: **Limited, Diffuse, Sine Scleroderma, Overlap**
2. **Raynaud's is almost always present** (>95%); hesitate to diagnose without it
3. Systemic involvement: **ILD, PAH, GI dysmotility, Myocarditis/Pericarditis**
4. Autoantibodies are **highly specific but not sensitive:**
   - **SCL-70** = Diffuse SSc (+ increased ILD severity)
   - **ACA (anti-centromere)** = Limited SSc
   - **Anti-RNA Polymerase III** = rapidly progressive skin disease, renal crisis, cancer risk
5. Define extent and severity → manage accordingly (treatment is organ-directed)

### Sjögren's Syndrome
1. Diagnosis requires objective evidence of dry eyes/mouth + SSA(Ro) OR salivary gland biopsy + exclusion of alternative causes
2. 5–10% have extraglandular disease
3. Treat dry eyes and mouth with local measures; treat extraglandular disease with appropriate immunosuppressive therapy
4. Patients are at increased risk of **lymphoma**
