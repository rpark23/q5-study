## Objective 1: History and Physical Examination in Myositis

### Approach to Weakness

Start by determining if weakness is **objective** (true muscle weakness) or functional. If no objective weakness, consider: cardiopulmonary disease, anemia, chronic infection, malignancy, depression, deconditioning, arthritis, fibromyalgia.

If objective weakness is present, localize it:
- **Generalized:** cachexia, myasthenia gravis, periodic paralysis
- **Localized asymmetric:** neurologic or spinal cord causes
- **Localized symmetric proximal:** **myopathy** (including inflammatory myopathies)
- **Localized symmetric distal:** peripheral neuropathy, motor neuron disease

> Note: Most myopathies cause objective proximal weakness, but "**amyopathic**" variants exist with little to no muscle weakness.

---

### History: Evaluating Muscle Weakness

| Feature | Key Points |
|---|---|
| **Proximal vs. distal weakness** | Proximal: difficulty rising from chairs, climbing stairs, combing/washing hair. Distal: hand grip, walking difficulties |
| **Symmetric vs. asymmetric** | IIMs are symmetric (except IBM can be asymmetric) |
| **Absence of sensory findings** | Sensory findings suggest neuropathy, not myopathy |
| **Onset timing** | Acute (hours): toxic/metabolic. Acute (days): rhabdomyolysis, acute DM. Subacute (weeks-months): most IIMs. Chronic (years): IBM |
| **Myalgias** | More common in infectious myopathies, drug-induced, thyroid, some metabolic; seen in up to 25% of IIMs (mostly IMNM) |
| **Dysphagia/dysphonia** | Seen in ~10-30% of IIM patients; portends worse prognosis |

### Associated Symptoms to Ask About

| System | Symptoms |
|---|---|
| **Constitutional** | Fever, weight loss, fatigue |
| **Arthralgias** | Joint pain, stiffness, swelling |
| **Skin** | Rashes, pruritus, photosensitivity, alopecia, oral ulcers, cutaneous ulcers, dry scaly fingers/fingertips, cuticle overgrowth, redness around nails, hard calcium deposits |
| **Raynaud's** | Digital ulcers |
| **Pulmonary** | Shortness of breath, cough |
| **Cardiac** | Chest pain, palpitations |

---

### Physical Exam: Dermatomyositis Skin Findings

The skin exam is critical in dermatomyositis (DM). Key findings:

- **Heliotrope rash:** Macular violaceous (purple-red) erythema of the eyelids or periorbital skin, with or without scale; +/- eyelid edema
- **Gottron's papules:** Erythematous to violaceous papules symmetrically over the extensor MCP and IP joints of the hands
- **Gottron's sign:** Erythematous to violaceous macules/patches/papules on extensor surfaces of joints (elbows, knees, ankles)
- **Facial erythema:** Photosensitive; can mimic the malar rash of SLE, but **involves the nasolabial fold** (SLE spares it)
- **V-neck and shawl signs:** Photodistributed **poikiloderma** (mixed hyper/hypopigmentation, telangiectasias, epidermal atrophy) on the anterior chest (V-neck) or upper back/shoulders (shawl). Often associated with **significant pruritus**
- **Holster sign:** Poikiloderma on the lateral aspects of the thighs
- **Psoriasiform scalp changes:** Diffuse scaling resembling seborrheic dermatitis or psoriasis; associated with severe burning and pruritus
- **Periungual nailfold telangiectasia:** Abnormal capillary nail beds with erythema, vascular dilatation and dropout, and "**ragged cuticles**" (cuticular overgrowth)
- **Cutaneous calcinosis:** Calcium deposits in the skin; more common in juvenile DM; visible on X-ray
- **Cutaneous ulcers:** Can occur over the knuckles or other sites

### Anti-synthetase Syndrome Findings

A specific DM subtype with associated antibodies (most commonly **anti-Jo-1**, also PL-7, PL-12, KS, OJ, EJ, Zo, Ha, SC, JS):
- **Mechanic's hands:** Rough, cracked, hyperkeratotic skin on the lateral and palmar surfaces of the fingers
- Raynaud's phenomenon
- Nonerosive arthritis
- **ILD (interstitial lung disease)** — a major feature
- Constitutional symptoms (fever, weight loss)

## Objective 2: Differential Diagnosis for Myositis

### Localizing the Lesion

Weakness can arise from any point along the motor pathway. Myopathy = lesion at the **muscle** level.

| Site of lesion | Inflammatory example | Key distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Upper motor neuron | Vasculitis | Upper motor neuron signs |
| Anterior horn cell | ALS | Fasciculations, no sensory loss |
| Peripheral nerve | Guillain-Barre | Sensory findings present |
| NM junction | Myasthenia gravis | Fatigable weakness |
| **Muscle** | **Polymyositis, IMNM** | Proximal, symmetric, no sensory loss |

### DDx: Myopathies

**Idiopathic Inflammatory Myopathies (IIMs):**
- Dermatomyositis (DM)
- Polymyositis (PM)
- Overlap myopathies (lupus, scleroderma, RA, Sjogren's)
- Immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy (IMNM)
- Inclusion body myositis (IBM)

**Non-inflammatory myopathies to consider:**
- **Endocrine:** thyroid (hypo or hyper), Cushing's, Addison's, acromegaly
- **Electrolyte disturbances:** low K, low Phos, low Ca, abnormal Na
- **Metabolic myopathies:** glycogen storage, lipid metabolism, mitochondrial, nutritional
- **Drugs/toxins:** EtOH, cocaine, steroids, colchicine, antimalarials, **statins**, zidovudine
- **Infections:** viral (flu, coxsackie, HIV, CMV), bacterial (pyomyositis, Lyme), fungal, parasitic (trichinosis, toxoplasmosis)
- **Rhabdomyolysis:** crush trauma, seizures, EtOH abuse, malignant hyperthermia
- **Inherited myopathies:** muscular dystrophy

## Objective 3: Diagnostic Studies for Myositis

### Laboratory Tests

**Initial labs:**
- **Muscle enzymes:** CK, aldolase, LDH, AST, ALT — elevated in most IIMs; can be markedly elevated in IMNM
- CBC, ESR, CRP
- TFTs (to rule out thyroid myopathy)
- Electrolytes, Ca, Mg (to rule out electrolyte causes)
- BUN/Cr
- Urinalysis with microscopy: may see **myoglobinuria** (dark urine from muscle breakdown)
- ANA

**Autoantibodies (extended panel):**
- **Myositis-specific antibodies (MSAs):** Each has a specific phenotype, degree of extramuscular involvement, and malignancy risk
- **Myositis-associated antibodies (MAAs):** Associated with overlap syndromes
- The **Mayo Myositis Panel (FMP3)** includes: Anti-Jo-1, PL-7, PL-12, EJ, OJ, **SRP**, MI-2, TIF1 Gamma (P155/140), MDA-5 (CADM-140), NXP-2, Anti-PM/Scl, Fibrillarin (U3 RNP), U2 snRNP
- Anti-Jo-1 and SRP are highlighted in First Aid

### Antibody-Clinical Associations (Summary)

| Antibody | Association |
|---|---|
| **Anti-Jo-1** (and other anti-synthetase Abs) | Anti-synthetase syndrome: ILD, mechanic's hands, arthritis, Raynaud's |
| **Anti-SRP** | IMNM, severe myopathy, cardiac involvement |
| **Anti-HMGCR** | IMNM, statin-associated |
| **Anti-TIF1 gamma (p155/140)** | DM with high malignancy risk |
| **Anti-NXP2** | DM with malignancy risk, calcinosis |
| **Anti-MDA5 (CADM-140)** | DM with lung disease, skin disease, amyopathic DM |
| **Anti-Mi-2** | Classic DM, good prognosis |
| **Anti-cN1A** | IBM (not specific but can help distinguish) |

### EMG/NCS

- **EMG** in PM/DM: good sensitivity (85%) but low specificity (33%)
- Can determine if myopathy is **active or chronic**, but cannot distinguish inflammatory from toxic/dystrophic
- **Typical EMG pattern** in inflammatory myopathies (not specific):
  1. Increased insertional activity with spontaneous fibrillations
  2. Myopathic low amplitude, short duration polyphasic motor unit action potentials (MUAPs)
  3. Complex high-frequency repetitive discharges
- **NCS** are normal in IIMs (exception: IBM, where peripheral neuropathy can co-develop)
- Use NCS to **exclude neuropathic disorders**

### MRI

- **T2-weighted images:** bright signal indicates edema/inflammation
- **T1-weighted images:** fatty degeneration in chronic disease (unlikely to improve with medications)
- MRI can identify: edema, inflammation, fatty infiltration, fibrosis, atrophy
- Useful to **guide muscle biopsy site selection**
- MRI abnormalities in IBM tend to be localized to **anterior muscle groups**

### Muscle Biopsy

- Typically biopsy **quadriceps or deltoid**
- Avoid muscles with severe weakness, marked atrophy, or recent EMG testing (causes artifact)
- Can differentiate among inflammatory myopathy subtypes
- Special stains can demonstrate enzyme deficiencies (metabolic myopathies)

| IIM Subtype | Biopsy Findings |
|---|---|
| **DM** | Perimysial/perivascular inflammation (B cells and CD4+ T cells); injury to capillaries and perifascicular myofibers; **perifascicular atrophy** |
| **PM** | Endomysial (interstitial) inflammation; CD8+ T cells surrounding and invading **non-necrotic** myofibers; enhanced MHC I expression |
| **IBM** | Endomysial CD8+ T cells; **rimmed vacuoles**; ragged red fibers; beta amyloid deposition |
| **IMNM** | **Prominent myofiber necrosis**; degeneration and regeneration of fibers; **minimal** lymphocytic infiltrate |

### Skin Biopsy (DM)

- **Interface dermatitis** (vacuolar degeneration of basal keratinocytes) — similar to lupus but usually less pronounced
- Epidermal atrophy
- Perivascular lymphocytic infiltration composed of CD4+ T cells

### Other Studies

- Genetic testing (hereditary myopathies)
- EKG/Echo (cardiac involvement)
- PFTs (pulmonary involvement, ILD)
- Skin biopsy (if rash present)

## Objective 4: Classification of Idiopathic Inflammatory Myopathies (IIMs)

### Epidemiology

| Category | Age | Prevalence/Gender | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dermatomyositis** | Bimodal: childhood and 50-70 | F > M (2:1); ~1/100,000 | Cancer risk 9-32% within 3 years of onset |
| **Polymyositis** | Rare in childhood | F > M | Lower malignancy risk than DM |
| **IMNM** | Any | Up to 19% of all inflammatory myopathies | 2/3 have +SRP or HMGCoA Abs; can be cancer-associated |
| **IBM** | > 50 years | M > F (3:1) | Most common inflammatory myopathy in patients >50 |

---

### Dermatomyositis (DM)

**Clinical features:**
- Autoimmune disease with inflammation in **muscle and skin**
- Proximal muscle weakness, usually subacute onset (can be amyopathic)
- Elevated muscle enzymes (CK, Aldolase, LDH, AST, ALT)
- Characteristic skin findings (see Objective 1)
- Most are ANA positive; variety of MSAs and MAAs

**Extra-muscular/extra-dermatologic manifestations:**

| Domain | Manifestations |
|---|---|
| Constitutional | Fatigue, low-grade fever, weight loss |
| Musculoskeletal | Arthralgias/arthritis (20-70%); often with anti-synthetase syndrome |
| **Pulmonary** | **ILD (10-20%, up to 70% with anti-synthetase syndrome)** as NSIP; aspiration pneumonia; respiratory muscle weakness; pulmonary hypertension |
| Gastrointestinal | Esophageal dysmotility (10-30%); reflux (15-50%); rectal incontinence; intestinal perforation (juvenile DM) |
| Cardiac | ECG abnormalities (dysrhythmias, conduction blocks); myocarditis |
| Vascular | Vasculitis (especially juvenile DM); skin ulcerations; livedo reticularis; Raynaud's (20-40%) |
| Overlaps | Scleroderma, MCTD, SLE; less often Sjogren's, RA |

**Association with malignancy:**
- ~15-30% of DM patients develop cancer (standardized incidence ratio 3.0-7.7)
- Risk is highest within **3 years of DM symptom onset** (both before and after)
- Higher risk with **anti-TIF1 Gamma** and **anti-NXP2** antibodies
- 5-year survival: 10-62% with cancer vs. ~91% without
- All newly diagnosed DM patients should undergo **intensive cancer screening**

**Treatment:**
- **Corticosteroids (CS)** as first-line
- **Immunosuppressive agents** started early as steroid-sparing: methotrexate, azathioprine, mycophenolate mofetil
- Hydroxychloroquine and sun avoidance for cutaneous manifestations
- Tacrolimus for resistant PM and lung disease
- **IVIG** for severe/refractory DM/PM (especially with dysphagia)
- **Rituximab** effective in DM and in PM with myositis-specific antibodies

---

### Polymyositis (PM)

**Clinical features:**
- Autoimmune disease with muscle inflammation (no characteristic skin findings)
- Proximal muscle weakness, subacute onset
- Elevated muscle enzymes (CK, Aldolase, LDH, AST, ALT)
- Some ANA positive; overlap syndromes with systemic sclerosis, SLE, MCTD, Sjogren's
- **Lower risk of associated malignancy** than DM
- Similar rates of ILD as DM

**Muscle biopsy:** Endomysial inflammation with **CD8+ T cells** invading non-necrotic fibers; enhanced MHC I expression — this distinguishes PM from DM (which has CD4+ T cells and perimysial/perivascular pattern)

**Treatment:** Similar to DM (corticosteroids + immunosuppressives)

---

### Inclusion Body Myositis (IBM)

**Clinical features:**
- **Insidious onset**, slowly progressive — always chronic
- **Both proximal and distal** muscle involvement (unlike other IIMs)
- Pattern: **finger flexors and knee extensors** (long finger flexors involved early)
- Can be **asymmetric**
- Dysphagia in ~1/3 of patients
- Muscle enzymes **normal or mildly elevated** (CK < 10x ULN)
- May develop mild **peripheral neuropathy** with loss of DTRs
- Demographics: M > F (3:1), age > 50

**Diagnosis:**
- Muscle biopsy: **rimmed vacuoles**, ragged red fibers, endomysial CD8+ T cells, **beta amyloid deposition**
- EMG: "irritable myopathy" (myopathic and neuropathic pattern)
- MRI: abnormalities localized to anterior muscle groups
- **Anti-cN1A antibodies**: can help distinguish IBM from other myositis, but not specific

**Treatment:**
- Typically **poor response to immunosuppression**
- Empiric trial for ~3-6 months to assess for improvement
- Encourage exercise to maintain strength as long as possible
- Assistive devices
- For severe dysphagia: Botulinum toxin or myotomy, PEG

---

### IBM vs. Polymyositis Comparison

| Feature | IBM | Polymyositis |
|---|---|---|
| Demographics | M > F, age > 50 | F > M, all ages |
| Onset | Insidious | Acute or subacute |
| Course | Slowly progressive | More rapid |
| Muscle involvement | Proximal and distal, asymmetric | Proximal, symmetric |
| Other organ involvement | Neuropathy | ILD, arthritis, cardiac |
| Myositis-specific Abs | No (cN1A, not specific) | Yes |
| EMG | Myopathic and neuropathic | Myopathic |
| Muscle biopsy | CD8+ T cells, rimmed vacuoles, beta amyloid | CD8+ T cell infiltrate |
| Response to immunosuppression | No | Frequent |

### Immune-Mediated Necrotizing Myopathy (IMNM)

**Clinical features:**
- **Acute onset**, severe proximal weakness
- Significant **myalgias** due to muscle fiber necrosis
- **Prominently elevated CK** (exceptionally high, up to 50x ULN)
- Prominent dysphagia
- ILD and skin involvement rare (<10%)
- Can be associated with malignancy (1/3 of cases)
- Key autoantibodies: **anti-SRP** and **anti-HMGCR**

**Anti-SRP myopathy:**
- Increased cardiac involvement
- Previously poor prognosis (5-yr survival 30%), but more recent data shows ~86%

**Anti-HMGCR IMNM (statin-associated):**
- Associated with statin exposure (atorvastatin and others)
- Characterized by a genetic risk factor: **HLA DRB1* 11:01**
- Can occur any time during statin use, or even **after stopping** the statin
- Requires **immunosuppression** — does not resolve simply with statin discontinuation
- Can be refractory to treatment

**Statin-associated muscle adverse events (general):**
- Myalgias and myopathy: 2-11%
- Severe myonecrosis: 0.5%
- Rhabdomyolysis: <0.1%
- Most toxic events resolve within days to weeks of stopping the statin
- **Persistent myositis after stopping** = suspect anti-HMGCR IMNM

**Muscle biopsy:** Prominent **myofiber necrosis**; degeneration and regeneration of muscle fibers; **minimal** lymphocytic infiltrate (distinguishes from PM/DM)

**Treatment:** GC + AZA/MTX/MMF; escalation with RTX, CYC, IVIG often required

## Key Takeaways

- Muscle weakness has many causes; myopathy localizes to the **muscle** itself and presents as **symmetric proximal weakness without sensory findings**
- Most IIMs present subacutely over weeks to months; IBM is the exception with a **chronic, insidious course**
- **Muscle biopsy** evidence of inflammation in DM, PM, and IBM; IMNM is characterized by necrosis with **minimal inflammation**
- **Malignancy screening is crucial** at DM diagnosis, especially with TIF1-gamma or NXP2 antibodies
- **Overlap myopathies** can occur with other rheumatologic diseases (scleroderma, SLE, MCTD)
- **Statin-associated IMNM (anti-HMGCR)** does not resolve with statin cessation alone and requires immunosuppression
- Autoantibodies (MSAs and MAAs) help define clinical phenotype, prognosis, and guide management
