## Definition of Vasculitis

**Vasculitis** = invasion of inflammatory leukocytes into vessel walls with reactive damage to mural structures.
- Results in loss of vessel integrity
- Major consequences: **bleeding** and **tissue ischemia/necrosis**

---

## Objective 1: Classify Vasculitides by Vessel Size

Vasculitides are classified by the size of vessel predominantly affected. Note that there is significant overlap — vessels exist on a continuum.

### 2012 Chapel Hill Consensus Classification

| Vessel Size | Diseases |
|---|---|
| **Large Vessel Vasculitis (LVV)** | Takayasu Arteritis (TAK), Giant Cell Arteritis (GCA) |
| **Medium Vessel Vasculitis (MVV)** | Polyarteritis Nodosa (PAN), Kawasaki Disease (KD) |
| **Small Vessel Vasculitis (SVV) — ANCA-associated** | Microscopic Polyangiitis (MPA), Granulomatosis with Polyangiitis (GPA), Eosinophilic Granulomatosis with Polyangiitis (EGPA) |
| **Small Vessel Vasculitis — Immune Complex** | Cryoglobulinemic Vasculitis, IgA Vasculitis (Henoch-Schonlein), Hypocomplementemic Urticarial Vasculitis |
| **Variable Vessel** | Behcet's Disease, Cogan's Syndrome |
| **Vasculitis associated with systemic disease** | Lupus vasculitis, Rheumatoid vasculitis, Sarcoid vasculitis |
| **Vasculitis with probable etiology** | Hep C-associated cryoglobulinemic, Hep B-associated, Drug-associated ANCA, Cancer-associated |

> Note: Vasculitis can be a feature of other systemic rheumatic diseases (SLE, RA, Sarcoid, etc.)

### Clinical Manifestations by Vessel Size

| Large Vessels | Medium Vessels | Small Vessels |
|---|---|---|
| Claudication | Nodules | Purpura |
| Absent pulses | Ulcers | Glomerulonephritis (GN) |
| Bruits | Livedo | Alveolar hemorrhage |
| | Digital ischemia | Scleritis |
| | Neuropathy | |

## Objective 3: Clinical Presentations of Common Vasculitides

### Large Vessel Vasculitis

---

#### Giant Cell Arteritis (GCA)

**Epidemiology:**
- Most common systemic vasculitis; annual incidence 17 per 100,000 persons over 50
- Mean age at diagnosis: **77**; female:male = **3:1**
- Lifetime risk: ~1% in women, ~0.5% in men
- Most commonly involves the **temporal arteries** and branches of the **aortic arch**

**Presenting Symptoms:**
- **New onset temporal headache** in patient >50 years old
- **Jaw claudication** — typically jaw fatigue with chewing (not pain); helps differentiate from TMJ dysfunction
- Constitutional symptoms: low-grade fevers, weight loss, night sweats, fatigue
- Dreaded complication: **acute unilateral vision loss**
- Aortic arch involvement: claudication, weakness of upper extremities

**Physical Exam:**
- Diminished temporal artery pulses; thick, palpable cord-like temporal arteries
- Unequal peripheral pulses
- Bruits over aortic arch vessels
- Aortic regurgitation murmur
- Less common but notable: **scalp or tongue ulcerations/necrosis**

**Vision Loss:**
- Occurs in 8–28% of patients; typically **painless, acute, complete or partial**
- Usually **nonreversible**
- Within one week, 25–50% of untreated patients lose vision in the unaffected eye
- Mechanism: **Anterior Ischemic Optic Neuropathy (AION)** in 80–90%

**Association with Polymyalgia Rheumatica (PMR):**
- PMR = aching and stiffness in the **shoulders and hip girdles**, elevated ESR/CRP, resolves within 48 hours of prednisone
- PMR occurs in ~40% of GCA patients; GCA occurs in ~10% of PMR patients

**Diagnostics:**

| Test | Finding |
|---|---|
| CBC | Low Hb, elevated WBC and platelets |
| ESR / CRP | Elevated in >96% of cases |
| Temporal artery ultrasound | First-line imaging |
| CTA / MRA / PET-CT | Vascular imaging of aortic arch |
| **Temporal Artery Biopsy (TAB)** | Gold standard; do within 1–2 weeks |

- TAB sensitivity: 85–90% unilateral; bilateral adds ~5% sensitivity
- Histology: **panarteritis** with CD4+ lymphocytes and macrophages; **giant cells** are common but not required; **internal elastic lamina** often fragmented

**Treatment:**
- **Start prednisone 1 mg/kg immediately** — do NOT wait for biopsy (risk of irreversible vision loss)
- Taper steroids over 6–12 months
- **Glucocorticoid-sparing therapies:** Tocilizumab (IL-6 inhibitor), Upadacitinib (JAK inhibitor), Methotrexate

---

#### Takayasu Arteritis (TAK)

**Epidemiology:**
- Age of onset: **10–40 years**
- 80–90% of cases are **women**
- Highest prevalence in patients of **Asian descent**
- Commonly involves the **aortic arch** but also abdominal and intracranial circulation

**Presenting Symptoms:**
- Constitutional: fatigue, fevers, chills, weight loss
- **Limb claudication** (cramping, fatigue, weakness with repetitive use)
- **Carotidynia**: tenderness of the carotid artery (10–30%)
- Arthralgias (true arthritis is less common)
- **Resistant hypertension** — typically due to renal artery stenosis
- GI symptoms: post-prandial pain from mesenteric ischemia

**Physical Exam:**
- Bruits over aortic arch, carotids, subclavian, or axillary arteries
- Aortic murmurs
- Diminished, absent, or unequal peripheral pulses
- **BP measurements differ in all four extremities** (can be falsely low)

**Diagnostics:**

| Test | Finding |
|---|---|
| ESR / CRP | Elevated |
| CBC | Low Hb, elevated WBC |
| MRA / PET-CT / CTA | Vascular imaging of aortic arch |

### Medium Vessel Vasculitis

---

#### Polyarteritis Nodosa (PAN)

**Epidemiology:**
- Necrotizing vasculitis of medium-sized vessels
- **Segmental transmural inflammation** of muscular arteries → stenosis → ischemia, aneurysm → rupture
- Subsets: Classic PAN, Viral-associated PAN (HBV > HCV > HIV), Single-organ PAN, Cutaneous-only PAN
- Male:female = **1.5:1**; peak incidence in the 60s

**Clinical Manifestations:**

| System | Description | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| General | Fever, malaise, weight loss, arthralgias, myalgias | 90% |
| Neurologic | **Mononeuritis multiplex**, peripheral neuropathy | 75% |
| Cutaneous | Nodules, purpura, livedo | 60% |
| Renal | Increased creatinine, hypertension, hematuria, proteinuria | 50% |
| GI | Abdominal pain, rectal bleeding | 40% |
| Orchitis | Testicular pain/swelling | 20% |
| Ophthalmologic | Retinal vasculitis, conjunctivitis, keratitis, uveitis | 8% |
| Vascular | Claudication, ischemia, necrosis | 6% |
| Cardiac | Cardiomyopathy, pericarditis | 5% |
| CNS | Stroke, confusion | 5% |

> Key: PAN **spares the lungs** (distinguishes from AAV)

**Viral-Associated PAN Comparison:**

| Feature | HBV-PAN | HCV-PAN | HIV-PAN |
|---|---|---|---|
| Presentation | Waxing/waning | Acute | Acute |
| Age | <40 y | Older women | — |
| Organs | Renal, GI, CNS, skin | Skin (purpura) | Skin, joints, nerves, muscles |
| Severity | Severe (35% mortality) | Moderate (10% mortality) | Mild (excellent prognosis) |

**Diagnostics:**

| Test | Purpose |
|---|---|
| ESR / CRP | Inflammation |
| Hepatitis B and C serology | Viral association |
| HIV | Viral association |
| **ANCA testing (negative)** | Helps rule out AAV |
| Urine toxicology | Drug-associated |
| Abdominal vessel imaging | Renal/mesenteric aneurysms, stenosis |
| Testicular ultrasound | Orchitis |
| EMG/NCS | Neuropathy |
| **Biopsy any available tissue** | Nerve biopsy shows vasculitis and nerve-fiber loss; CD3+ T cell infiltrate |

**Prognosis:**
- Untreated 5-year survival: **13%**
- Treated 5-year survival: **80–90%**
- Relapses in up to 20% of idiopathic PAN

---

#### Kawasaki Disease (KD)

**Epidemiology:**
- Medium vessel vasculitis with predilection for **coronary arteries**
- Most common in children **under 5**; rarely affects adolescents or young adults (especially those with HIV)
- Male:female = **2:1**
- Highest incidence in **Japan and children of Asian descent**

**Presenting Symptoms:**
- Fevers
- **Cervical lymphadenopathy**
- Mucocutaneous inflammation:
  - Bilateral **nonexudative conjunctivitis**
  - Oral desquamation — **"Strawberry tongue"**
  - Lip erythema
- **Desquamation of hands and feet**
- GI symptoms: abdominal pain, diarrhea
- Arthritis
- Cardiac disease: **coronary artery aneurysms** → myocardial infarction, ventricular dysfunction
- Concurrent infection: up to 40% of patients

**Diagnostics:**

| Test | Finding |
|---|---|
| ESR, CRP, ferritin | Elevated |
| WBC | Initially low, then elevated |
| Platelets | Elevated |
| AST/ALT | Elevated |
| UA | Pyuria |
| Echocardiogram | Coronary artery aneurysms (present in 25%) |
| Cardiac angiogram / Coronary CT | Aneurysm characterization |

**Treatment and Prognosis:**
- **Aspirin** 30–100 mg/kg per day
- **IVIG** within the first 10 days: reduces coronary artery aneurysm risk by **75%** and mortality by **95%**
- Typically self-limited (~12 days without therapy)
- 50–75% of coronary artery aneurysms regress to normal within 2 years
- Giant aneurysms (>8 mm) are less likely to regress

### Small Vessel Vasculitis

---

#### ANCA-Associated Vasculitis (AAV)

**Overview:**
- Spectrum of 3 diseases: **GPA** (Granulomatosis with Polyangiitis), **MPA** (Microscopic Polyangiitis), **EGPA** (Eosinophilic Granulomatosis with Polyangiitis)
- Characterized by **necrotizing inflammation** of vessels in multiple organ systems
- **ANCA** = Antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibodies
  - **C-ANCA** (cytoplasmic pattern) → antigen: **PR3** (associated with GPA)
  - **P-ANCA** (perinuclear pattern) → antigen: **MPO** (associated with MPA and EGPA)

**Comparison Table — GPA vs. MPA vs. EGPA:**

| Feature | GPA | MPA | EGPA |
|---|---|---|---|
| ANCA positivity | 80–90% | 70% | 50% |
| ANCA antigen | PR3 >> MPO | MPO > PR3 | MPO > PR3 |
| Histology | Leukocytoclastic; necrotizing, granulomatous inflammation | Leukocytoclastic; no granulomas | Eosinophilic infiltrates; granulomas with eosinophilic necrosis |
| ENT | Nasal septal perforation, **saddle-nose deformity**, hearing loss, subglottic stenosis | Absent or mild | Nasal polyps, allergic rhinitis, conductive hearing loss |
| Eye | Orbital pseudotumor, scleritis, episcleritis, uveitis | Occasional scleritis/uveitis | Occasional scleritis/uveitis |
| Lung | Nodules, infiltrates, cavitary lesions, alveolar hemorrhage | Alveolar hemorrhage | **Asthma**, fleeting infiltrates, alveolar hemorrhage |
| Kidney | Segmental necrotizing GN (pauci-immune) | Segmental necrotizing GN | Segmental necrotizing GN |
| Peripheral neuropathy | 10% | 58% | **78%** |
| Eosinophilia | Mild occasionally | None | **All** |
| Heart | Occasional valvular lesions | Rare | **Heart failure** |

**Key Clinical Patterns to Recognize:**
- **Pulmonary-renal syndrome**: diffuse alveolar hemorrhage (DAH) + pauci-immune glomerulonephritis
- **Destructive sinus and upper airway disease** (subglottic/bronchial stenosis)
- **Mononeuritis multiplex**

**Pauci-immune GN:** AAV causes GN with few immune complex deposits on immunofluorescence — distinguishes it from anti-GBM disease, SLE ("full house" pattern), and IgA nephropathy.

**Imaging findings:**
- GPA lung: nodules, infiltrates, cavitary lesions (DDx: bacterial/mycobacterial infection)
- DAH on CT chest: bilateral ground glass opacities (GGOs); also seen in pulmonary edema and infection
- BAL in DAH: progressively more bloody with each lavage (serial aliquots)

**Diagnostics:**

| Test | Purpose |
|---|---|
| ANCA immunofluorescence (IF) | Screen |
| Anti-PR3, Anti-MPO (ELISA) | Confirm and subtype |
| Anti-GBM | Rule out concurrent anti-GBM disease |
| CBC, Creatinine | Organ involvement |
| UA: hematuria, proteinuria | Renal involvement |
| ESR, CRP | Inflammation |
| Urine toxicology | Rule out drug etiology |
| CT Chest | Pulmonary lesions |
| Bronchoscopy + BAL | Evaluate for DAH |
| Renal biopsy | Confirm pauci-immune GN |
| Skin biopsy | Leukocytoclastic vasculitis |
| EMG/NCS | Neuropathy |

#### IgA Vasculitis (formerly Henoch-Schonlein Purpura)

**Epidemiology:**
- Most common vasculitis of childhood; 20–30% of cases are adults
- Male:female = **~1.5:1**
- **50% of cases preceded by infection** — especially Streptococcus, but also RSV, influenza, norovirus

**Presenting Symptoms:**
- **Palpable purpura** — favors the lower extremities
- Arthritis
- GI symptoms: acute abdominal pain, diarrhea, **hematochezia**
- Constitutional symptoms: fevers, sweats
- Renal disease: 20–50%

**Diagnostics:**

| Test | Finding |
|---|---|
| ESR / CRP | Elevated |
| UA | Hematuria, proteinuria |
| CBC | Elevated WBC, low Hb |
| Fecal occult blood | Positive in GI involvement |
| ASO | Prior strep infection |
| C3, C4, IgA | Elevated IgA |
| US or CT abdomen | GI complications |
| **Skin biopsy** | LCV with **IgA and C3 deposits on immunofluorescence** |
| **Renal biopsy** | IgA deposition in the mesangium; ranges from isolated mesangial proliferation to crescentic GN |

**Complications and Prognosis:**
- Intussusception (small bowel) in 3% of children, rare in adults
- GI hemorrhage, rarely perforation
- Kidney involvement: 20–54% of children, 30% of adults
  - Most cases resolve within 1 month; 89–94% resolve within 18 months
  - **ESRD**: 4% of children, 10–30% of adults

## Objective 2: Differential Diagnosis for Signs of Vasculitis

### Leukocytoclastic Vasculitis (LCV) — Etiologies

When a patient presents with palpable purpura (small vessel vasculitis), consider:

| Category | Frequency | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Idiopathic** | 45% | Unknown trigger |
| **Infectious** | 15–20% | Strep pyogenes, endocarditis, Hep C > B, influenza, parvovirus, HIV |
| **Autoimmune/CTD** | 15% | ANCA vasculitis, RA, Sjogren's, SLE, IBD, Behcet's |
| **Drug** | 10–15% | Beta-lactams, sulfa drugs, levamisole, PTU, thiazides, allopurinol, NSAIDs |
| **Neoplasm** | 5% | Plasma cell dyscrasias, MDS, leukemia/lymphoma |

> Skin biopsy histology: neutrophilic infiltrate surrounding and disrupting small vessels, fibrin deposits, **leukocytoclasia** (nuclear debris), extravasated RBCs (purpura) in adjacent dermis.

## Objective 4: Diagnostic Studies Summary

| Disease | Key Labs | Key Imaging/Procedure |
|---|---|---|
| **GCA** | ESR/CRP (>96%), CBC | Temporal artery US; CTA/MRA/PET-CT aortic arch; **Temporal artery biopsy** |
| **TAK** | ESR/CRP, CBC | MRA/PET-CT/CTA aortic arch |
| **PAN** | ESR/CRP, Hep B/C, HIV, **negative ANCA**, urine tox | Abdominal vessel imaging (aneurysms), testicular US, EMG/NCS, nerve/skin biopsy |
| **KD** | ESR/CRP/ferritin, elevated platelets/AST/ALT, UA (pyuria) | **Echocardiogram**, cardiac angiogram/coronary CT |
| **AAV** | ANCA IF, anti-PR3/MPO, anti-GBM, UA (hematuria/proteinuria), creatinine | CT chest, bronchoscopy (BAL for DAH), **renal biopsy** (pauci-immune GN), skin/nerve biopsy |
| **IgA Vasculitis** | ESR/CRP, UA, ASO, C3/C4, elevated IgA | **Skin biopsy** (IgA+C3 on IF), renal biopsy (mesangial IgA), US/CT abdomen |

## Objective 5: Treatment Paradigm

| Disease | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **GCA** | **Prednisone 1 mg/kg immediately** (do not wait for biopsy); taper over 6–12 months; add Tocilizumab (IL-6i), Upadacitinib (JAKi), or Methotrexate as steroid-sparing agents |
| **KD** | **Aspirin** 30–100 mg/kg/day + **IVIG** within 10 days of onset (reduces aneurysm risk by 75%, mortality by 95%) |
| **PAN** | Glucocorticoids ± cyclophosphamide; treat underlying viral infection for viral-associated PAN |
| **AAV** | High-dose glucocorticoids + cyclophosphamide or rituximab for induction; azathioprine or rituximab for maintenance |
| **IgA Vasculitis** | Mostly supportive; glucocorticoids for severe GI or renal disease |

## Take Home Points

- Vasculitides are classified by vessel size, which determines likely clinical manifestations
- **Buzzwords:**
  - **GCA**: temporal headaches, vision loss, associated with PMR
  - **TAK**: claudication, asymmetric pulses, young Asian women
  - **PAN**: Hepatitis and HIV association, spares the lungs, mononeuritis multiplex
  - **KD**: children, mucocutaneous inflammation, coronary artery aneurysms, IVIG
  - **AAV — GPA**: sinus disease, c-ANCA & PR3, saddle-nose deformity
  - **AAV — MPA**: classic pulmonary-renal syndrome, p-ANCA & MPO
  - **AAV — EGPA**: eosinophilia, asthma, neurologic involvement, p-ANCA & MPO
  - **IgA Vasculitis**: GI symptoms, lower extremity purpura, post-infectious
