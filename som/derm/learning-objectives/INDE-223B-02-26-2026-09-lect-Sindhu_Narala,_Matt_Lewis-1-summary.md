## 1. Importance of Recognizing Skin Disease

- Skin diseases account for ~8% of diagnoses in family medicine (NAMCS data)
- A skin finding may be the **first or only sign** of an underlying systemic disease
  - Example: **Gottron's papules** (flat-topped papules over the knuckles) are pathognomonic for **dermatomyositis**, which may be associated with internal malignancy
- Skin disease can have significant **psychosocial and quality of life impact**
  - Example: **vitiligo** (depigmented patches) can cause social stigma, especially in patients with darker skin
  - Example: severe **nodulocystic acne** can cause physical pain and psychosocial stress
- The **Dermatology Life Quality Index (DLQI)** is a tool used to measure impairment of quality of life from skin disease

---

## 2. Climate Change and Skin Disease

- The skin is an environmental interface, making it sensitive to changing environmental conditions
- **Air pollutants** can exacerbate chronic skin conditions such as **pemphigus** and **atopic dermatitis**
- **Vector-borne infectious diseases** are increasing due to climate-sensitive habitats (e.g., Lyme disease, Leishmaniasis, Dengue, Chikungunya, Zika, Coccidioidomycosis)
- **Increased skin cancer risk:**
  - A 3.6°F increase in ambient temperature is estimated to increase skin cancer incidence 11% globally by 2050
  - A 10% reduction in stratospheric ozone is estimated to cause an additional 300,000 skin cancers
- These effects **disproportionately impact the socially and economically disadvantaged**
  - People of color and those of lower socioeconomic status are disproportionately exposed to heat and pollution
  - Low-income outdoor laborers face prolonged sun/heat exposure

## 3. Full Body Skin Examination

**Key principles:**
- Use an **adequately lit room**
- Have the **patient fully gowned** to improve your exam
- Follow a **systematic order** consistently to avoid missing easy-to-miss locations:
  - Conchal bowl, behind the ears, in the mouth, alar creases, interdigital spaces, intergluteal cleft, soles
- Consider an **integrated skin examination**: examine skin while performing other parts of the physical exam (e.g., check chest/back while auscultating heart/lungs; check legs while testing reflexes and edema)

> Note: The USPSTF has found **insufficient evidence** to recommend routine skin cancer screenings for low-risk patients. PCPs therefore play an important role in initial screening.

## 4. Fitzpatrick Skin Types

The **Fitzpatrick skin scale** classifies skin type according to pigmentation and reaction to UV radiation. It has historically been used to assess skin cancer risk.

| Type | Description | UV Reaction |
|------|-------------|-------------|
| I | Pale white skin, blue/green eyes, blond/red hair | Always burns, never tans |
| II | Fair skin, blue eyes | Burns easily, tans poorly |
| III | Darker white skin | Tans after initial burn |
| IV | Light brown skin | Never burns, tans easily |
| V | Brown skin | Rarely burns, tans darkly |
| VI | Dark brown or black skin | Never burns, always tans |

**Controversy:** The correlation of skin pigmentation with skin cancer susceptibility is not linear. The scale may give patients and physicians a false sense of security that people of color cannot develop skin cancers.

## 5. Primary and Secondary Morphologies of Skin Lesions

### Primary Morphology
The **earliest appearance** of a disease lesion. Determined by: flat vs. raised, size, and contents.

**Non-palpable (flat) lesions:**

| Term | Size | Description | Example |
|------|------|-------------|---------|
| **Macule** | < 10 mm | Flat color change | Benign nevus (mole) |
| **Patch** | > 10 mm | Flat color change | Cafe-au-lait macule |

**Palpable (raised) lesions:**

| Term | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Papule** | Raised, < 10 mm | Dermatofibroma |
| **Plaque** | Raised, > 10 mm | Psoriasis |
| **Nodule** | Firm and deep, dermal or subcutaneous | Cyst, lipoma, rheumatoid nodule |
| **Vesicle** | Clear fluid-filled, < 10 mm | Herpes infection, miliaria ("heat rash") |
| **Bullae** | Clear fluid-filled, > 10 mm | Bullous pemphigoid |
| **Pustule** | Pus-filled (white, yellow, or hemorrhagic) | Acne, impetigo, pustular psoriasis |
| **Wheal** | Smooth edematous papule/plaque with surrounding erythema, very itchy | Urticaria (hives) |

### Secondary Morphology
Further characterization by **color, shape, texture, and location**. Describes changes over time, with scratching, or secondary infection.

| Term | Definition | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **Erosion** | Localized loss of epidermis or mucosa | Pemphigus foliaceus |
| **Excoriation** | Linear erosion from scratching/picking | Generalized itching |
| **Ulceration** | Loss of epidermis and (at least) upper dermis | Venous stasis ulcer, vasculitis |
| **Scale** | Excess stratum corneum in flakes/plates; often white or gray | Psoriasis |
| **Crust** | Hardened dried exudate (serous, hemorrhagic, or purulent) | Impetigo |
| **Eschar** | Adherent, thick, dry black crust | Cutaneous vasculitis |
| **Atrophy** | Thinning of skin; may look dry and wrinkled ("cigarette paper") | Morphea, long-term topical steroids |
| **Lichenification** | Thickened skin with accentuation of markings; from repeated rubbing | Chronic atopic dermatitis |

> **Memorize:** erosion, ulceration, and scale.

## 6. Approach to Morphological Description: The "Skin Menu"

Use this framework to build a complete lesion description:

| Category | Options |
|----------|---------|
| Size | Measure in cm |
| Color | Black, white, blue, purple, brown, tan, red, pink, yellow, violaceous, erythematous, hypopigmented, hyperpigmented, dusky |
| Basic/primary morphology | Macule, patch, papule, plaque, nodule, tumor, vesicle, bullae, pustule |
| Secondary morphology | Scale, crust, erosion, ulceration, excoriation, eschar, lichenification, atrophy |
| Demarcation | Well-demarcated, poorly-demarcated |
| Arrangement/configuration | Linear, grouped, scattered, reticular, polycyclic, annular, targetoid, serpiginous |
| Distribution | Generalized, unilateral, bilateral, chest, back, face, upper or lower extremities, sun-exposed |

**Template sentence:**
> "The skin examination is notable for __(size)__ cm __(color)__, __(primary lesion)__ with __(secondary changes)__ in a __(configuration)__ configuration, located over the __(distribution)__."

### Lesion Configurations

| Configuration | Appearance | Example |
|--------------|-----------|---------|
| **Grouped** | Clustered together | Herpes simplex/zoster |
| **Serpiginous** | Snake-like/serpentine | Cutaneous larva migrans |
| **Linear** | Straight line | Lichen planus (Koebner phenomenon) |
| **Targetoid** | Bulls-eye | Erythema multiforme |
| **Annular** | Ring-shaped | Granuloma annulare |
| **Reticular** | Net-like | Livedo reticularis |
| **Scattered** | Irregularly distributed | Eruptive xanthomas |
| **Polycyclic** | Coalescing rings/incomplete rings | Erythema gyratum repens |

## 7. Basic Anatomy and Histology of the Skin

### Three Main Layers

| Layer | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Epidermis** | Surface epithelium; mainly keratinocytes covered by keratin (stratum corneum) |
| **Dermis** | Connective tissue, blood vessels, oil and sweat glands, nerves, hair follicles |
| **Subcutaneous tissue (subcutis)** | Deepest layer; composed of adipose and connective tissue |

### Skin Appendages (Adnexa)

| Appendage | Function | Location |
|-----------|----------|----------|
| **Hair follicles** | Hair growth; tunnel-shaped structure | Throughout skin |
| **Sebaceous glands** | Holocrine secretion of sebum | Associated with hair follicles |
| **Eccrine glands** | Secrete sweat | Everywhere; concentrated on palms/soles |
| **Apocrine glands** | Secrete viscous fluid (may be malodorous) | Axillae, genitalia, areola |

### Layers of the Epidermis (innermost to outermost)

1. **Stratum basale** (basal layer) — stem cells that divide; melanosomes cap nuclei to protect from UV radiation; attached to basement membrane via **hemidesmosomes**
2. **Stratum spinosum** — keratinocytes connected by **desmosomes**
3. **Stratum granulosum** — contains **keratohyaline granules**
4. **Stratum lucidum** — only present on palms and soles
5. **Stratum corneum** — prevents **transepidermal water loss**; a thickened stratum corneum appears clinically as **scale or crust**

### Components of the Epidermis

| Cell Type | Proportion | Function |
|-----------|-----------|----------|
| **Keratinocytes** | ~90% | Form environmental barrier; produce keratin intermediate filaments |
| **Melanocytes** | ~1 per 4-10 basal keratinocytes | Produce melanin in melanosomes; transfer pigment to keratinocytes |
| **Langerhans cells** | Minority | Antigen-presenting cells (intraepidermal macrophages); migrate to lymph nodes when stimulated |
| **Merkel cells** | Minority | Slow-adapting mechanoreceptors for touch sensation; in basal layer |

### Cell Details

**Keratinocytes:**
- Form a functional barrier against temperature, UV radiation, water loss, and infection
- Connected to each other via **desmosomes** (adhesion molecules)
- Connected to basement membrane via **hemidesmosomes**
- Clinical: in **pemphigus vulgaris**, antibodies target desmosomes, causing keratinocytes to separate and form vesicles/bullae

**Melanocytes:**
- Originate from the **neural crest**
- Produce **melanosomes** (specialized organelles), then transfer pigment to keratinocytes
- Melanosomes in skin of color are larger and more evenly distributed, allowing darker skin to filter out ~2x as much UVB radiation
- Disease: **vitiligo** = destruction of melanocytes; **albinism** = normal melanocyte number but dysfunction in melanosomes; **melanoma** = malignant proliferation

**Langerhans cells:**
- Derived from monocyte-macrophage lineage
- Contain **Birbeck granules** (tennis racket-shaped organelles) on electron microscopy
- Malignant form: **Langerhans cell histiocytosis** — clonal proliferation, seen more in children; ranges from isolated bone disease to multisystem disease

**Merkel cells:**
- Located in the **basal layer**
- Slow-adapting mechanoreceptors of neural crest origin
- Found on tactile areas of hair-bearing skin, taste buds, anal canal, labial epithelium, and around eccrine glands
- Malignant form: **Merkel cell carcinoma** (associated with Merkel cell polyomavirus)

## 8. Key Terminology in Dermatopathology

Focus on the **bolded** terms; others are for reference.

| Term | Definition | Clinical Association |
|------|-----------|---------------------|
| **Acantholysis** | Separation of keratinocytes due to loss of intercellular adhesions (desmosomes) | Pemphigus vulgaris |
| **Acanthosis** | Thickened squamous/spinous cell layer | Psoriasis, lichen planus, seborrheic keratoses |
| Dyskeratosis | Abnormally/prematurely keratinized keratinocytes (eosinophilic cytoplasm) | Precancerous lesions |
| Epidermotropism | Abnormal cells within epidermis without significant spongiosis | Mycosis fungoides, melanoma metastasis |
| Exocytosis | Inflammatory cells within epidermis with spongiosis | Contact dermatitis, eczematous processes |
| Hypergranulosis | Increased thickness of the granular layer | Warts, lichen planus |
| Hypogranulosis | Decreased thickness of the granular layer | Psoriasis |
| **Hyperkeratosis** | Thickening of the stratum corneum (excess keratin) | Psoriasis, seborrheic keratoses, verrucae, SCC |
| Koilocytes | Squamous cells altered by HPV; cytoplasmic clearing around nucleus ("halo") | HPV/warts |
| **Orthokeratosis** | Normal stratum corneum; nuclei extruded from keratinocytes ("basket weave") | Normal skin |
| **Parakeratosis** | Retention of nuclei in the stratum corneum; altered keratinocyte maturation | Psoriasis, actinic keratosis |
| **Spongiosis** | Intercellular edema; widening of intercellular spaces | Contact dermatitis, atopic dermatitis |
| Vacuolar degeneration | Damage to basal layer with intracellular edema and clear spaces at dermal-epidermal junction | Necrotic keratinocytes |

## 9. Tissue Reaction Patterns in Dermatopathology

Focus on **spongiotic** and **psoriasiform** patterns.

### Spongiotic Pattern
- **Widening of intercellular spaces** (spongiosis) between keratinocytes
- Elongation of desmosomes (intercellular bridges)
- Inflammatory cells within intercellular spaces of epidermis
- **Classic association: atopic dermatitis and contact dermatitis ("eczema")**

### Psoriasiform Pattern
- **Regular elongation of rete ridges**
- Dilated capillaries in papillary dermis → easy bleeding when scale removed = **Auspitz sign**
- Thinning of the suprapapillary plate
- Acanthosis, hyperkeratosis, diffuse parakeratosis, hypogranulosis
- Neutrophils in stratum corneum
- **Classic association: psoriasis, pityriasis rubra pilaris, some drug eruptions**

### Lichenoid Pattern
- **Band-like infiltrate that hugs the dermal-epidermal junction**, blurring the line between epidermis and dermis
- **Classic association: lichen planus, certain drug eruptions**

### Vesiculobullous Pattern
- Splits (blisters) either **within** or **beneath** the epidermis
- **Intraepidermal split** (with acantholysis) → "flaccid," easily ruptured vesicles — **pemphigus vulgaris**
- **Subepidermal split** (below dermal-epidermal junction) → "tense," harder to break bullae — **bullous pemphigoid**

## 10. Skin Biopsy Techniques

| Technique | Method | Best Used For |
|-----------|--------|--------------|
| **Shave biopsy** | Blade/razor; partial or excisional (saucerization) | Pathology in epidermis or superficial dermis; removing exophytic benign lesions |
| **Punch biopsy** | "Cookie cutter" tool; removes down to subcutaneous fat | Pathology in dermis; when small sample can represent overall disease process |

## 11. Common Benign Skin Neoplasms

### Skin Tag (Acrochordon)
- Soft, **pedunculated** tan/brown papule
- Location: neck, axilla, groin
- Associated with **insulin resistance** (may be related to growth hormone-like activity of insulin)
- Increase with weight gain, pregnancy; may be associated with **diabetes**

### Seborrheic Keratoses (SKs)
- Very common; usually multiple; onset typically in **30s-40s**, increase with age
- Clinical: oval, raised, tan/brown/black papules and plaques with a verrucous, greasy **"stuck on"** appearance
- Location: chest, back, scalp, face, extremities
- **Sign of Leser-Trélat:** sudden appearance of hundreds of SKs in an adult; may be cutaneous sign of internal malignancy (usually **gastric adenocarcinoma** or lymphoma); controversial association
- **Dermatosis papulosa nigra:** SK variant; appears in ~35% of Black people and in Asian populations; brown/black papules starting around eyes, spreading to face and neck

### Cherry Angioma
- Extremely common benign **vascular lesions**; proliferation of dilated blood vessels
- Increase with age; asymptomatic
- Clinical: **bright red vascular papule**
- Distribution: trunk > extremities

### Verrucae (Warts)
- Cellular proliferation caused by **HPV virus**; spread by skin contact
- Variants: **verruca vulgaris** (common warts, hands/face/trunk), **verruca plana** (flat warts), **condyloma acuminata** (genital warts)
- Clinical: tan, scaly papules, often with **black dots** (thrombosed blood vessels)
- Histopathology: **koilocytes** (cells with cytoplasmic clearing/"halo" around nucleus)

### Keloid
- Benign **scar formation** with increased collagen deposition; develops soon or months after trauma
- Clinical: firm, rubbery nodules ranging from **pink to skin-colored**; can be itchy or painful
- Most common location: **earlobes** (after piercings)
- Treatment: **intralesional steroid injections (#1)**, surgical excision, radiation

### Lipoma
- Benign **tumor of fat**
- Clinical: soft, mobile **subcutaneous nodule**; typically asymptomatic
- **Angiolipoma** variant can be painful
- **Familial multiple lipomatosis:** rare genetic disorder with multiple lipomas on trunk, arms, and upper legs

### Dermatofibroma
- Benign fibrous nodule (mentioned in lecture summary; covered in full in this block)

### Sebaceous Hyperplasia
- Benign enlargement of sebaceous glands (mentioned in lecture summary; covered in full in this block)
