## Objective 1: Inflammatory vs. Non-Inflammatory Arthritis

| Feature | Inflammatory Pain | Noninflammatory Pain |
|---|---|---|
| Physical exam | Erythema, warmth, soft-tissue swelling, joint effusions; reduced ROM is frequent | Minimal or no warmth; no soft-tissue swelling; bony enlargement and joint effusions may occur in OA; reduced ROM may occur |
| Morning stiffness | >60 min; worsens with immobility | <30 min |
| Constitutional symptoms | Fever, fatigue, malaise | Generally absent |
| Synovial fluid | Leukocyte count >2000/uL; predominantly neutrophils (acute) or monocytes (chronic) | Leukocyte count 200-2000/uL; predominantly monocytes |
| Lab findings | Elevated ESR, CRP; anemia of chronic disease | Inflammatory markers usually normal or minimally elevated |
| Corticosteroid response | **Yes** | **No** |

### Examples by Category

**Inflammatory:**
- Rheumatoid arthritis
- Spondyloarthritis
- Psoriatic arthritis
- Reactive arthritis
- IBD-associated arthritis
- Crystal arthropathies (gout, CPPD)
- Connective tissue disease arthritis
- Infectious inflammatory arthritis (Parvovirus, Lyme disease)

**Non-Inflammatory:**
- Osteoarthritis
- Chronic CPPD
- Hemochromatosis
- Fibromyalgia
- Hypermobility syndrome

## Objective 2: Definition and Epidemiology of Rheumatoid Arthritis

**RA = Symmetrical synovitis (joint inflammation) of small and large joints**

- **Synovitis** = Pain + Tenderness + Swelling (requires physical examination to confirm)
- "Symmetrical" = both sides of the body, but not necessarily the exact same joints (e.g., right wrist and two left MCPs)

### Joint Definitions
- **Small joints:** MCPs (metacarpophalangeal), PIPs (proximal interphalangeal), MTPs (metatarsophalangeal), wrists
  - On exam: tender to palpation, puffy/spongy/boggy swelling
- **Large joints:** elbows, knees, ankles, shoulders
  - On exam: effusions, painful range of motion (ROM)

### Epidemiology
- Women affected 2-3x more than men
- Peak onset: 30-50 years; juvenile forms exist
- **Comorbidities and outcomes:**
  - 2x more likely to have MI or CVA
  - Increased risk of infection
  - Risk of lymphoma 3x greater than general population
  - Significant cause of disability: 35% of patients have work disability after 10 years

### Social Determinants of Health
- No disparity in disease activity across racial/ethnic groups when corrected for SES
- Significant difference in outcomes with lower SES; risk of co-morbidities greater in African Americans
- Environmental risk factors: cigarette smoking, airborne inhalants, fertilizers/solvents, stress, food availability

## Objective 3: Clinical Features and Diagnosis of RA

### Clinical and Lab Features Needed for Diagnosis

**Clinical:**
- Symmetrical synovitis
- Duration of symptoms >6 weeks (shorter duration may be viral or post-viral arthritis)

**Immunologic/Inflammatory:**
- Rheumatoid Factor (RF)
- Anti-CCP antibody
- ESR
- CRP

> Cannot diagnose RA using lab tests alone. Must have synovitis on exam.

### Rheumatoid Factor (RF)
- IgM antibody against IgG
- Sensitivity = 80%; Specificity = 80-95%
- **(−) does NOT rule out RA; (+) is likely RA**
- (+) RF associated with more aggressive joint disease and extra-articular manifestations
- **Causes of false-positive RF:** Sjogren's syndrome (most common), Hepatitis B or C, Sarcoidosis, Bacterial endocarditis, Cryoglobulinemia

### Anti-CCP Antibody
- Antibody to cyclic citrullinated peptide
- Sensitivity = 30-70%; Specificity = 70-90%
- **(−) does NOT rule out RA; (+) is likely RA**
- Must order CCP in addition to RF when suspicious of RA
- 20% of patients with RA have both a negative RF and negative CCP

### ESR (Erythrocyte Sedimentation Rate)
- Acute phase reactant; reflects fibrinogen production
- Elevated in: infection, malignancy, trauma, autoimmune diseases, diabetes, ESRD, anemia, thyroid disease
- Upper limit normals: Males = Age/2; Females = (Age + 10)/2
- Slower to respond than CRP; rises within 24-48 hrs of onset

### CRP (C-Reactive Protein)
- Induced by IL-6; rises and falls more rapidly than ESR
- Elevated in: infection, malignancy, autoimmune disease, trauma, acute organ inflammation
- Very high CRP >50 mg/dL associated with bacterial infections (~90%)
- NSAIDs and statins can falsely decrease CRP
- **When inflammatory disorder is suspected, priority should be given to CRP**

### Clinical Progression and Deformities
- Disease progresses from early RA (mild swelling) to intermediate to severe RA
- Classic deformities of longstanding disease:
  - **Boutonniere deformity:** flexion of PIP + hyperextension of DIP
  - **Z-deformity of the thumb:** hyperextension at the interphalangeal joint
  - Subluxation of MCP joints

### Extraarticular Manifestations
- Rheumatoid nodules
- Interstitial lung disease
- Pleuropericarditis
- Sjogren's syndrome
- Scleritis/episcleritis
- Digital infarcts
- Felty's syndrome
- Lower extremity ulcers
- Amyloidosis

### 2010 ACR Diagnostic Criteria (Score 6/10 = RA)

| Category | Criteria | Points |
|---|---|---|
| Synovitis | 1 medium-large joint | 0 |
| | 2-10 medium-large joints | 1 |
| | 1-3 small joints | 2 |
| | 4-10 small joints | 3 |
| | >10 joints (at least 1 small) | **5** |
| Serology | RF (−) and anti-CCP (−) | 0 |
| | RF (+) or anti-CCP (+) low positive | 2 |
| | High RF (+) or anti-CCP (+) (>3x ULN) | **3** |
| Duration | <6 weeks | 0 |
| | ≥6 weeks | 1 |
| Acute phase reactants | CRP and ESR within normal | 0 |
| | Elevated CRP or ESR | 1 |

> Must have synovitis on exam to make diagnosis. Cannot diagnose on lab tests alone.

### Imaging and Synovial Fluid
- **X-rays:** relatively insensitive; articular erosions suggest severe disease but may take years
- **Ultrasound / MRI:** valuable when unable to determine synovitis on physical exam
- **Arthrocentesis:** PMN >2k suggests inflammatory features

### Differential Diagnosis Clues
| If RA-like joint pain PLUS... | Consider... |
|---|---|
| Rashes | SLE, scleroderma, psoriatic arthritis |
| Age >50, hip and shoulder pain | Polymyalgia rheumatica; temporal arteritis |
| Inflammatory back pain, IBD, uveitis | Spondyloarthropathy |
| Short duration (<6 weeks) | Viral arthritis |
| Self-limiting, severe acute episodes | Crystalline arthropathy |

> RA **rarely** involves the DIP (distal interphalangeal) joints — this is characteristic of osteoarthritis.

## Objective 4: Common Treatment Regimens for RA

### Goals of Treatment
- Control pain (measured by pain scale 0-10)
- Improve function (self-report of activity)
- Prevent progression (pain + function + imaging)
- Avoid toxicity (vaccinations; Quantiferon; avoid during infection)
- Protect pregnancy (test prior to drug initiation; avoid teratogenic drugs)

### Short-Term Treatment: NSAIDs and Corticosteroids
- Used for immediate, short-term relief while immunosuppressive therapies take effect (up to 3 months)
- **Avoid NSAIDs if:** aspirin allergy/anticoagulation (A), GI bleeding (B), elevated creatinine/CHF (C)
- **Corticosteroids:** more potent; low dose 2.5-10 mg daily prednisone preferred
  - Risk of infection increases at ≥20 mg prednisone daily
  - Attempt taper within 3 months of starting immunosuppressive treatment
  - Monitor bone health

### Long-Term Treatment: DMARDs and Biologics

**Traditional DMARDs (small molecules, oral):**
- **Methotrexate (MTX)**, Leflunomide, Azathioprine — decrease T-cell proliferation
- **Hydroxychloroquine (HCQ)** — Toll receptor inhibitor
- **Sulfasalazine (SSZ)**
- **Apremilast** — inhibits PDE4, decreases TNF/IL

**Biologics (monoclonal antibodies, IV or SQ):**
- **TNF inhibitors:** etanercept, adalimumab, certolizumab, infliximab, golimumab — bind TNF
- **Abatacept** — inhibits APC/T cell interaction
- **Tocilizumab, Sarilumab** — bind IL-6
- **Secukinumab, Ixekizumab** — bind IL-17
- **Guselkumab, Tildrakizumab, Rizankizumab** — bind IL-23
- **Rituximab** — targets B cell receptor

**Small Molecule Biologics (JAK inhibitors, oral):**
- **Tofacitinib, Baricitinib, Upadicitinib** — JAK inhibitor → decrease cytokines

> Biologics are protein molecules requiring IV or SQ administration. Small molecules are oral.
> MTX or Leflunomide in combination with a biologic or JAK inhibitor is **more effective** than monotherapy.
> **Cannot combine a JAK inhibitor with a biologic agent.**

### Step Treatment for RA

| Step | Disease Activity | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mild disease | HCQ or SSZ |
| 2 | Moderate to severe | MTX (monotherapy) ± HCQ ± SSZ |
| 3 | Intolerant or ineffective | MTX + TNF inhibitor OR MTX/Leflunomide + Abatacept |
| 4 | Next step | IL-6 inhibitor ± MTX/Leflunomide OR JAK inhibitor ± MTX/Leflunomide |
| 5 | | Rituximab |
| 6 | | Clinical trials |

**Notes on step selection:**
- Choice of biologic or JAK inhibitor based on potential side effects, contraindications, and insurance approval
- Contraindications to specific agents:
  - Demyelinating disease: avoid TNF inhibitors
  - Severe cardiovascular disease/VTE: avoid JAK inhibitors
  - Severe diverticular disease: avoid Tocilizumab
  - Hepatic disease: avoid MTX and Leflunomide
  - Severe renal disease: avoid MTX
- **MTX and Leflunomide are contraindicated in pregnancy**

### General Rules of Treatment
- Allow adequate time to observe drug efficacy:
  - HCQ and SSZ: 3-6 months
  - MTX and Leflunomide: 3 months
  - TNF inhibitors: 2-3 months
  - Other biologics: 2-3 months
- High disease activity → modify treatment
- Moderate disease activity → modify or observe
- Low disease activity → observe
- Minimize use of steroids

## Objective 5: Common Treatment Outcomes for RA

- **Remission:** 5-24%
- **Low or minimal disease activity:** 25-30%
- **At least 20% improvement:** 75%
- **Serious infection rate:** <0.5-4%

### Measuring Outcomes

| Goal | Measure | Disease |
|---|---|---|
| Decrease pain | Visual Analogue Pain Scale (0-10) | RA and SpA |
| Improve function | Self-reported tasks (HAQ/RAPID 3); Joint ROM | RA and SpA |
| Decrease inflammation | Tender and swollen joints; dactylitis/enthesitis; AM stiffness >1hr; ESR and CRP | RA and SpA |
| Overall disease activity | "Disease Activity" by MD and patient (0-10 scale) | RA and SpA |
| Slow progression | Joint erosion on imaging; SI joint/spinal ligament calcification; ROM | RA and SpA |

### Drug Toxicities

| Drug | Principal Side Effects |
|---|---|
| Hydroxychloroquine | Retinal toxicity, rash |
| Sulfasalazine | Rash, GI |
| Methotrexate | Elevated LFTs, cytopenia, serious infection (~0.9%), GI, flu-like; **TERATOGENIC** |
| Leflunomide | Diarrhea, hair loss; **TERATOGENIC** |
| TNF inhibitors | Serious infection (1-4%), lymphoma risk |
| Abatacept | Serious infection (1-2%) |
| IL-6 inhibitors | Low WBC, lipids, serious infection (2%), diverticular perforation |
| JAK inhibitors | CBC changes, lipids, serious infection (2%) |
| Apremilast | Depression |
| IL-17 & IL-23 inhibitors | Serious infection (1%) |

> **Serious infection = hospitalized infection** (sepsis, pneumonia, pyelonephritis, disseminated viral infection)
> Common to ALL immunosuppressive treatments.
> TB can be re-activated — check Quantiferon before starting.
> Cannot give live vaccines (Yellow Fever, MMR, rotavirus) while on immunosuppressives.

## Objective 6: Define Spondyloarthropathies and Understand the Subtypes

**Spondyloarthropathy (SpA)** = a group of inflammatory arthritis conditions sharing two features:
1. **Seronegative** arthritis (RF and CCP negative)
2. **Peripheral and/or axial** inflammatory arthritis

### Key Definitions
- **Enthesitis** = inflammation of tendons/ligaments at their attachment to bone (e.g., bilateral Achilles tendonitis)
- **Dactylitis** = inflammation of an entire digit ("sausage digit")
- **Uveitis** = inflammation of the anterior chamber of the eye
- **Sacroiliitis** = inflammation of the SI joints
- **Inflammatory Back Pain (IBP):** >30 min AM stiffness; back pain decreases with exercise but NOT rest; awakening from back pain in second half of night only; alternating buttock pain

### SpA Subtypes (under the "umbrella")

| Subtype | Key Features |
|---|---|
| **Ankylosing Spondylitis (AS)** | Bilateral sacroiliitis (100%), B27 (+) 90%, fuses spine; prevalence 0.1-0.2%; male:female 3:1 |
| **Psoriatic Arthritis (PsA)** | 20-30% sacroiliitis (unilateral), does NOT fuse spine; almost all have peripheral arthritis and psoriasis; B27 ~50%; prevalence 0.2-0.4%; male:female 1:1 |
| **Reactive Arthritis (Reiter's)** | Follows GI or GU infection; B27 (+) 60-80%; 90% peripheral arthritis; male:female 5:1 |
| **Enteropathic (IBD-associated)** | 20% of Crohn's/UC patients; primarily peripheral arthritis; B27 ~20%; any age |
| **Undifferentiated SpA** | Features of SpA without fitting a specific subtype |

### SpA vs. RA Comparison

| Feature | RA | SpA |
|---|---|---|
| RF or CCP | (+) 80% | (--) |
| Synovitis | Small/large, **symmetrical** | **Asymmetrical** |
| Enthesitis | Uncommon | **Common** |
| Dactylitis | **Absent** | 20-70% |
| Sacroiliitis | **Absent** | 20-100% |
| Spondylitis | **Absent** | 10-100% |

## Objective 7: How to Diagnose Spondyloarthropathies (SpA)

SpA is classified as **primarily axial** or **primarily peripheral:**
- **Primarily Axial:** Ankylosing Spondylitis
- **Primarily Peripheral:** Psoriatic Arthritis, Reactive Arthritis, Enteropathic Arthritis
- **Undifferentiated** spans both

### ASAS Criteria: Peripheral SpA
Must have **Arthritis OR Enthesitis OR Dactylitis**, PLUS:

**At least 1 of:**
- Psoriasis
- Inflammatory bowel disease
- Preceding infection
- HLA-B27
- Uveitis
- Sacroiliitis on imaging

**OR at least 2 of:**
- Arthritis, Enthesitis, Dactylitis
- IBP in the past
- Positive family history for SpA

> If one of the major "plus 1" features is present, HLA-B27 does not need to be ordered — and if negative it does not rule out peripheral SpA.

### ASAS Criteria: Axial SpA
For patients with back pain ≥3 months AND age at onset <45 years:

**Sacroiliitis on imaging + ≥1 SpA feature**
OR
**HLA-B27 + ≥2 SpA features**

**SpA features include:** inflammatory back pain, arthritis, enthesitis (heel), uveitis, dactylitis, psoriasis, Crohn's/UC, good response to NSAIDs, family history of SpA, HLA-B27, elevated CRP

**Sacroiliitis on imaging:** active inflammation on MRI highly suggestive of sacroiliitis, OR definitive radiographic sacroiliitis per modified New York criteria.

> If sacroiliitis on imaging + 1 SpA feature → HLA-B27 does not need to be ordered.
> Negative HLA-B27 does not rule out axial SpA if sacroiliitis is present on imaging.

### Labs and Imaging for SpA

| Test | Ankylosing Spondylitis | Reactive Arthritis | Psoriatic Arthritis |
|---|---|---|---|
| HLA-B27 (+) | 90% | 60-70% | 50-60% |
| Sacroiliitis | 100%, bilateral | 30%, unilateral | 20-30%, unilateral |

- 10% of normal population is HLA-B27 (+)

### Physical Examination Tests for SpA
- **Occiput-to-wall test:** heels and scapulae touch wall; occiput should also — tests cervical spine involvement
- **Modified Schober test:** detects limited lumbar forward flexion (mark at PSIS and 10 cm above; should expand to ≥15 cm)
- **Chest expansion:** measured at 4th intercostal space (men) or below breasts (women); normal ~5 cm; <2.5 cm is abnormal
- **Pelvic compression:** pain in SI joint when pelvis compressed while lying on side
- **Gaenslen's test:** one leg dropped off table, one drawn to chest; SI joint pain at dropped-leg side
- **Patrick's test (FABER):** flexion-abduction-external rotation with downward pressure on flexed knee causes contralateral SI joint pain

### Radiographic Findings in Ankylosing Spondylitis
- Early: sacroiliitis on plain film or MRI
- Advanced: **bamboo spine** (vertebral fusion), vertebral squaring, osteopenia, ossification of spinal ligaments

## Objective 8: Common Treatment Regimens for SpA

### Step Treatment: Peripheral SpA

| Step | Treatment |
|---|---|
| 1 | NSAID |
| 2 | Methotrexate (MTX) monotherapy |
| 3 | MTX or Leflunomide + TNF inhibitor |
| 4 | Apremilast ± MTX or Leflunomide |
| 5 | JAK inhibitor or IL-17 or IL-23 inhibitor ± MTX or Leflunomide |
| 6 | Clinical trial |

### Step Treatment: Axial SpA

| Step | Treatment |
|---|---|
| 1 | NSAIDs |
| 2 | **TNF inhibitor** |
| 3 | JAK inhibitor or IL-17 or IL-23 inhibitor |

> **IMPORTANT: Methotrexate and Leflunomide are NOT effective in purely axial disease.**
> Cannot combine a JAK inhibitor with a biologic agent.

### FDA Approval Summary

| Drug Class | RA | PsA | Ankylosing Spondylitis |
|---|---|---|---|
| TNF inhibitors | Yes (all) | Yes (all) | Yes |
| Abatacept | Yes | No | No |
| IL-6 inhibitors | Yes | No | No |
| JAK inhibitors | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| MTX, Leflunomide, Azathioprine | Yes | Yes | Not effective |
| IL-17 inhibitors | No | Yes | Yes |
| IL-23 inhibitors | No | Yes | No |
| Apremilast (PDE4 inhibitor) | No | Yes (SpA only) | No |

### Non-Pharmacologic Treatment
Provides 20-30% improvement:
- Exercise
- Self-management education
- Physical therapy
- Acupuncture
- Psychological techniques (distraction, relaxation, meditation, positive imagery)

## Key Summary Points

### RA
- **Symmetrical synovitis** in small and large joints; RF/CCP (+) or (−); need ≥6/10 ACR criteria
- Diagnosis **requires synovitis on exam** — cannot diagnose with labs alone
- 20% of patients with RA have both negative RF and negative CCP
- Treatment: stepwise — HCQ/SSZ → MTX → MTX + biologic/JAK inhibitor
- MTX + biologic/JAK is more effective than monotherapy
- **MTX and Leflunomide are teratogenic**; all immunosuppressives carry infection risk

### SpA
- **Asymmetric synovitis**, enthesitis, **dactylitis**, sacroiliitis, spondylitis; **RF and CCP negative**; HLA-B27 (+) or (−)
- ASAS Criteria: Peripheral (arthritis/enthesitis/dactylitis + features) OR Axial (≥3 months back pain + sacroiliitis or B27 + features)
- **RA dx can be made with negative RF and CCP**
- **SpA dx can be made with negative HLA-B27**
- **History and physical exam are the keys to diagnosis**
- MTX/Leflunomide effective in peripheral SpA but **NOT in purely axial disease**
- TNF inhibitor is the step-2 agent for axial SpA (not MTX)
- Diagnostic testing: RF, CCP, B27, and MRI (if suspicion of spondylitis or sacroiliitis)
