## 1. Opioid Receptors & Exogenous Opioids

### Opioid Receptors (GPCRs - Gi/o subtype)

| Receptor | Effect of Agonism |
|----------|-------------------|
| **Mu (most important)** | **Analgesia, euphoria, sedation**, respiratory depression, decreased gastric motility |
| Kappa | Spinal analgesia, **dysphoria** |
| Delta | Some analgesia, decreased gastric motility (low yield) |

- Dysphoria from kappa becomes important in **withdrawal**: chronic mu agonism upregulates kappa signaling, so when mu is withdrawn, unopposed kappa causes dysphoria

### Exogenous Opioids

| Prodrug | Active Opiate | Key Features |
|---------|--------------|--------------|
| Codeine | **Morphine** | Hospital standard, IV/IM preferred, **metabolites accumulate in renal failure** |
| Oxycodone | Oxymorphone | Oxycontin = extended release |
| Hydrocodone (Norco) | **Hydromorphone** (Dilaudid) | Rapid onset mu agonist, used in PCA, no active metabolites |
| — | Heroin | Diacetylated morphine, drug of abuse |

**Synthetic opioids:**
- **Fentanyl**: extremely fast, extremely potent pure agonist
- **Meperidine** (Demerol): anti-cholinergic (mydriasis masks miosis of OD!), Na+ channel blocking, first synthetic opioid
- **Methadone**: very long-acting, NMDA antagonist, iKr effect causes **QT prolongation (Torsades risk)**
- **Tramadol**: SNRI with opioid metabolites, good for neuropathic pain, less potent mu agonist

## 2. General Anesthetics

### Inhaled Anesthetics

**Agents**: Nitrous oxide, sevoflurane, isoflurane, halothane

**MOA**: Unknown!

**Side effects (PD):**
1. **Decreased ventilatory drive** → must secure airway (exception: nitrous oxide preserves ventilatory drive)
2. **Decreased BP/contractility** → need hemodynamic support (exception: nitrous oxide is CV stable)
3. Increased cerebral blood flow → increased ICP
4. Nausea/vomiting
5. Inhibited temperature regulation
6. **Malignant hyperthermia** if mutant RyR → treat with **dantrolene**

### MAC (Minimum Alveolar Concentration)

- = % anesthetic gas in alveoli to prevent movement in **50% of population** (ED50)
- **Low MAC = High potency** (inversely proportional to lipophilicity)
- 1.2 MAC = ED95 (95% don't respond)
- **MACs are additive** across drugs (combine drugs to reach target MAC)
- Eliminated **only via ventilation** (not metabolism)

### Speed up onset by:
1. **Decrease blood solubility** (lower blood:gas partition coefficient) — less soluble in blood = faster to brain
2. Increase inspired concentration
3. Increase ventilation

> Do NOT confuse: **lipid solubility** (correlates with potency/low MAC) vs. **blood solubility** (correlates with slower onset)

### IV Anesthetics (highly lipophilic → quick on/off → used for induction)

| Agent | MOA | Distinguishing Feature | Cons |
|-------|-----|----------------------|------|
| Midazolam | BDZ (GABA-A) | Anxiolytic, amnesia | — |
| Fentanyl | Opioid | **Blunts airway reactivity** | — |
| Thiopental | Barbiturate (GABA-A) | Amnesia, sedation | CV/resp depressant |
| Etomidate | GABA-A | **CV/respiratory stable** | Nausea, adrenal suppression |
| Ketamine | NMDA antagonist | **Dissociative, CV stimulatory, resp stable**, good for trauma | — |
| Propofol | GABA-A | **Antiemetic** | CV/resp depressant, PRIS, no analgesia |
| Dexmedetomidine | Alpha-2 agonist | **Respiratory stable** | CV depressant |

## 3. Eicosanoid Pharmacology / NSAIDs

### COX-1 vs COX-2

| | COX-1 ("Housekeeping") | COX-2 ("Inducible during stress") |
|---|---|---|
| Expression | Always on, constitutive | Induced by inflammation |
| Functions | Thromboxane/platelet aggregation, GI mucus protection, renal vasodilation | Pain/inflammation signaling (PGE2 → fever) |

### NSAIDs: General Principles
- **MOA**: inhibit COX enzymes → decrease prostaglandin production
- **Weak acids** → more active in **acidic tissue** (inflamed tissue, stomach, kidneys)
- Can be **irreversible** (aspirin) or **reversible** (ibuprofen, naproxen, etc.)
- Can be **selective** for COX-2 (celecoxib) or **non-selective** (all others)

### Adverse Effects of NSAIDs: **GI, Cardiac, Renal**
| Organ | Prostaglandin Role | NSAID Effect |
|-------|-------------------|--------------|
| Platelets | COX1 → TXA2 → aggregation | **Anticoagulation** |
| Vascular | PGE2/PGI2 → inflammation, inhibit platelet agg | Anti-inflammatory; COX2-selective → **CV risk (MI/stroke)** |
| Kidneys | COX1&2 → PGE vasodilates afferent arteriole | **Pre-renal AKI** |
| CNS | PGE2 → fever | Decreased fever |
| GI | PGE2 → mucosal protection | **GI ulcers & bleeding** |
| Lungs | PGE2 → bronchodilation | Aspirin-sensitive asthma (AERD) |

### Key Individual NSAIDs

- **Aspirin (ASA)**: **IRREVERSIBLE** COX1/2 inhibitor → platelets can't make thromboxane for ~7 days (platelet lifespan). Cardioprotective. Avoid in children (**Reye syndrome**) and AERD (**Samter's triad**: nasal polyps, aspirin sensitivity, asthma)
- **Celecoxib**: only COX-2 selective NSAID → **least GI side effects**, doesn't affect platelet aggregation, but **increased CV mortality**
- **Naproxen**: long half life (good for predictable pain)
- **Ibuprofen**: short half life (good for unpredictable pain)
- **Ketorolac** (Toradol): only NSAID available IV/IM

### Prostaglandin Analogs

| Prostaglandin | Analog | Use |
|--------------|--------|-----|
| PGE1 | Alprostadil | Erectile dysfunction |
| PGE1 | **Misoprostol** | Postpartum hemorrhage, medication abortion, GI protection |
| PGI2 | Epoprostenol | Pulmonary arterial hypertension |
| PGF2a | Carboprost | Postpartum hemorrhage |
| PGF2a | Bimatoprost/latanoprost | Glaucoma (eye drops) |

### Acetaminophen (APAP) Toxicity
- FDA max daily dose: **4 grams**; toxic dose: >7.5-10g (narrow therapeutic index)
- Toxicity pathway: overwhelms UDP-glucuronyl transferase → shunted to **CYP2E1** → produces **NAPQI** (toxic) → **Zone 3 hepatic necrosis**
- **Treatment**: **N-acetylcysteine (NAC)** to replenish glutathione (best within 16-24 hrs)

## 4. Toxicology & Reversal Agents

### The Big Table

| Agent | Distinguishing Symptoms | Reversal | Mechanism |
|-------|------------------------|----------|-----------|
| **Opioids** | CNS/resp/CV depression, **MIOSIS** | **Naloxone** | Competitive antagonist |
| **Benzodiazepines** | CNS/resp/CV depression, **NO miosis** | **Flumazenil** | Competitive antagonist |
| **Organophosphate** | DUMBELLS (Diarrhea, Urination, **Miosis**, Bradycardia, Emesis, Lacrimation, Lethargy, Salivation) | **Pralidoxime** + **Atropine** | Regenerate AChE; Antagonist |
| **Anticholinergic** | "Dry": urinary retention, **mydriasis**, absent bowel sounds | **Physostigmine** | Increase endogenous ACh to outcompete |
| **Aspirin** | Lactic acidosis / **respiratory alkalosis** | **Sodium bicarbonate** | Trap weak acid in basic urine |
| **TCA** | QT prolongation/QRS widening, anticholinergic sx | **Sodium bicarbonate** | Stabilize cardiac membranes |
| **Torsades** (any QTc drug) | Torsades de pointes | **Magnesium** | Stabilize cardiac membranes |
| **Acetaminophen** | Hepatotoxicity (elevated AST/ALT) | **N-acetylcysteine** | Replenish glutathione |
| **Local anesthetics** | LAST (CV/CNS toxicity), methemoglobinemia | **Lipid emulsion** | Increase Vd |
| **Beta blockers** | Bradycardia, hypotension, loss of consciousness | **Glucagon/atropine**; Intralipid | Bypass blocked B1; Increase Vd |
| **Ethylene glycol** | Coma, N/V, metabolic acidosis | **Fomepizole/ethanol**; Dialysis | Prevent/remove toxic metabolite |
| **Lead** | LEAD lines, Encephalopathy, Erythrocyte basophilic stippling, Abdominal colic, Drops (wrist/foot) | **Ca-EDTA, dimercaprol, succimer** | Chelation |
| **Mercury** | Tremor, neuropsych disturbance, gingivostomatitis | Dimercaprol, succimer | Chelation |
| **Arsenic** | "Stocking/glove" neuropathy, hyperpigmentation | Dimercaprol, succimer | Chelation |
| **Methemoglobinemia** | O2 sat ~86%, **chocolate-colored blood** | **Methylene blue** | Reduces Fe3+ → Fe2+ |
| **Carbon monoxide** | O2 sat ~99% (falsely normal!), headache, confusion | **100% O2 / hyperbaric** | Outcompete CO |
| **Lithium** | Nystagmus, tremor, confusion, nephrogenic DI | Dialysis | Remove toxic agent |

### Eye Findings as Clues
- **Miosis** (constriction): opioids vs. cholinergics (distinguish by diarrhea/lacrimation/salivation in cholinergic)
- **Mydriasis** (dilation): sympathomimetics (+ diaphoresis/sweating) vs. anticholinergics (+ dry skin, urinary retention)
- **Vertical nystagmus**: NMDA antagonists (PCP, ketamine, dextromethorphan)

### Opioid vs BDZ Overdose
Both are "downers" with somnolence, ataxia, coma, bradycardia, hypotension, resp depression. **Key difference**: opioids cause **miosis (pinpoint pupils)**; BDZs have **no effect on pupils**.
- Opioid OD → **Naloxone**
- BDZ OD → **Flumazenil** (multiple doses needed; short half life vs BDZs)

### Distinguishing Hyperthermic Syndromes

| | Malignant Hyperthermia | Anticholinergic | Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome | Serotonin Syndrome | Sympathomimetic |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cause | Inhaled anesthetics, succinylcholine | TCAs, Jimson weed | Antipsychotics, DA agonist withdrawal | Antidepressants, opioids, linezolid | Cocaine, meth, MDMA |
| Key Sx | Rhabdo, CO2 rise, rigidity | Dilated pupils, dry, absent bowel sounds | Lead pipe rigidity | Hyperactive bowels, ocular clonus, pupillary dilation | Pupillary dilation, diaphoresis, tachycardia |
| Tx | **Dantrolene** (RyR blocker) | **Physostigmine** | Stop DA agonist, restart if withdrawn | **Cyproheptadine** (5HT blocker) | **Beta blockers**; BDZs if rigid |

### Alcohol Withdrawal
- Presents with sympathetic hyperactivation, autonomic instability, **formication** ("bugs crawling on skin")
- **Withdrawal sx are opposite of intoxication** (anxiety vs sedation, hypertension vs hypotension, hyperthermia vs hypothermia)
- Treatment escalation: Alcohol withdrawal (6-24h) → BDZs/dexmedetomidine; Delirium tremens (48-96h) → high dose BDZs + ICU; Wernicke-Korsakoff → **Thiamine (B1) + Folic Acid (B9)** ("Banana Bag")

### Lead Poisoning
- Mnemonic **LEAD**: Lead lines (Burton lines on gingiva), Encephalopathy & Erythrocyte basophilic stippling, Abdominal colic & sideroblastic Anemia, Drops (wrist & foot drop)
- Buzzwords: old houses, chipped paint, old pipes
- Inhibits multiple heme synthesis enzymes
- At-risk: children (oral habits, rapid bone growth, immature BBB), iron-deficient, pregnant
- Tx: chelation (EDTA, dimercaprol, succimer) for high/persistent levels
- **No safe blood lead level in children**

### Hemoglobin Toxicities

| | Methemoglobinemia | CO Poisoning |
|---|---|---|
| O2 sat | **~86%** | **~99%** (falsely normal!) |
| Key finding | **Chocolate-colored blood**, cyanosis | Headache, nausea, confusion (nonspecific) |
| Cause | Ester local anesthetics (benzocaine), dapsone | Carbon monoxide |
| Tx | **Methylene blue** | **100% O2 / hyperbaric** |

Pulse ox reads CO-bound Hb as O2-bound Hb (same wavelength absorption), explaining the falsely normal reading.
