### Antibiotic Mnemonics

- **"Naf for simple Staph"**: Use nafcillin for MSSA
- **"My Cool Lungs Feel Awfully Tired"**: Atypical pneumonia (Mycoplasma, Chlamydia, Legionella) → treated with Fluoroquinolones, Azithromycin, Tetracyclines
- **HeNS PEcKing are HACEKing**: Gram- covered by ceph generations → 1st gen = PEcK (Proteus, E. coli, Klebsiella); 2nd gen = HeNS (H. infl, Neisseria, Serratia); 3rd gen = HACEK → infective endocarditis
- **"Furry fox drinking tea in a dark den"**: Cefuroxime, cefoxitin, cefotetan = 2nd gen cephs → anaerobes
- **MonoBATMAN**: Monobactams (aztreonam) only useful in special situations (PCN allergy, pseudomonas)
- **Errrrtapenem**: Only carbapenem WITHOUT pseudomonas/enterococcus coverage
- **"Don't tickle the tiger!"**: Tigecycline rarely used, only for VRE
- **"-CIN" of aminoglycosides**: Crush Incus and Nephrons (ototoxic, nephrotoxic)
- **"Buy AT $30, CELL at $50"**: 30S = Aminoglycosides, Tetracycline; 50S = Chloramphenicol, Erythromycin (macrolides), Linezolid, cLindamycin
- **FLUORoquinolones on the FLOOR**: Tendon rupture (black box warning)
- **"Many Cars Can't Park Between 2 and 5 PM"**: Anaerobic coverage = Metronidazole, Clindamycin, Carbapenems, Pip/taz, 2nd gen ceph, 5th gen ceph

## 2. Beta-Lactam Antibiotics

### General Principles
- All share a **beta-lactam ring** structure
- All are **bactericidal** and **time-dependent** (T>MIC)
- MOA: Bind **Penicillin-Binding Proteins (PBPs)** → disrupt cell wall cross-linking → lysis
- **Resistance**: Gram-positives mutate PBP (e.g., MRSA = PBP2a); Gram-negatives make **beta-lactamase** enzymes (including ESBLs)

### Penicillins (all end in -cillin)

| Subclass | Drugs | Coverage | Key Facts |
|----------|-------|----------|-----------|
| Natural | Penicillin G/V | Strep, syphilis | Pen V = PO, Pen G = IV/IM |
| Anti-staph | **Nafcillin**, Dicloxacillin | **MSSA** | Beta-lactamase resistant; "Naf for simple staph" |
| Aminopenicillins | **Amoxicillin**, Ampicillin | Strep, enterococcus, ENT, listeria | Add clavulanate/sulbactam for gram- & anaerobic coverage |
| Extended-spectrum | **Piperacillin**/tazobactam | **Pseudomonas**, gram+, gram-, anaerobes | "P in Piperacillin for Pseudomonas"; first-line for sepsis (+vanc for MRSA) |

- **Penicillins NEVER cover MRSA or ESBLs**
- Adverse effects: **Type 1 hypersensitivity (anaphylaxis)**, autoimmune hemolytic anemia, interstitial nephritis, renal dose adjustment needed

### Cephalosporins (all start with cef/ceph-)

| Gen | Drugs | Key Coverage | Mnemonics |
|-----|-------|-------------|-----------|
| 1st | Cephalexin, Cefazolin | Best gram+, MSSA | Sounds like "ceFA-something"; skin/surgical prophylaxis |
| 2nd | Cefoxitin, Cefuroxime | **Anaerobes** | "Furry FOX drinking tea in a dark den" |
| 3rd | **Ceftriaxone**, Ceftazidime | Broad gram+/gram-, meningitis, CAP, gonorrhea | CefTRIaxone = 3rd; Ceftazidime for **pseudomonas & ESBLs** |
| 4th | Cefepime | Great gram-, **pseudomonas** | — |
| 5th | **Ceftaroline**, Ceftolozane | **+MRSA** (ceftaroline); ESBLs/pseudomonas (ceftolozane) | CeftaRoline for MRSA |

- **Cephalosporins are LAME** (don't cover): **L**isteria, **A**typicals, **M**RSA (except 5th gen), **E**nterococcus
- Higher generations = better gram- but worse gram+ (except 5th gen adds MRSA back)

### Carbapenems (all end in -penem)
- Meropenem, Doripenem, Imipenem (+cilastatin), and *err* **Ertapenem**
- **"Big gun" broadest-spectrum beta-lactams**: cover everything except MRSA
- **Drug of choice for ESBLs/multi-drug resistance**
- Ertapenem is the awkward one: does NOT cover pseudomonas or enterococcus

### Monobactam (Aztreonam)
- Safe for **penicillin/cephalosporin allergy** (no cross-reactivity)
- Only active against **gram-negatives** (including pseudomonas)
- **MonoBATMAN**: only useful in special situations

## 3. Non-Beta-Lactam Antibiotics

### Vancomycin (Glycopeptide)
- MOA: Binds **D-Ala-D-Ala** residues → prevents cell wall cross-linking
- **Drug of choice for MRSA** (not affected by beta-lactamases — no beta-lactam ring)
- Gram-positive only; also covers **C. diff** (oral vanc)
- **GREEDY**: time- AND concentration-dependent (AUC/MIC)
- Side effects: **nephrotoxicity, ototoxicity**, Red-man syndrome (histamine release with fast infusion → slow it down)
- Resistance: VRE converts D-Ala-D-Ala to **D-Ala-D-Lac**

### Daptomycin
- MOA: Ca2+-mediated pore formation in bacterial membrane → depolarization → cell death
- **Gram+ only**, reserved for **vancomycin-resistant** pathogens (VRE, VRSA)
- **Inactivated by lung surfactant** → do NOT use for pneumonia (use Linezolid instead)
- Side effects: rhabdomyolysis/myopathy → check CK; avoid combining with statins

### Linezolid
- MOA: Inhibits **50s ribosomal subunit** (initiation complex) → bacterio**static**
- **Gram+ only**, reserved for **vancomycin-resistant** pathogens
- Contrast with daptomycin: **good oral bioavailability & works in lungs** → good for pneumonia
- Side effects: **thrombocytopenia**; weak MAOi → risk of **serotonin syndrome** (especially with SSRIs)

### What treats MRSA?
Big 3: **Vancomycin** (1st line), **Daptomycin & Linezolid** (reserved for vanc-resistant)
Also: **5th gen cephalosporins** (ceftaroline), tetracyclines (community), TMP/SMX (community), clindamycin (community)

### Fluoroquinolones (-floxacin)
- MOA: Inhibit bacterial **topoisomerases** → bactericidal, concentration-dependent (Cmax>MIC)
- All cover **atypical organisms**
- **Cipro** = best gram- = "urinary & GI fluoroquinolone" (UTI, pyelo)
- **Levo/Moxi** = "respiratory fluoroquinolones" (gram+ coverage for S. pneumo + atypicals + anaerobes for moxi)
- Side effects: **tendon rupture** (black box!), QT prolongation, peripheral neuropathy, teratogen
- Don't combine with **divalent cations** (tums, dairy, iron)
- Cannot give to children or pregnant patients

### Aminoglycosides (Gentamicin, Tobramycin, Amikacin)
- MOA: Inhibit **30s ribosomal subunit** → protein misfolding → bactericidal
- **Concentration-dependent** (Cmax>MIC); only work on **aerobic bacteria**
- Broad-spectrum **gram-negative** agents (including pseudomonas, ESBLs)
- Side effects: **Ototoxicity (irreversible)** and **Nephrotoxicity (reversible)**
- Mnemonic: "-CIN" = Crush Incus and Nephrons

### Macrolides (-thromycin): Azithromycin, Clarithromycin, Erythromycin
- MOA: Inhibit **50s ribosomal subunit** (translocation) → bacterio**static**
- Time AND concentration-dependent (AUC/MIC)
- **Drug of choice for CAP** (covers atypicals + S. pneumo) = "Z pack"
- Also: whooping cough (Bordetella), Clarithromycin for H. pylori
- Side effects: **QT prolongation**, hepatic injury, CYP450 inhibitor, erythromycin promotes gut motility

### Tetracyclines (-cycline): Doxycycline, Minocycline, Tigecycline
- MOA: Inhibit **30s ribosomal subunit** → bacterio**static**
- Cover gram+, **atypicals**, and **community-acquired MRSA**
- **Doxycycline** = zoonoses (ticks), acne, chlamydia STI, CAP backup
- Side effects: **tooth discoloration & bone growth depression** (contraindicated in children/pregnant), **photosensitivity**, don't combine with divalent cations

### Clindamycin
- MOA: Inhibit **50s ribosomal subunit** → **prevents toxin production** by strep & staph
- Coverage: **Anaerobes**, gram+ (including CA-MRSA)
- Drug of choice for **necrotizing fasciitis & toxic shock syndrome**
- Side effects: GI upset, increased risk of **C. difficile**

### TMP/SMX (Bactrim)
- MOA: Inhibit **folate synthesis** (SMX blocks dihydrofolate synthase; TMP blocks dihydrofolate reductase)
- Bactericidal in combination
- Clinical use: **first-line UTI**, skin/soft tissue, **PJP prophylaxis** (HIV), toxoplasmosis
- Side effects: **pancytopenia** (low folate), **teratogen** (NTDs), sulfa allergy, hemolytic anemia in G6PD deficiency, photosensitivity

### Metronidazole
- MOA: Free-radical DNA damage → bactericidal, concentration-dependent
- Coverage: **Anaerobes only** (GI/GU protozoa, C. diff with oral vanc, H. pylori, BV)
- Mnemonic: "Metro = underground where there's no oxygen"
- Side effects: metallic taste, neuropathy; **cannot mix with alcohol** (disulfiram-like reaction)

### PK/PD Summary

| Dosing Type | Examples | Strategy |
|-------------|----------|----------|
| **Time-dependent** (T>MIC) | Beta-lactams, Linezolid, Clindamycin | Low doses frequently |
| **Concentration-dependent** (Cmax>MIC) | Fluoroquinolones, Aminoglycosides, Daptomycin, Metronidazole | One big daily dose |
| **AUC/MIC** ("greedy") | Macrolides, Tetracyclines, Vancomycin | Balance dose + frequency |

## 4. Antivirals

### Antimetabolites (Guanosine Analogues)

| Drug | Activated By | Use | Key Side Effect |
|------|-------------|-----|-----------------|
| **Acyclovir** (prodrug: valacyclovir) | Viral **thymidine kinase** (HSV/VZV) | HSV, VZV | **Crystalline nephropathy** (hydrate!) |
| **Ganciclovir** (prodrug: valganciclovir) | **CMV UL-97 kinase** | CMV | **Pancytopenia** |
| **Foscarnet, Cidofovir** | Not virus-dependent (direct DNA/RNA pol inhibition) | Acyclovir/ganciclovir-**resistant** viruses | **ATN (nephrotoxic)**, hypocalcemia |

### HIV Antiretrovirals

| Class | Naming | MOA | Side Effects |
|-------|--------|-----|-------------|
| **NRTIs** | Various (-ine, -vudine) | Nucleoside analogues → **chain termination** during reverse transcription | **Mitochondrial toxicity** (lactic acidosis); tenofovir → nephrotoxic; emtricitabine → hyperpigmentation; abacavir → HLA-B*5701 |
| **NNRTIs** | "-vir-" in name | Block **outside active site** of reverse transcriptase | Lactic acidosis; **efavirenz → vivid dreams** ("effervescent dreams"); hepatotoxicity; teratogen |
| **Integrase inhibitors** | "-gravir" | Block **integration** of viral DNA into host genome | **Rhabdomyolysis/CK elevation**; well tolerated (current gold standard) |
| **Protease inhibitors** | "-navir" | Prevent **cleavage** of viral proteins → immature nonfunctional virions | Metabolic abnormalities (hyperglycemia, lipodystrophy); **ritonavir = CYP450 inhibitor**; indinavir → crystal nephropathy |

- **HAART** = 2 NRTIs + 1 other agent (NNRTI, integrase inhibitor, or protease inhibitor)
- **PrEP** = 2 NRTIs (e.g., Truvada = emtricitabine/tenofovir)
- **PEP** = 2 NRTIs + integrase inhibitor

### COVID-19 Antivirals
- **Paxlovid** (nirmatrelvir/ritonavir): nirmatrelvir blocks SARS-CoV-2 protease; ritonavir = CYP inhibition to boost levels → **many drug-drug interactions**
- **Molnupiravir**: ribonucleoside analog for pan-RNA viruses; fewer DDIs

### Influenza Antivirals
- **Neuraminidase inhibitors** (-amivir): **Oseltamivir (Tamiflu)**, zanamivir → block viral release from infected cells; must use **within 48h** of symptom onset
- **Amantadine** (adamantane): blocks M2-mediated uncoating; Influenza A only; broadly resistant so mainly used off-label for PD/pain now
- **Baloxavir**: endonuclease inhibitor → prevents "cap snatching"; for immunosuppressed patients

## 5. Antifungals

### Sterol Synthesis Inhibitors

**Squalene epoxidase inhibitors (-afine): Terbinafine, Butenafine**
- Inhibit squalene epoxidase → squalene buildup → membrane permeabilization → fungicidal
- Uses: **dermatophyte infections** (tinea, onychomycosis) — accumulates in skin/nail/fat
- Side effects: hepatotoxicity, CYP2D6 inhibitor

**Azoles (-azole): Ketoconazole, Fluconazole, Voriconazole, etc.**
- Inhibit **14α-sterol demethylase** (fungal CYP450) → block ergosterol synthesis → fungistatic
- Major theme: **drug-drug interactions** (inhibit human CYP450 too)
- **TRIazoles** (itraconazole, fluconazole, voriconazole) → **QT prolongation** (like TRIcyclics)
- Triazoles have **better tissue penetration** than imidazoles

| Azole | Key Use | Key Features |
|-------|---------|-------------|
| **Ketoconazole** (imidazole) | Cushing's (steroid synthesis inhibition) | Needs gastric pH; poor CSF penetration |
| Clotrimazole/miconazole (topical) | Vaginal candidiasis | Topical only |
| Itraconazole (triazole) | Dimorphic fungi | Needs gastric pH; CYP3A4 inhibitor |
| **Fluconazole** (triazole) | Candida, Cryptococcus | **Good CSF/sputum/urine penetration**; fewer DDIs |
| **Voriconazole** (triazole) | **Aspergillosis**; bone/CSF/ENT | Color vision changes; nephrotoxic in IV form |

### Cell Wall Inhibitors (Echinocandins)
- **Caspofungin**: Inhibit **β-(1,3)-D-glucan** synthesis → cell wall disruption → lysis/death
- Uses: **Invasive candidiasis** and aspergillosis (2nd line)
- Side effects: **Very few!** (no Cyp involvement, no human cell wall analogue)
- IV only; poor CSF penetration

### Cell Membrane Inhibitors (Polyenes)
- **Amphotericin B, Nystatin**: Bind **ergosterol** → form pores → cell lysis
- **Amphotericin B**: IV for severe systemic infections → **"amphoTERRIBLE"** side effects: nephrotoxic, cytokine storm, thrombophlebitis, anemia, arrhythmia
- **Nystatin**: Topical only for surface Candida → well tolerated (not systemically absorbed)

### Nucleic Acid Synthesis Inhibitors
- **Flucytosine**: Converted to **5-FU** by fungal cytosine deaminase → inhibits DNA/RNA synthesis
- Excellent **CNS penetration** → **cryptococcal meningitis** (used with amphotericin B)
- Side effects: bone marrow suppression, GI effects, hepatotoxicity (chemo-like)

## 6. Antiparasitics

### Antimalarials

| Drug | MOA | Stage | Key Side Effects |
|------|-----|-------|-----------------|
| **Quinolines** (quinine, chloroquine, mefloquine) | Inhibit ferriprotoporphyrin IX polymerization → toxic heme buildup | **Blood only** | Cinchonism, QT prolongation; mefloquine → vivid dreams/neuropsych |
| **Malarone** (atovaquone/proguanil) | Atovaquone: inhibit malarial ETC; Proguanil: folate synthesis inhibitor | **Blood + hepatic** | — |
| **Primaquine** | Inhibit ETC → ROS generation → kills gametocytes + hepatic stage | **Hepatic** (P. vivax/ovale) | Do NOT give in **G6PD deficiency** |
| **Artemisinin** (artesunate IV, artemether PO) | Reacts with Fe2+ → free radicals → protein/nucleic acid inhibition | Multi-stage | Short half-life → must combo |

- Treatment: chloroquine if sensitive; otherwise **artemisinin-based combination therapy (ACT)**
- Prophylaxis: most commonly malarone & mefloquine; add primaquine if P. vivax area

### Other Antiparasitics
- **Ivermectin**: ↑GABA + activates **glutamate-gated chloride channels (GluCl)** → paralysis/death of parasites/worms
- **Praziquantel**: Increase **cell permeability to Ca2+** → spastic paralysis/death of worms
- **Albendazole/mebendazole**: Bind **beta-tubulin** → inhibit microtubule polymerization → starvation; used in **neurocysticercosis**
- **Permethrin**: Insecticide → **disruption of sodium transport** → depolarization of arthropods
- **Malathion**: **Organophosphate** → irreversible AChE inhibition → paralysis of arthropods

## 7. Transplant Immunosuppression

### Induction Therapy (immediate post-op, prevent acute rejection)
- **Anti-thymocyte globulin (ATG)**: mix of anti-T cell antibodies → rapid T cell killing; highly immunogenic
- **Anti-IL2Rα mAbs (basiliximab, daclizumab)**: specific targeting of activated T cells (IL2Rα+) → better side effect profile

### Maintenance Therapy (triple-drug regimen)

| Class | Drugs | MOA | Key Side Effects |
|-------|-------|-----|-----------------|
| **Corticosteroids** | Prednisone, methylprednisolone | Anti-inflammatory (transactivation, transrepression) | Cushing syndrome, infection, osteoporosis, osteonecrosis, weight gain, adrenal insufficiency |
| **Calcineurin inhibitors** | **Tacrolimus** (FK506), **Cyclosporine** | Calcineurin inhibition → ↓NFAT → ↓IL-2 → ↓T cell activation | **Nephrotoxicity**, HTN |
| **mTOR inhibitors** | **Sirolimus** | ↓mTOR → ↓T cell proliferation | **No nephrotoxicity** (unlike calcineurin inhibitors); protects against graft coronary artery disease; pancytopenia (panSIRtopenia) |
| **Antimetabolites** | **Mycophenolate mofetil**, **Azathioprine** | Inhibit DNA/purine synthesis | Mycophenolate: teratogen, diarrhea; Azathioprine: pancytopenia (check TPMT mutation) |

## 8. Dermatologic Pharmacology & Treatment of Symptoms

### Drug Absorption Into Skin Increases With:
- **Drug factors**: ↓molecular size, ↑lipophilicity, ↑partition coefficient, ↑concentration, ↑solubility, vehicle optimization, occlusion
- **Skin factors**: ↓stratum corneum thickness, ↓age (infants have weaker barrier), ↓intactness, presence of skin folds

### Topical Agents

| Category | Drugs | Key Facts |
|----------|-------|-----------|
| Topical steroids | Hydrocortisone | Anti-inflammatory; major AE: **skin atrophy** |
| Calcineurin inhibitors | Tacrolimus (topical) | Anti-inflammatory; AE: burning/stinging |
| Imiquimod | — | **PRO-inflammatory** (TLR7 agonist → ↑NFkB → cytokines); for AKs, basal cell, warts |
| Topical antimicrobials | Neosporin, mupirocin, clindamycin, metronidazole | Note: topical OTC abx (Neosporin) → **allergic contact dermatitis** risk |
| Other | Minoxidil (hair loss), Tretinoin (acne/wrinkles, teratogen), Calcipotriene (psoriasis), 5-FU (AK/BCC), **JAK inhibitors (ruxolitinib)** for alopecia areata | — |

### Rheumatologic Pharmacology Summary
- Step 1: Corticosteroids if **major organ involvement** or refractory
- Step 2: Choose a steroid-sparing immunomodulatory agent (disease-specific)
- Key toxicities: most agents → serious infection risk; methotrexate/mycophenolate/cyclophosphamide → **teratogen**; azathioprine → pancytopenia; cyclophosphamide → hemorrhagic cystitis (treat with mesna); rituximab → hypogammaglobulinemia; HCQ → **retinal damage**; JAK inhibitors → VZV reactivation; TNF inhibitors → **TB reactivation**

### Treatment of Symptoms (N/V, Constipation, Diarrhea, Allergy)

**Antiemetics:**

| Class | Drug | Key Facts |
|-------|------|-----------|
| 5-HT3 antagonist | **Ondansetron** | General/chemo/post-op N/V; very safe but **QT prolongation** |
| NK1 antagonist | **Aprepitant** | Chemo N/V prophylaxis; CYP3A4 metabolism |
| D2 antagonist | **Metoclopramide** | Prokinetic effect (helps GERD); EPS risk |
| Corticosteroid | Dexamethasone | N/V prophylaxis |
| Cannabinoid | **Dronabinol** | Chemo N/V, anorexia in AIDS |
| BDZ | Lorazepam | **Anticipatory** N/V |

**Laxatives:** Bulk forming (psyllium), Stool softeners (docusate), Osmotic (polyethylene glycol, lactulose), Stimulant (senna, bisacodyl), **Loperamide** (peripheral mu opioid agonist for chemo-induced diarrhea)

**Allergy/Cold/Fever:**
- **1st gen antihistamines** (diphenhydramine): H1 inverse agonist + mAChR antagonist → **BBB penetrant** → sedation/anticholinergic effects; motion sickness
- **2nd gen antihistamines** (loratadine, fexofenadine, cetirizine): H1 inverse agonist → **NOT BBB penetrant** → allergic symptoms without sedation
- **Oral decongestants** (pseudoephedrine): α1-agonist → vasoconstriction; **HTN risk**; abuse as stimulant common
- **Antipyretics**: Acetaminophen, NSAIDs, Glucocorticoids → inhibit PGE2 synthesis → ↓hypothalamic fever
