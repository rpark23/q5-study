## 1. Approach to a Patient with Suspected Neuro-Infection

### The 3-Step Approach
1. **Formulate key symptoms + signs** and establish a time course
2. **Localize the lesion** -- which CNS compartment is involved? (skin/subcutaneous, epidural, meninges/subarachnoid, subdural, intraparenchymal, ventricles, base of brain, posterior fossa)
3. **Etiological diagnosis** using the mnemonic **I MIND THE VIP**

### Time Course and Typical Etiology

| Decline Pattern | Time Course | Typical Etiology |
|---|---|---|
| Acute | Seconds-minutes | Strokes, hemorrhage, mass effect, seizure |
| Subacute | Days-weeks | Bacterial, viral |
| Progressive/chronic | Months-years | Fungal, mycobacterial, spirochetal, parasitic |
| Waxing and waning | Days-weeks | Demyelinating |
| Episodic | Minutes-hours | TIA, seizure, migraine |

### Classic Presentation
- **Triad of fever + headache + altered mental status (AMS)** raises suspicion for CNS infection
- May also present as a mass lesion with focal neurologic signs, or as meningitis/encephalitis with generalized symptoms (headache, fever, nuchal rigidity, seizures, encephalopathy/coma)
- Fever may be absent in neonates, elderly, immunocompromised patients, or with abscess

### Meningeal Signs (Meningismus)
Both signs are **highly specific but not sensitive** for meningeal irritation -- their absence does not exclude CNS infection.
- **Kernig's sign:** Flex hip 90 degrees; extending the knee causes pain
- **Brudzinski's sign:** Flex the neck; hips and knees reflexively bend

### Empiric Treatment (Neurologic Emergency)
Do not delay treatment pending diagnostic workup. CSF cultures do not become sterile until hours after antibiotic administration.

**Empiric Rx for meningoencephalitis (know this red box):**
> **Vancomycin + 3rd generation cephalosporin (e.g., ceftriaxone) + acyclovir**

- Add **ampicillin** if immunocompromised or >50 years old (covers *Listeria monocytogenes*)
- Consider **IV dexamethasone** with/before antibiotics to reduce mortality (adults, *S. pneumoniae*) and hearing loss (children, *H. influenzae*)
- Consider neurosurgical intervention if needed (I&D, lesionectomy, skull decompression, shunt)

## 2. Risk Factors for Neuro-Infections

### Host Factors
- **Immunocompromised state** (congenital, acquired, or iatrogenic): alters the differential significantly
  - HIV/AIDS CD4+ <200: cryptococcus, PML (JC virus)
  - HIV/AIDS CD4+ <50: CMV
- **Head trauma/surgery/indwelling shunts:** risk for hospital-acquired organisms (*Staphylococcus*, *Pseudomonas*)
- **Congenital heart disease, cardiac valve disease, IVDU:** predisposes to endocarditis, which can lead to meningitis, intracranial abscesses, strokes, mycotic aneurysms
- **Wound botulism:** skin-popping or subcutaneous heroin injection

### Exposure History
- Geography (residence, travel, country of origin)
- Sick contacts, sexual history
- Vaccination status
- Animal exposures, foods, outdoor activities

### Geographic Clues
- **Coccidioidomycosis ("Valley Fever"):** endemic in Central Valley of CA and southwestern US
- **Neurocysticercosis:** common in immigrants from Central/South America, India, Asia, Caribbean, Africa

### Systemic Signs That Suggest Specific Organisms
- Petechial/purpuric rash: meningococcal meningitis
- Dermatomal vesicular rash: zoster (VZV)
- Target-shaped rash: Lyme disease
- Pulmonary involvement: cocci, histoplasmosis, TB
- Genital lesions: syphilis, HSV-2
- Orchitis: mumps, flavivirus

## 3. Bacterial, Viral, Parasitic, Fungal Infections, and Prion Disease on the Neuraxis

### Bacterial Infections

**Common pathogens by age/host (for reference; know the empiric regimen):**
- Neonates: *S. agalactiae* (Group B strep), *E. coli*, *L. monocytogenes*
- Children: *S. pneumoniae*, *N. meningitidis*, *H. influenzae* (Type B)
- Young adults: *S. pneumoniae*, *N. meningitidis*
- Adults >50 and immunocompromised: add *L. monocytogenes*
- Head trauma/surgery/shunt: *Staphylococcus*, *S. pneumoniae*, *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*

**Imaging findings:**
- CT: epidural abscess (above dura), intracranial abscess (ring-enhancing pus-filled pocket in parenchyma)
- MRI with contrast: meningitis (leptomeningeal enhancement), encephalitis (T2/FLAIR hyperintensity in parenchyma)

**Gross pathology:**
- Brain abscess: tissue necrosis and encapsulation (the brain "walls off" the infection); usually blood-borne (from endocarditis, pulmonary disease, IVDU, ENT spread, dental)
- Meningitis: white purulent exudate filling the subarachnoid space

**Histology:**
- **Bacterial infection = neutrophil (PMN)-predominant inflammatory infiltrate**
- Gram stain of CSF can identify the organism (gram-positive diplococci = *S. pna*; gram-negative diplococci = *N. meningitidis*; gram-positive rod = *Listeria*)

**Sequelae of untreated/undertreated bacterial meningitis:**
- Seizures (cortical irritation)
- Cerebral edema/herniation
- Obstructive hydrocephalus (may require VP shunt)
- Secondary strokes
- Cranial nerve damage (e.g., CN VIII causing deafness)

#### Botulism (Bacterial PNS/NMJ Infection)
- **Pathogen:** *Clostridium botulinum* (anaerobic, gram positive); exotoxin inhibits acetylcholine (Ach) release at the neuromuscular junction (NMJ)
- **Presentation:** "Descending flaccid paralysis -- pure motor" including bulbar muscles (ptosis, ophthalmoparesis, dysarthria, dysphagia), autonomic dysfunction, respiratory weakness (up to 70% require intubation); sensation is spared; reflexes normal or reduced
- **Sources:**
  - Infants: ingestion of spores in honey that multiply in the gut ("floppy baby")
  - Adults: ingestion of toxin in improperly home-canned food, or wound contamination (subcutaneous heroin injection)
- **Dx:** Clinical; toxin detected in stool, serum, or wound
- **Rx:** Supportive care (paralysis lasts 2-8 weeks); IVIg in infants; antitoxin in adults
- **Key contrast:** Botulism = descending pure motor paralysis; Guillain-Barré Syndrome (GBS) = ascending sensorimotor syndrome with loss of reflexes

### Viral Infections

**Key concept: viral infection = lymphocyte-predominant inflammatory infiltrate** (vs. bacterial = neutrophil-predominant)

#### HSV-1 Encephalitis (Case Example)
- **Most common identified cause of fatal encephalitis** (70% fatality untreated; 20-30% with treatment)
- **Presentation:** Fever, headache, AMS, seizures (especially focal onset with olfactory hallucinations and automatisms due to temporal lobe involvement), nuchal rigidity
- **MRI:** T2/FLAIR hyperintensity in bilateral inferior/medial temporal lobes, insular, frontal, orbitofrontal regions; often hemorrhagic
- **CSF:** Lymphocytic pleocytosis, elevated protein, normal glucose, pink/hemorrhagic CSF (RBCs present in tube 1 and tube 4 = not traumatic tap)
- **Dx:** CSF HSV-1 PCR (gold standard: 98% sensitive, 99% specific)
- **Rx:** Acyclovir x 21 days (inhibits viral DNA polymerase by chain termination)
- **Prognosis:** Permanent sequelae common (memory problems, language deficits, seizures, behavioral changes)

**Gross pathology of HSV-1:** Acute hemorrhagic necrosis of bilateral temporal lobes and inferior frontal lobes

**Histology:** Lymphocytic inflammation; **Cowdry type A intranuclear inclusion bodies** in neurons are characteristic

### Parasitic Infections

**Key CSF finding: CSF eosinophilia suggests parasitic > fungal infection**

#### Neurocysticercosis (NCC)
- **Pathogen:** *Taenia solium* (tapeworm)
- **Most common worldwide cause of epilepsy**; most common preventable cause of acquired epilepsy in adults
- **Transmission:** Fecal-oral route with ingestion of *T. solium* eggs in food/water contaminated by human feces -- NOT from eating undercooked pork (a common misconception)
- **Presentation:** Seizures (most common), focal deficits, increased ICP, hydrocephalus
- **Imaging:** Multiple calcified lesions (bright on CT) -- calcifications = dead NCC; alive NCC lesions can also be seen
- **Dx:** Neuroimaging alone is usually sufficient; calcified lesions = dead parasites
- **Rx:** If calcified (dead), treat seizures only; active lesions: steroids + anti-parasitic agents (praziquantel or albendazole)

#### Toxoplasmosis
- **Opportunistic infection** in immunocompromised: HIV/AIDS CD4+ <100 cells/mm3 (reactivation of latent infection)
- **Congenital:** Triad of chorioretinitis, hydrocephalus, intracranial calcifications; source = cat feces (pregnant women should not change cat litter)
- **MRI:** Ring-enhancing lesions with predilection for basal ganglia
- **Dx:** Seropositive toxoplasma IgG; CSF PCR
- **Rx:** Sulfadiazine + pyrimethamine

### Fungal Infections

**Key features:** Chronic/smoldering time course; often opportunistic; hematogenous spread from primary pulmonary infection; CSF may show low glucose and eosinophilia

#### Coccidioidomycosis ("Valley Fever") (Case Example)
- **Pathogen:** *Coccidioides* spp.; endemic in southwestern US and Central Valley of CA
- **Transmission:** Inhalation of fungal spores from disturbed soil (construction, farming, earthquakes, dry/windy conditions); cases have tripled in CA from 2014-2019 -- climate change is a contributing factor
- **Risk factors:** outdoor work, pregnancy, >60 years old, DM, Black race, Filipino descent, immunocompromised
- **CNS presentation:** Chronic progressive headache (75%), cognitive slowing, cranial nerve palsies (CN VI, III, IV), signs of increased ICP, communicating hydrocephalus
- **CSF:** Elevated opening pressure, high WBC (can be neutrophilic), low glucose, high protein; CSF coccidioidal antigen/antibody positive
- **MRI:** Basilar meningitis (enhancement at skull base/posterior fossa surfaces), dilated ventricles, effaced sulci
- **Rx:** Lifelong fluconazole or other azole antifungals; treat elevated ICP; may require VP shunt for hydrocephalus

**Gross pathology:** White granulomas visible on cortical surface

**Histology:** Thick-walled spherules containing endospores -- diagnostic on microscopy
- **Fungal infections = granulomatous inflammation (histiocytes)**

**Other fungal pathogens (for reference):**
- **Cryptococcus:** Common in AIDS patients; does not provoke inflammatory response (capsule is non-immunogenic); detected by cryptococcal antigen in CSF; India ink stain
- **Aspergillus:** Common in DM and immunocompromised; invades blood vessels causing hemorrhagic/necrotic lesions; often fatal
- **Histoplasmosis:** Mississippi and Ohio River Valleys; causes subacute/chronic meningitis and hydrocephalus

### Prion Disease

- **Prion** = **pro**teinaceous **in**fectious particle
- Mechanism: Normal alpha-helical protein (PrP^C) is converted into a misfolded, resistant beta-pleated isoform (PrP^SC), which then acts as a template to convert more normal protein exponentially
- **Presentation:** Rapidly progressive dementia (within 6 months), psychiatric symptoms first, then cerebellar ataxia, extrapyramidal signs (dystonia, myoclonic jerks), cortical signs (aphasia, apraxia, frontal release signs), then death
- **Acquired forms (rare, <1%):**
  - Kuru: funerary cannibalism in Papua New Guinea (last case 2010)
  - Variant CJD (vCJD): bovine spongiform encephalopathy ("mad cow disease"); food contaminated by infected cattle (last case 2012)
  - Iatrogenic: CNS tissue, blood transfusion, corneal transplants, dura mater grafts
- **Sporadic CJD:** Most common form (80-95%); no known exposure
- **Genetic forms:** 10-15%; mutations in PRNP gene
- **Dx:** History, MRI brain, CSF biomarkers (mildly elevated protein <100 mg/dL), EEG
- **No treatment; universally fatal**

**Histology (spongiform changes):**
- Nerve cell loss
- Gliosis (astrocyte proliferation)
- **Vacuolation (spongiform):** intracytoplasmic empty spaces within the neuropil -- characteristic "swiss cheese" appearance
- PrP extracellular amyloid plaques (more prominent in vCJD and familial forms)
- No inflammatory infiltrate

## 4. Interpreting Lumbar Puncture (LP) Results

**LP is performed after neuroimaging.** Avoid LP if risk for herniation, spinal epidural abscess, or coagulopathy (INR >1.4, platelets <50K).

| LP Results | Opening Pressure (cm H2O) | Color | Cells | Protein | Glucose | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Normal** | 6-20 | Clear | <5 WBC (monos), 0 RBC | <50 (15-45) | 1/2-2/3 serum glucose | No PMNs |
| **Traumatic tap** | Normal | Pink/yellow | RBC/WBC like blood; clears tube 1 to 4 | Slightly up | Normal | 1 WBC per 750 RBC |
| **SAH** | Increased | Pink/yellow; xanthochromia | RBC/WBC; does NOT clear tube 1 to 4 | 50-800 | Normal | Xanthochromia |
| **Acute bacterial** | Increased (often >30) | Turbid | WBC >100, mostly PMNs | 100-1000 | <20% of serum | Do gram stain |
| **Viral meningoencephalitis** | Normal | Clear, pink if hemorrhagic | 10-350 WBCs, mostly monos; if high RBCs not traumatic -- think HSV-1 | 40-100 | Normal | PMNs can be seen early |
| **Fungal** | Increased | PMNs early, then monos | 30-300 WBC | 100-700 | Low (<30% of serum) | India ink for cryptococcus |
| **TB** | Increased | Pearly, clumps | 50-500 WBC | 60-700 | Low (20-40% of serum) | AFB culture and stain |

**Key pattern recognition:**
- Abscess + positive gram stain + neutrophils + low CSF glucose → bacterial
- Lymphocytic pleocytosis → viral; if hemorrhagic, consider HSV-1
- CSF eosinophilia → parasitic > fungal
- Granulomas/histiocytes → fungal (or mycobacterial)

## 5. Tissue Pathology Patterns by Infection Type

| Infection Type | Inflammatory Pattern | Key Histologic Finding |
|---|---|---|
| **Bacterial** | Neutrophil (PMN)-predominant | Abscesses; gram stain positive |
| **Viral** | Lymphocyte-predominant | Intranuclear/cytoplasmic inclusion bodies (e.g., Cowdry type A in HSV-1) |
| **Fungal** | Granulomatous (histiocytes) | Granulomas; organism-specific morphology (e.g., spherules with endospores in cocci) |
| **Mycobacterial (TB)** | Caseating granulomatous | Central pink necrosis surrounded by purple histiocytes; acid-fast bacilli stain |
| **Parasitic** | Eosinophilic | Organism visible (e.g., calcified cysts in NCC; tachyzoites in toxoplasmosis) |
| **Prion** | No inflammatory infiltrate | Spongiform (vacuolated) cortex; nerve cell loss; gliosis |

## 6. Applying the 3-Step Approach: Clinical Cases

### Case 1: HSV-1 Encephalitis (52 yo M, fever/HA/AMS x 2 days)
1. **Sx + signs:** Fever, HA, AMS, focal seizures (olfactory hallucinations + automatisms), nuchal rigidity -- subacute onset
2. **Localize:** CNS (AMS, seizures, hyperreflexia = UMN); cortex (focal seizures); meninges (nuchal rigidity)
3. **Dx:** CNS infection -- bacterial or viral meningoencephalitis; confirmed HSV-1 by PCR

### Case 2: Neurocysticercosis (30 yo F, status epilepticus)
1. **Sx + signs:** Focal aware seizure evolving to focal to bilateral tonic-clonic seizure, then status epilepticus (convulsive seizure ≥5 minutes); immigration from Honduras; discontinuation of valproic acid
2. **Localize:** CNS cortex (focal onset seizures from left hemisphere); status epilepticus is a medical emergency (mortality 20-30%)
3. **Dx:** Multiple calcified lesions on head CT = dead NCC; treat seizures only

### Case 3: Coccidioidomycosis (58 yo M, chronic HA x 9 months)
1. **Sx + signs:** Chronic progressive headache, scotomata, diplopia, cognitive slowing, papilledema, CN VI/III/IV palsies, hyperreflexia
2. **Localize:** CNS, diffuse; increased ICP; brainstem CN involvement; construction worker in Los Banos, CA (endemic area)
3. **Dx:** CNS cocci meningitis confirmed by CSF antigen/antibody; MRI shows basilar meningitis and communicating hydrocephalus

### Case 4: Prion Disease/Kuru (27 yo M, psychiatric then neurologic symptoms)
1. **Sx + signs:** Hallucinations (6 months ago), then aphasia, apraxia, alien-limb, cerebellar ataxia, dystonia, myoclonic jerks, frontal release signs -- chronic, progressive
2. **Localize:** CNS, multifocal: cortex (frontal, temporal, parietal) + cerebellum + basal ganglia + UMN
3. **Dx:** Rapidly progressive dementia -- prion disease (kuru from funerary cannibalism in Papua New Guinea)

### Case 5: Botulism (25 yo M, descending weakness)
1. **Sx + signs:** Pure motor descending weakness -- bilateral ptosis, ophthalmoparesis, facial weakness, dysarthria, dysphagia, difficulty breathing; intact sensation; cans food at home
2. **Localize:** PNS (intact mental status, no CNS signs); NMJ (predilection for extraocular and bulbar muscles; pure motor; descending)
3. **Dx:** Botulism -- toxin from improperly home-canned food
