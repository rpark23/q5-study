# Anti-Seizure Medications — Pre-Quiz

### Q1. What is the minimum duration of continuous seizure activity that defines status epilepticus?

- A) 1 minute
- B) 3 minutes
- C) 5 minutes
- D) 10 minutes

### Q2. What is the first-line medication and dose for the initial treatment of status epilepticus?

- A) Phenytoin 15 mg/kg IV
- B) Lorazepam 0.1 mg/kg IV
- C) Valproic acid 20 mg/kg IV
- D) Phenobarbital 20 mg/kg IV

### Q3. What are the two main mechanisms by which anti-seizure medications suppress seizures?

- A) Block dopamine receptors and increase serotonin
- B) Decrease glutamate excitation and increase GABA inhibition
- C) Increase acetylcholine release and block norepinephrine
- D) Block histamine receptors and increase endorphin release

### Q4. Which of the following is a narrow spectrum ASM that should NOT be used for generalized onset seizures?

- A) Valproic acid
- B) Lamotrigine
- C) Levetiracetam
- D) Carbamazepine

### Q5. Lorazepam terminates status epilepticus by which mechanism?

- A) Blocking sodium channels
- B) Blocking T-type calcium channels
- C) Enhancing GABA inhibition at the GABAA receptor
- D) Binding to SV2A protein on synaptic vesicles

### Q6. Which pharmacokinetic property of phenytoin makes its dosing particularly challenging?

- A) First-order elimination kinetics
- B) Zero-order (non-linear) kinetics
- C) Exclusive renal elimination
- D) Extremely short half-life of 2 hours

### Q7. Which genetic marker should be tested before prescribing carbamazepine, particularly in patients of Asian descent, due to the risk of Stevens-Johnson syndrome?

- A) HLA-B*5701
- B) CYP2D6
- C) HLA-B*1502
- D) BRCA1

### Q8. Ethosuximide is classified as the narrowest spectrum ASM because it is effective only for which seizure type?

- A) Focal seizures with impaired awareness
- B) Generalized tonic-clonic seizures
- C) Generalized absence seizures
- D) Myoclonic seizures

### Q9. Levetiracetam (Keppra) has a unique mechanism of action. What is its primary target?

- A) GABAA receptor
- B) Sodium channels
- C) SV2A protein on presynaptic vesicles
- D) T-type calcium channels

### Q10. Which ASM is highly teratogenic and associated with neural tube defects and a 10 IQ point neurodevelopmental loss in exposed children?

- A) Lamotrigine
- B) Levetiracetam
- C) Gabapentin
- D) Valproic acid

---

## Answer Key

### Q1. Answer: C
- **A) 1 minute.** Incorrect. One minute of seizure activity is concerning but does not meet the clinical definition of status epilepticus. Brief seizures that self-terminate within a few minutes do not require emergency SE protocols.
- **B) 3 minutes.** Incorrect. While seizures lasting beyond 2-3 minutes should prompt concern and a call to 911 with benzodiazepine administration, the formal threshold for status epilepticus is 5 minutes of continuous seizure activity.
- **C) 5 minutes.** Correct. Status epilepticus is defined as continuous seizure activity lasting 5 minutes or longer. It is a medical and neurological emergency because prolonged seizure activity (especially beyond 30 minutes) can cause neuronal death, neuronal injury, and irreversible brain damage.
- **D) 10 minutes.** Incorrect. Waiting 10 minutes to classify a seizure as status epilepticus would dangerously delay treatment. The principle of "time is brain" applies to SE, and earlier treatment (within 30 minutes) achieves an 80% response rate compared to only 40% after 2 hours.

### Q2. Answer: B
- **A) Phenytoin 15 mg/kg IV.** Incorrect. IV fosphenytoin (not phenytoin, to avoid purple glove syndrome) is a second-line agent used when lorazepam fails to terminate status epilepticus. It is not the initial treatment of choice.
- **B) Lorazepam 0.1 mg/kg IV.** Correct. Lorazepam 0.1 mg/kg IV, given in 2 mg pushes, is the first-line treatment for status epilepticus. The VA Cooperative Study demonstrated that lorazepam is the most effective agent for terminating SE, with a 65% success rate and a longer duration of anti-seizure action than other benzodiazepines.
- **C) Valproic acid 20 mg/kg IV.** Incorrect. IV valproic acid is an option if initial therapy with lorazepam fails, but it is not the first-line treatment. It may be given as a second-line agent alongside fosphenytoin.
- **D) Phenobarbital 20 mg/kg IV.** Incorrect. IV phenobarbital can be used in refractory SE but is not the recommended initial treatment. Lorazepam has a superior success rate and a more favorable safety profile for initial SE management.

### Q3. Answer: B
- **A) Block dopamine receptors and increase serotonin.** Incorrect. These are mechanisms associated with antipsychotics and antidepressants, respectively, not anti-seizure medications. ASMs target the balance between excitatory and inhibitory neurotransmission.
- **B) Decrease glutamate excitation and increase GABA inhibition.** Correct. ASMs suppress seizures by raising the seizure threshold through two main pathways: decreasing excitatory neurotransmission (via sodium channel blockade, calcium channel blockade, or glutamate receptor antagonism) and enhancing inhibitory neurotransmission (via GABAA receptor modulation or increasing GABA levels).
- **C) Increase acetylcholine release and block norepinephrine.** Incorrect. Acetylcholine and norepinephrine pathways are not the primary targets of anti-seizure medications. The core mechanisms involve the glutamate-GABA balance in the brain.
- **D) Block histamine receptors and increase endorphin release.** Incorrect. These mechanisms are associated with antihistamines and opioids, respectively. They do not represent the pharmacological basis of anti-seizure medication action.

### Q4. Answer: D
- **A) Valproic acid.** Incorrect. Valproic acid is a broad spectrum ASM that is effective for all seizure types, including both focal and generalized onset seizures. It is considered the standard broad-spectrum agent and is particularly effective for generalized seizure types.
- **B) Lamotrigine.** Incorrect. Lamotrigine is a broad spectrum ASM that works for both focal and generalized seizures. However, it may not be as effective for generalized myoclonic seizures and can potentially worsen them.
- **C) Levetiracetam.** Incorrect. Levetiracetam is a broad spectrum ASM effective for all seizure types. It is one of the more commonly used ASMs and can be started quickly with an available IV formulation.
- **D) Carbamazepine.** Correct. Carbamazepine is a narrow spectrum ASM that works for focal (partial) seizures only. Using narrow spectrum agents like carbamazepine for generalized onset seizures can actually worsen them. Other narrow spectrum ASMs include phenobarbital, phenytoin, and gabapentin.

### Q5. Answer: C
- **A) Blocking sodium channels.** Incorrect. Sodium channel blockade is the mechanism of phenytoin and carbamazepine, not lorazepam. As a benzodiazepine, lorazepam works exclusively through the GABAergic system.
- **B) Blocking T-type calcium channels.** Incorrect. T-type calcium channel blockade is the mechanism of ethosuximide, which decreases thalamocortical firing to treat absence seizures specifically. Lorazepam does not act on T-type calcium channels.
- **C) Enhancing GABA inhibition at the GABAA receptor.** Correct. Lorazepam is a benzodiazepine that works solely by modulating the GABAA receptor, thereby increasing inhibitory neurotransmission. This rapid enhancement of GABA inhibition is what makes it effective for emergency seizure termination in status epilepticus.
- **D) Binding to SV2A protein on synaptic vesicles.** Incorrect. SV2A binding is the unique mechanism of levetiracetam (Keppra), which interferes with vesicular glutamate release at the presynaptic terminal. This mechanism is distinct from the GABA-modulating action of lorazepam.

### Q6. Answer: B
- **A) First-order elimination kinetics.** Incorrect. Most drugs follow first-order kinetics, where a constant fraction of drug is eliminated per unit time, making dose adjustments predictable. Phenytoin is notable specifically because it does NOT follow first-order kinetics at therapeutic doses.
- **B) Zero-order (non-linear) kinetics.** Correct. Phenytoin exhibits zero-order (non-linear, saturation) kinetics, meaning that small dose increases can produce disproportionately large increases in serum levels. This makes dosing challenging because the relationship between dose and blood level is unpredictable, and the drug can easily reach toxic levels with minor dose adjustments.
- **C) Exclusive renal elimination.** Incorrect. Phenytoin is metabolized by the liver, not the kidneys. Gabapentin and levetiracetam are the ASMs that are primarily renally eliminated. Phenytoin is also highly protein-bound and is a CYP3A4 enzyme inducer.
- **D) Extremely short half-life of 2 hours.** Incorrect. Phenytoin has a half-life of approximately 24 hours, not 2 hours. Its dosing difficulty stems from non-linear kinetics, not from a short half-life requiring frequent dosing.

### Q7. Answer: C
- **A) HLA-B*5701.** Incorrect. HLA-B*5701 testing is associated with abacavir (an HIV medication) hypersensitivity, not carbamazepine-associated Stevens-Johnson syndrome. This is a different pharmacogenomic marker used in a different clinical context.
- **B) CYP2D6.** Incorrect. CYP2D6 is a cytochrome P450 enzyme involved in the metabolism of many drugs. While pharmacogenomic testing for CYP2D6 is used in various clinical scenarios, it is not the marker associated with carbamazepine-induced severe cutaneous reactions.
- **C) HLA-B*1502.** Correct. HLA-B*1502 is a genetic marker strongly associated with carbamazepine-induced Stevens-Johnson syndrome and toxic epidermal necrolysis (SJS/TEN). This allele is most prevalent in patients of Asian descent, and testing should be performed before initiating carbamazepine therapy in this population.
- **D) BRCA1.** Incorrect. BRCA1 is a gene associated with hereditary breast and ovarian cancer risk. It has no role in predicting drug hypersensitivity reactions and is unrelated to anti-seizure medication pharmacogenomics.

### Q8. Answer: C
- **A) Focal seizures with impaired awareness.** Incorrect. Ethosuximide is not effective for focal seizures of any type. In fact, it is an exception to the general rule that narrow spectrum medications work for focal seizures. It can worsen other seizure types if used inappropriately.
- **B) Generalized tonic-clonic seizures.** Incorrect. Ethosuximide does not treat generalized tonic-clonic seizures and should not be used for this indication. Broad spectrum ASMs like valproic acid, lamotrigine, or levetiracetam are appropriate for generalized tonic-clonic seizures.
- **C) Generalized absence seizures.** Correct. Ethosuximide is the narrowest spectrum ASM and works ONLY for generalized absence (petit mal) seizures. It blocks T-type calcium channels in the thalamus, decreasing the thalamocortical firing pattern that generates absence seizures. It is an exception because, although classified as narrow spectrum, it does not work for focal seizures.
- **D) Myoclonic seizures.** Incorrect. Ethosuximide is not effective for myoclonic seizures. Broad spectrum medications such as valproic acid or levetiracetam are typically used for myoclonic seizure types.

### Q9. Answer: C
- **A) GABAA receptor.** Incorrect. The GABAA receptor is the primary target of benzodiazepines (like lorazepam) and barbiturates (like phenobarbital). Levetiracetam has a unique mechanism that is distinct from GABA receptor modulation.
- **B) Sodium channels.** Incorrect. Sodium channel blockade is the mechanism of phenytoin, carbamazepine, and lamotrigine. Levetiracetam does not primarily act through sodium channel blockade.
- **C) SV2A protein on presynaptic vesicles.** Correct. Levetiracetam has a unique mechanism of action: it binds to the SV2A (synaptic vesicle glycoprotein 2A) protein on presynaptic vesicles containing glutamate. This binding interferes with vesicular glutamate release, thereby decreasing excitatory neurotransmission. This distinguishes it from all other ASMs.
- **D) T-type calcium channels.** Incorrect. T-type calcium channel blockade is the mechanism of ethosuximide, which specifically targets thalamocortical firing to treat absence seizures. Levetiracetam does not act on T-type calcium channels.

### Q10. Answer: D
- **A) Lamotrigine.** Incorrect. Lamotrigine is actually considered a newer broad-spectrum alternative to valproic acid with a better side effect profile. While it carries a risk of Stevens-Johnson syndrome with rapid titration, it is not associated with the severe teratogenicity of neural tube defects and IQ loss seen with valproic acid.
- **B) Levetiracetam.** Incorrect. Levetiracetam has a relatively favorable safety profile regarding teratogenicity. Its most concerning side effect is mood changes and irritability (affecting 20% of patients), not birth defects.
- **C) Gabapentin.** Incorrect. Gabapentin has a very safe pharmacological profile with no significant drug interactions and no hepatic metabolism. It is not considered highly teratogenic and is among the safer ASMs in terms of fetal risk.
- **D) Valproic acid.** Correct. Valproic acid is the most teratogenic ASM and should be avoided in patients of childbearing potential. It causes neural tube defects and is associated with a 10 IQ point neurodevelopmental loss in children exposed in utero. The overall birth defect risk with ASMs rises from a baseline of 1-2% to 4-10%, with valproic acid carrying the highest risk.
