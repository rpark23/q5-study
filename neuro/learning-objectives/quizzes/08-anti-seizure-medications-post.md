# Anti-Seizure Medications — Post-Quiz

### Q1. A 42-year-old man is brought to the emergency department after witnesses observed him having a generalized tonic-clonic seizure that has been ongoing for 8 minutes. His airway is secured and IV access is established. What is the most appropriate next step?

- A) Administer IV fosphenytoin 20 mg/kg
- B) Administer IV lorazepam 2 mg, repeated every 1-2 minutes up to 0.1 mg/kg total
- C) Intubate and begin propofol infusion
- D) Obtain a stat CT head before giving any medications

### Q2. A 55-year-old woman with status epilepticus receives lorazepam 0.1 mg/kg IV, but seizure activity continues. IV fosphenytoin is administered as a second agent, but seizures persist. According to the VA Cooperative Study findings, approximately what percentage of patients fail to respond to the initial first-line treatment for SE?

- A) 10%
- B) 20%
- C) 35%
- D) 50%

### Q3. A neurologist is explaining to a medical student how levetiracetam works differently from other anti-seizure medications. Which statement best describes levetiracetam's unique mechanism of action?

### Q4. A 14-year-old girl is diagnosed with juvenile absence epilepsy after her teacher notices frequent staring spells lasting 5-10 seconds each. Her EEG shows 3 Hz generalized spike-and-wave discharges. Which of the following medications would be MOST LIKELY to worsen her seizures?

- A) Valproic acid
- B) Lamotrigine
- C) Carbamazepine
- D) Levetiracetam

### Q5. A 28-year-old man on phenytoin 300 mg daily presents with a serum level of 12 mcg/mL (therapeutic: 10-20 mcg/mL). His neurologist increases the dose to 400 mg daily. Two weeks later, his level is 35 mcg/mL and he presents with severe ataxia and nystagmus. What pharmacokinetic property of phenytoin best explains this disproportionate increase in serum level?

- A) High protein binding causing displacement by other drugs
- B) Zero-order (non-linear) elimination kinetics
- C) Rapid first-pass hepatic metabolism

### Q6. A 65-year-old woman with new-onset focal epilepsy also has osteoporosis, mild cognitive impairment, and takes multiple medications including warfarin and a statin. Which ASM would be the most appropriate choice for her?

- A) Phenytoin
- B) Carbamazepine
- C) Phenobarbital

### Q7. A 19-year-old Filipino woman is being started on an anti-seizure medication for newly diagnosed focal epilepsy. Her neurologist orders a genetic test before prescribing a specific ASM. ### Q8. A 32-year-old woman with generalized tonic-clonic epilepsy well-controlled on valproic acid presents for preconception counseling. She and her husband plan to start trying to conceive in 6 months. Which of the following is the most appropriate management strategy?

- A) Continue valproic acid and add high-dose folic acid supplementation
- B) Switch to a less teratogenic broad-spectrum ASM like lamotrigine before conception

### Q9. A 45-year-old man is started on lamotrigine for focal epilepsy. His physician tells him the dose must be increased very slowly over approximately 2 months. What is the most likely diagnosis?

- A) Drug-induced lupus
- B) Allergic contact dermatitis
- C) Stevens-Johnson syndrome

### Q10. A 38-year-old software engineer with newly diagnosed focal epilepsy is started on topiramate 25 mg daily. Which of the following best describes this clinical picture?

- A) These are expected side effects of topiramate; it is nicknamed "Dopamax" because cognitive effects occur in approximately one-third of patients

- C) Weight loss and cognitive changes indicate the patient is non-adherent and having subclinical seizures

### Q11. A 22-year-old college student with epilepsy takes levetiracetam 1000 mg twice daily. His roommate reports that over the past month, the patient has become increasingly irritable, hostile, and had an episode of uncharacteristic aggression. The patient says he feels "angry all the time" but cannot explain why. What is the most likely explanation, and what is the approximate incidence of this side effect?

- A) Uncontrolled seizures causing post-ictal personality changes; incidence 5%
- B) Levetiracetam-associated mood and behavioral changes; incidence approximately 20%
- C) Substance abuse disorder commonly seen in epilepsy patients; incidence 10%
- D) Development of a comorbid psychiatric illness unrelated to medication; incidence 1%

### Q12. A 26-year-old woman with epilepsy controlled on carbamazepine uses combined oral contraceptive pills for birth control. She presents with an unplanned pregnancy at 8 weeks gestation. Which property of carbamazepine most likely contributed to contraceptive failure?

- A) Carbamazepine inhibits hepatic enzymes, increasing estrogen levels to toxic thresholds
- B) Carbamazepine is a CYP3A4 enzyme inducer, which decreases oral contraceptive hormone levels
- C) Carbamazepine competes with estrogen for protein binding sites, displacing the hormone
- D) Carbamazepine directly antagonizes estrogen receptors in the uterus

---

## Answer Key

### Q1. Answer: B
- **A) Administer IV fosphenytoin 20 mg/kg.** Incorrect. Fosphenytoin is a second-line agent for status epilepticus, used only after lorazepam fails to terminate the seizure. Skipping first-line therapy and going directly to fosphenytoin does not follow the established SE treatment algorithm.
- **B) Administer IV lorazepam 2 mg, repeated every 1-2 minutes up to 0.1 mg/kg total.** Correct. This patient meets criteria for status epilepticus (continuous seizure activity for 8 minutes, exceeding the 5-minute threshold). The first-line treatment is lorazepam 0.1 mg/kg IV given in 2 mg pushes. For an average 70 kg adult, this would be approximately 7 mg total. The VA Cooperative Study demonstrated lorazepam has a 65% success rate for terminating SE, and early treatment achieves an 80% response rate.
- **C) Intubate and begin propofol infusion.** Incorrect. Intubation and general anesthesia with propofol or pentobarbital are reserved for refractory status epilepticus, after both first-line (lorazepam) and second-line (fosphenytoin or valproic acid) therapies have failed. Starting with this would be overly aggressive.
- **D) Obtain a stat CT head before giving any medications.** Incorrect. In status epilepticus, "time is brain" -- delays in treatment increase the risk of irreversible brain damage. Treatment with lorazepam should begin immediately while the workup, including imaging, proceeds concurrently. Delaying treatment for imaging could be catastrophic.

### Q2. Answer: C
- **A) 10%.** Incorrect. A 10% failure rate significantly underestimates the difficulty of treating status epilepticus. The VA Cooperative Study showed that SE is much more refractory to treatment than this figure suggests.
- **B) 20%.** Incorrect. While this is closer, it still underestimates the failure rate. The study found that lorazepam, despite being the most effective single agent, still fails in a substantial proportion of patients.
- **C) 35%.** Correct. The VA Cooperative Study found that initial treatment with lorazepam fails to stop status epilepticus in approximately one-third (35%) of cases. Furthermore, when the first medication fails, the second medication is usually unsuccessful as well, emphasizing the importance of early and aggressive initial treatment. This is why delayed treatment (after 2 hours) drops the success rate to only 40%.
- **D) 50%.** Incorrect. A 50% failure rate overestimates the proportion of refractory cases. Lorazepam has a 65% success rate, making it the most effective first-line agent. However, the overall failure rate across all initial treatments was about one-third.

### Q3. Answer: C
- **A) It enhances GABAA receptor activity at the post-synaptic membrane.** Incorrect. This describes the mechanism of benzodiazepines (like lorazepam) and barbiturates (like phenobarbital). These drugs increase GABA inhibition at the post-synaptic membrane but have a different molecular target than levetiracetam.
- **B) It blocks voltage-gated sodium channels to prevent action potential propagation.** Incorrect. Sodium channel blockade is the mechanism of phenytoin, carbamazepine, and lamotrigine. These agents decrease excitatory neurotransmission by preventing the propagation of action potentials in rapidly firing neurons.
- **C) It binds to SV2A protein on presynaptic vesicles to interfere with glutamate release.** Correct. Levetiracetam has a unique mechanism among ASMs: it binds to the synaptic vesicle glycoprotein 2A (SV2A) on presynaptic vesicles containing glutamate. This binding decreases excitatory neurotransmission by interfering with vesicular glutamate release, rather than by blocking ion channels or modulating receptors directly.
- **D) It blocks T-type calcium channels in the thalamus to reduce thalamocortical firing.** Incorrect. This is the mechanism of ethosuximide, which specifically blocks T-type calcium channels to disrupt the thalamocortical rhythm that generates absence seizures. Ethosuximide is the narrowest spectrum ASM, working only for generalized absence seizures.

### Q4. Answer: C
- **A) Valproic acid.** Incorrect. Valproic acid is a broad spectrum ASM that is the most effective medication for generalized seizure types, including absence seizures. It would be an appropriate first-line choice for this patient, though its use in a 14-year-old girl requires discussion of teratogenic risks given her childbearing potential.
- **B) Lamotrigine.** Incorrect. Lamotrigine is a broad spectrum ASM that can be used for absence seizures, though it may not be as effective as valproic acid for generalized myoclonic seizures specifically. It would not be expected to worsen absence epilepsy.
- **C) Carbamazepine.** Correct. Carbamazepine is a narrow spectrum ASM that works only for focal seizures. Using narrow spectrum agents for generalized onset seizures, including absence seizures, can worsen the seizure disorder. This patient has generalized absence epilepsy, and carbamazepine, along with other narrow spectrum agents (phenytoin, gabapentin), would be contraindicated.
- **D) Levetiracetam.** Incorrect. Levetiracetam is a broad spectrum ASM effective for all seizure types. It would be a reasonable option for this patient, though its mood and irritability side effects (20% incidence) should be discussed.

### Q5. Answer: B
- **A) High protein binding causing displacement by other drugs.** Incorrect. While phenytoin is indeed highly protein-bound, and drug displacement interactions can alter its free fraction, this does not explain the disproportionate level increase from a simple dose change without adding any new medications. The core issue is the drug's non-linear metabolism.
- **B) Zero-order (non-linear) elimination kinetics.** Correct. Phenytoin exhibits zero-order (saturation) kinetics at therapeutic doses, meaning its metabolic enzymes become saturated. Once saturation occurs, small dose increases produce disproportionately large increases in serum levels. In this case, a 33% dose increase (300 to 400 mg) nearly tripled the serum level (12 to 35 mcg/mL), a hallmark of non-linear kinetics. This is why phenytoin dosing is particularly challenging and levels must be monitored closely.
- **C) Rapid first-pass hepatic metabolism.** Incorrect. First-pass metabolism would reduce drug bioavailability and lead to lower levels, not the dramatic level increase seen here. Phenytoin is metabolized by the liver, but its problematic property is non-linear kinetics, not first-pass effects.
- **D) Extensive renal tubular reabsorption.** Incorrect. Phenytoin is metabolized by the liver, not primarily eliminated by the kidneys. Renal tubular reabsorption is not a relevant pharmacokinetic concern for phenytoin. Gabapentin and levetiracetam are the ASMs with primary renal elimination.

### Q6. Answer: D
- **A) Phenytoin.** Incorrect. Phenytoin is a CYP3A4 enzyme inducer, which would interact with her warfarin and statin. It also causes osteomalacia (worsening her osteoporosis), cognitive dysfunction (worsening her cognitive impairment), and peripheral neuropathy -- all particularly problematic in elderly patients. Additionally, its zero-order kinetics make dosing difficult.
- **B) Carbamazepine.** Incorrect. Carbamazepine is a CYP3A4 enzyme inducer that would decrease warfarin and statin levels, potentially leading to therapeutic failure. As a sodium channel blocker, it can worsen ataxia and cognitive function in the elderly. It also contributes to osteoporosis.
- **C) Phenobarbital.** Incorrect. Phenobarbital is a CYP3A4 enzyme inducer with numerous drug interactions, including with warfarin and statins. It causes sedation, cognitive impairment, depression, and long-term osteoporosis, making it one of the worst choices for this elderly patient with multiple comorbidities.
- **D) Lamotrigine.** Correct. Lamotrigine is a broad spectrum ASM that is well-suited for elderly patients. It is not a CYP3A4 enzyme inducer, so it will not alter warfarin or statin levels significantly. It has a relatively favorable cognitive side effect profile compared to older ASMs. While it requires slow titration over 2 months to avoid rash, it is recommended as one of the preferred ASMs in the elderly, along with levetiracetam and gabapentin.

### Q7. Answer: B
- **A) Lamotrigine; hepatotoxicity.** Incorrect. While lamotrigine does carry a risk of Stevens-Johnson syndrome with rapid titration, HLA-B*1502 testing is specifically recommended before carbamazepine (and related drugs), not lamotrigine. Lamotrigine's rash risk is managed by slow titration over 2 months, not pharmacogenomic testing.
- **B) Carbamazepine; Stevens-Johnson syndrome/toxic epidermal necrolysis.** Correct. HLA-B*1502 is a genetic marker found predominantly in patients of Asian descent (including Filipino populations) that is strongly associated with carbamazepine-induced Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS) and toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN). These are life-threatening dermatological emergencies. Screening for this allele is recommended before initiating carbamazepine in at-risk populations.
- **C) Phenytoin; aplastic anemia.** Incorrect. While phenytoin can cause rash (including SJS), HLA-B*1502 testing is primarily associated with carbamazepine prescribing decisions. Aplastic anemia is not the primary concern with HLA-B*1502 positivity; the feared reaction is SJS/TEN.
- **D) Valproic acid; pancreatitis.** Incorrect. HLA-B*1502 is not associated with valproic acid reactions. Valproic acid's major concerns include hepatotoxicity (especially in children), teratogenicity (neural tube defects, IQ loss), and pancreatitis, but these are not predicted by HLA typing.

### Q8. Answer: B
- **A) Continue valproic acid and add high-dose folic acid supplementation.** Incorrect. While folic acid supplementation (at least 0.4 mg before pregnancy) is recommended for all women on ASMs and reduces birth defect risk by 50-70%, it does not sufficiently mitigate the severe teratogenic risk of valproic acid. Valproic acid causes neural tube defects and a 10 IQ point neurodevelopmental loss in exposed children. The appropriate step is to switch medications before conception.
- **B) Switch to a less teratogenic broad-spectrum ASM like lamotrigine before conception.** Correct. Valproic acid is the most teratogenic ASM and should be avoided in women of childbearing potential. Since this patient has generalized tonic-clonic epilepsy, she needs a broad spectrum agent, so lamotrigine or levetiracetam would be appropriate alternatives. The switch should occur before conception because teratogenic medications should be substituted ahead of pregnancy, not during it. Additionally, seizure freedom for 9 or more months before pregnancy is associated with an 84-92% likelihood of remaining seizure-free during pregnancy.
- **C) Stop all anti-seizure medications immediately since she is planning pregnancy.** Incorrect. Stopping ASMs abruptly is dangerous and can precipitate status epilepticus. ASMs should not be discontinued during pregnancy; rather, teratogenic medications should be substituted with safer alternatives before conception. Uncontrolled seizures during pregnancy also pose significant risks to both mother and fetus.
- **D) Continue valproic acid since the risk of seizures during pregnancy outweighs teratogenic risk.** Incorrect. The availability of safer broad spectrum alternatives (lamotrigine, levetiracetam) means there is no justification for continuing a highly teratogenic agent when substitution is possible. The switch should be made well before conception to ensure seizure control on the new medication.

### Q9. Answer: C
- **A) Drug-induced lupus.** Incorrect. Drug-induced lupus presents with systemic symptoms such as joint pain, serositis, and positive ANA/anti-histone antibodies, typically developing after months to years of drug exposure. It does not present with blistering skin lesions and mucosal involvement after one week of treatment.
- **B) Allergic contact dermatitis.** Incorrect. Contact dermatitis is caused by direct skin exposure to an allergen or irritant and presents with localized erythema, vesicles, and pruritus at the contact site. It does not involve oral mucosa or present as a diffuse blistering rash from an oral medication.
- **C) Stevens-Johnson syndrome.** Correct. This patient's presentation -- a diffuse, blistering rash with mucosal involvement occurring after rapid self-titration of lamotrigine -- is classic for Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS). Lamotrigine carries a 5-10% incidence of initiation rash, and rapid titration (as occurred when this patient doubled his dose) significantly increases the risk of SJS/TEN, a life-threatening dermatological emergency. This is precisely why lamotrigine requires slow titration over approximately 2 months.
- **D) Serum sickness.** Incorrect. Serum sickness presents with fever, arthralgia, lymphadenopathy, and urticaria, typically 7-14 days after exposure to a foreign protein or certain drugs. While the timing could overlap, the blistering nature of the rash and mucosal involvement point strongly toward SJS rather than serum sickness.

### Q10. Answer: A
- **A) These are expected side effects of topiramate; it is nicknamed "Dopamax" because cognitive effects occur in approximately one-third of patients.** Correct. Topiramate is well known for causing cognitive side effects -- particularly word-finding difficulties, memory problems, and mental fogginess -- in approximately one-third of patients. This earned it the nickname "Dopamax." Weight loss is also a recognized side effect (unlike most other ASMs that cause weight gain). Other notable side effects include kidney stones (1-2%) and rare glaucoma. These effects are significant enough to limit topiramate's use, especially in patients whose occupations require high cognitive function.
- **B) These symptoms suggest the patient has developed non-convulsive status epilepticus and needs urgent EEG monitoring.** Incorrect. While non-convulsive status epilepticus can present with confusion and cognitive impairment, the gradual onset over months, the accompanying weight loss (a known topiramate effect), and the temporal relationship to reaching the target dose all point to medication side effects rather than ongoing seizure activity.
- **C) Weight loss and cognitive changes indicate the patient is non-adherent and having subclinical seizures.** Incorrect. Weight loss is a well-documented side effect of topiramate (not a sign of non-adherence), and the cognitive changes described are characteristic of the drug. Non-adherence would more likely present with breakthrough seizures, not the specific pattern of word-finding difficulty and mental fogginess that typifies topiramate's cognitive effects.
- **D) These symptoms are unrelated to topiramate and suggest a new neurological diagnosis.** Incorrect. The word-finding difficulties, mental fogginess, and weight loss described in this vignette are all well-characterized side effects of topiramate. Starting a workup for a new neurological condition without first considering the medication as the cause would be premature and inappropriate.

### Q11. Answer: B
- **A) Uncontrolled seizures causing post-ictal personality changes; incidence 5%.** Incorrect. Post-ictal behavioral changes are transient and occur immediately after a seizure, not as a chronic pattern lasting a month. The sustained personality change and irritability described in this vignette are much more consistent with a medication side effect than with post-ictal phenomena.
- **B) Levetiracetam-associated mood and behavioral changes; incidence approximately 20%.** Correct. Mood changes, including irritability, hostility, and aggression, are the most troublesome side effects of levetiracetam, occurring in approximately 20% of patients. The range extends from mild irritability to severe psychosis and suicidality. Patients must be warned about these potential effects at the time of prescription. The effects are reversible upon dose reduction or discontinuation, and levetiracetam should be avoided in patients with significant psychiatric history.
- **C) Substance abuse disorder commonly seen in epilepsy patients; incidence 10%.** Incorrect. While substance use disorders can occur in patients with epilepsy, the clinical presentation -- a gradual onset of irritability and uncharacteristic aggression temporally related to starting levetiracetam -- is much more consistent with a known drug side effect than with new-onset substance abuse.
- **D) Development of a comorbid psychiatric illness unrelated to medication; incidence 1%.** Incorrect. While new psychiatric illness is always a consideration, the temporal relationship to levetiracetam initiation and the specific pattern of irritability and behavioral change match the well-documented 20% incidence of mood side effects. It would be incorrect to attribute these symptoms to an unrelated cause without first considering the medication.

### Q12. Answer: B
- **A) Carbamazepine inhibits hepatic enzymes, increasing estrogen levels to toxic thresholds.** Incorrect. This is the opposite of carbamazepine's actual effect. Carbamazepine is a CYP3A4 enzyme inducer, not an inhibitor. Valproic acid is the ASM known for being a liver enzyme inhibitor. Enzyme induction increases the metabolism of other drugs, lowering their levels.
- **B) Carbamazepine is a CYP3A4 enzyme inducer, which decreases oral contraceptive hormone levels.** Correct. Carbamazepine induces CYP3A4 hepatic enzymes, which increases the metabolism of estrogen and progesterone in oral contraceptives, reducing their serum levels and decreasing their effectiveness. This is why 50% of pregnancies in the US are unplanned, and why teratogenicity and contraceptive strategies must be discussed with all patients of childbearing age. Other CYP3A4 enzyme inducers that reduce OCP effectiveness include phenytoin, phenobarbital, and topiramate. Safe alternatives that do not affect hormonal contraceptives include levetiracetam and gabapentin, or a non-hormonal copper IUD can be considered.
- **C) Carbamazepine competes with estrogen for protein binding sites, displacing the hormone.** Incorrect. While some drugs can displace other protein-bound drugs, this is not the mechanism by which carbamazepine reduces contraceptive efficacy. The relevant mechanism is hepatic enzyme induction, which increases the metabolic clearance of contraceptive hormones rather than displacing them from binding proteins.
- **D) Carbamazepine directly antagonizes estrogen receptors in the uterus.** Incorrect. Carbamazepine has no direct anti-estrogenic activity at the receptor level. It is an anti-seizure medication that works by blocking sodium channels, not by interacting with hormone receptors. The contraceptive failure occurs because of increased hepatic metabolism of hormones via CYP3A4 induction.
