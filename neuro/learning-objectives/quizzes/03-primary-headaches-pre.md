# Primary Headaches — Pre-Quiz

**1. Which of the following is NOT one of the SNOOP mnemonic categories used to screen for secondary headache features?**

A) Systemic symptoms or illness
B) Neurologic symptoms or signs
C) Onset characteristics
D) Nociceptive pain intensity rating

**2. According to the ICHD-3 diagnostic criteria for migraine without aura, how many attacks fulfilling the remaining criteria must a patient have experienced?**

A) At least 2 attacks
B) At least 3 attacks
C) At least 5 attacks
D) At least 10 attacks

**3. What is the typical untreated duration of a single migraine headache attack?**

A) 30 minutes to 2 hours
B) 4 to 72 hours
C) 1 to 7 days
D) 15 to 180 minutes

**4. Which demographic group has the highest prevalence of migraine?**

A) Men aged 50-60
B) Women of childbearing age (20-40 years)
C) Adolescent males
D) Postmenopausal women

**5. Which of the following best describes the most common type of migraine aura?**

A) Motor weakness on one side of the body
B) Auditory hallucinations
C) Visual phenomena such as zigzag lines or scotomata
D) Olfactory disturbances

**6. Which feature best distinguishes tension-type headache from migraine?**

A) Tension-type headache is always unilateral
B) Tension-type headache is associated with nausea and vomiting
C) Tension-type headache presents as bilateral, band-like pressure that is not worsened by activity
D) Tension-type headache lasts less than 30 minutes per episode

**7. Which triad of findings defines Horner's syndrome?**

A) Ptosis, mydriasis, and hyperhidrosis
B) Ptosis, miosis, and anhidrosis
C) Proptosis, miosis, and lacrimation
D) Diplopia, mydriasis, and anhidrosis

**8. Which class of abortive migraine medication acts as a 5-HT1B/D receptor agonist and is contraindicated in patients with ischemic heart disease?**

A) NSAIDs
B) Ergots
C) Triptans
D) CGRP receptor antagonists

**9. Which of the following is an indication for starting migraine prophylactic therapy?**

A) A single severe migraine episode per year
B) More than 4 migraine attacks per month
C) Migraine with visual aura occurring once every 6 months
D) Mild headache that resolves with acetaminophen

**10. In the trigeminothalamic pathway, sensory afferents from CN V ultimately synapse in which thalamic nucleus before projecting to the primary somatosensory cortex?**

A) Ventral posterolateral (VPL) nucleus

## Answer Key

### Q1. Answer: D
- **A) Systemic symptoms or illness.** Incorrect. This is the "S" in SNOOP and includes fever, rash, vomiting, pregnancy/postpartum status, cancer, and immunocompromised states.
- **B) Neurologic symptoms or signs.** Incorrect. This is the "N" in SNOOP and includes focal deficits, altered mental status, seizure, syncope, papilledema, and stiff neck.
- **C) Onset characteristics.** Incorrect. This is the first "O" in SNOOP and refers to first or worst headache, sudden onset, and age considerations (<5 or >40 years old).
- **D) Nociceptive pain intensity rating.** Correct. There is no "nociceptive pain intensity rating" category in the SNOOP mnemonic. The second "P" stands for "Prior headache history is different," referring to a worsening pattern or rapid progression in severity or frequency.

### Q2. Answer: C
- **A) At least 2 attacks.** Incorrect. Two attacks is the minimum threshold for diagnosing migraine with aura, not migraine without aura, which requires at least 5 fulfilling attacks.
- **B) At least 3 attacks.** Incorrect. This number does not correspond to any migraine diagnostic criterion. The ICHD-3 criteria specify at least 5 attacks for migraine without aura.
- **C) At least 5 attacks.** Correct. Criterion A of the ICHD-3 migraine without aura diagnosis requires at least 5 attacks fulfilling criteria B through D, which address duration, headache characteristics, and associated symptoms.
- **D) At least 10 attacks.** Incorrect. Ten attacks is not required. The threshold is 5 attacks, which helps distinguish migraine from isolated or infrequent headache episodes.

### Q3. Answer: B
- **A) 30 minutes to 2 hours.** Incorrect. This duration is too short for migraine. A duration of 15-180 minutes is more characteristic of cluster headache, not migraine.
- **B) 4 to 72 hours.** Correct. Criterion B of the migraine diagnostic criteria specifies that untreated or unsuccessfully treated headache attacks last 4 to 72 hours. This prolonged duration is a key differentiating feature from cluster headache and tension-type headache.
- **C) 1 to 7 days.** Incorrect. While migraine can last up to 3 days (72 hours), a duration exceeding 72 hours should prompt consideration of status migrainosus or alternative diagnoses.
- **D) 15 to 180 minutes.** Incorrect. This duration range (15 minutes to 3 hours) is characteristic of cluster headache, not migraine.

### Q4. Answer: B
- **A) Men aged 50-60.** Incorrect. Migraine prevalence peaks in the late 30s and declines during the 50s. Additionally, males have a significantly lower prevalence (5.6%) compared to females (17.1%).
- **B) Women of childbearing age (20-40 years).** Correct. Migraine prevalence is 17.1% in females and peaks in women of childbearing age (20-40 years). Hormonal fluctuations, particularly estrogen changes, are believed to contribute to this heightened prevalence.
- **C) Adolescent males.** Incorrect. Although migraine can occur in adolescents, the highest prevalence is in women aged 20-40. Before puberty, migraine prevalence is roughly equal between sexes.
- **D) Postmenopausal women.** Incorrect. Migraine prevalence declines during the 50s, coinciding with menopause. The peak prevalence is in women of childbearing age.

### Q5. Answer: C
- **A) Motor weakness on one side of the body.** Incorrect. Motor weakness is explicitly NOT considered part of typical migraine aura. Hemiplegic migraine, which involves motor weakness, is classified separately.
- **B) Auditory hallucinations.** Incorrect. Auditory hallucinations are not a recognized type of migraine aura. Common aura types include visual, sensory, and language/speech disturbances.
- **C) Visual phenomena such as zigzag lines or scotomata.** Correct. Approximately 99% of migraine auras are visual, manifesting as zigzag lines (fortification spectra), scotomata, or other visual disturbances. Auras develop gradually over at least 4 minutes and resolve within 60 minutes.
- **D) Olfactory disturbances.** Incorrect. Olfactory disturbances are not a standard migraine aura type. While osmophobia (sensitivity to smells) can accompany migraine, it is a symptom rather than a formal aura.

### Q6. Answer: C
- **A) Tension-type headache is always unilateral.** Incorrect. Tension-type headache is typically bilateral, in contrast to migraine, which is often unilateral. The bilateral, band-like distribution is a hallmark of tension-type headache.
- **B) Tension-type headache is associated with nausea and vomiting.** Incorrect. Tension-type headache is devoid of migrainous phenomena such as nausea and vomiting. The presence of nausea or vomiting should raise suspicion for migraine instead.
- **C) Tension-type headache presents as bilateral, band-like pressure that is not worsened by activity.** Correct. TTH is characterized by bilateral, band-like pressure of mild-to-moderate intensity that waxes and wanes. Unlike migraine, it is not aggravated by routine physical activity and lacks nausea or vomiting.
- **D) Tension-type headache lasts less than 30 minutes per episode.** Incorrect. Tension-type headache can last from minutes to days. While individual episodes may be brief, the typical duration is not limited to less than 30 minutes.

### Q7. Answer: B
- **A) Ptosis, mydriasis, and hyperhidrosis.** Incorrect. Horner's syndrome results from a sympathetic pathway lesion, causing the opposite: miosis (not mydriasis) and anhidrosis (not hyperhidrosis). Mydriasis and hyperhidrosis would indicate sympathetic overactivity.
- **B) Ptosis, miosis, and anhidrosis.** Correct. Horner's syndrome results from disruption of the sympathetic pathway and is characterized by ptosis (drooping eyelid due to loss of superior tarsal muscle innervation), miosis (constricted pupil due to unopposed parasympathetic tone on the sphincter pupillae), and anhidrosis (decreased sweating on the ipsilateral face).
- **C) Proptosis, miosis, and lacrimation.** Incorrect. Proptosis (eye protrusion) is not a feature of Horner's syndrome. Lacrimation (tearing) is associated with cluster headache autonomic features but is not part of the Horner's triad.
- **D) Diplopia, mydriasis, and anhidrosis.** Incorrect. Diplopia and mydriasis are not features of Horner's syndrome. Mydriasis would suggest a parasympathetic (CN III) lesion, while Horner's syndrome produces miosis.

### Q8. Answer: C
- **A) NSAIDs.** Incorrect. NSAIDs such as naproxen and ibuprofen work by inhibiting cyclooxygenase enzymes, not serotonin receptors. They are first-line abortive treatments but do not carry cardiovascular contraindications specific to 5-HT receptor agonism.
- **B) Ergots.** Incorrect. Although ergots (e.g., dihydroergotamine) interact with serotonin receptors, they are not classified specifically as selective 5-HT1B/D agonists. Ergots have broader receptor activity and are contraindicated in peripheral vascular disease and coronary artery disease.
- **C) Triptans.** Correct. Triptans (e.g., sumatriptan) are selective 5-HT1B/D receptor agonists that cause vasoconstriction of dilated cranial arteries and inhibit neuropeptide release. Due to their vasoconstrictive properties, they are contraindicated in patients with ischemic heart disease and uncontrolled hypertension.
- **D) CGRP receptor antagonists.** Incorrect. CGRP receptor antagonists (gepants), such as ubrogepant and rimegepant, block calcitonin gene-related peptide receptors. They do not act on serotonin receptors and lack the vasoconstrictive effects that make triptans contraindicated in cardiovascular disease.

### Q9. Answer: B
- **A) A single severe migraine episode per year.** Incorrect. Prophylactic therapy is reserved for patients with frequent migraines (typically >4 per month) or significant functional impairment despite acute treatment. A single annual episode would be managed with abortive therapy alone.
- **B) More than 4 migraine attacks per month.** Correct. Having more than 4 attacks per month is a standard indication for initiating prophylactic therapy. Other indications include recurring migraines significantly interfering with daily routine despite acute treatment, failure or contraindication of acute medications, and medication overuse headache.
- **C) Migraine with visual aura occurring once every 6 months.** Incorrect. Infrequent migraines, even those with aura, do not meet the threshold for prophylaxis. The presence of aura alone is not an indication for prophylactic treatment.
- **D) Mild headache that resolves with acetaminophen.** Incorrect. A headache that responds well to simple over-the-counter analgesics does not warrant prophylactic therapy. Prophylaxis is indicated when acute treatment fails or is insufficient.

### Q10. Answer: C
- **A) Ventral posterolateral (VPL) nucleus.** Incorrect. The VPL nucleus receives somatosensory input from the body via the spinothalamic tract and dorsal column-medial lemniscal pathway. Facial sensory information carried by CN V projects to the VPM nucleus instead.
- **B) Lateral geniculate nucleus (LGN).** Incorrect. The LGN is the thalamic relay for visual information from the optic tract. It is not involved in somatosensory processing from the trigeminal nerve.
- **C) Ventral posteromedial (VPM) nucleus.** Correct. The trigeminothalamic pathway carries facial pain, temperature, and touch information from CN V. After synapsing in the spinal nucleus of V in the medulla, fibers decussate and ascend to the VPM nucleus of the thalamus, which then projects to the primary somatosensory cortex.
- **D) Medial dorsal nucleus.** Incorrect. The medial dorsal nucleus of the thalamus is primarily associated with connections to the prefrontal cortex and is involved in executive function and emotional processing, not primary somatosensory relay.
