## Part 1: Clinical PNS Diseases (Dr. Le)

### General PNS Pattern Recognition
- PNS pattern of disease is characterized by **absence of cognitive deficits**, low/absent/normal reflexes, decreased tone (flaccidity), fasciculations, and atrophy
- Depending on the PNS lesion site, sensory +/- motor deficits may follow a nerve/root/plexus distribution

### Y-Axis Localization: Sites Along the PNS/LMN
- **Radiculopathy** (e.g., slipped disc)
- **Sensory ganglionopathy** (e.g., shingles)
- **Brachial plexopathy** (e.g., Erb-Duchenne palsy, Klumpke palsy)
- **Peripheral neuropathies:**
  - Focal mononeuropathy (e.g., Bell's palsy)
  - Mononeuritis multiplex (e.g., vasculitis)
  - Chronic distal-symmetric polyneuropathy (e.g., DM neuropathy, hereditary neuropathies)
  - Acute inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy (e.g., GBS)
- **Neuromuscular junction** (e.g., myasthenia gravis)
- **Muscle** (e.g., polymyositis/dermatomyositis, genetic muscular dystrophies)
- **Anterior horn cell** (e.g., SMA, ALS)

### Case 1: Bell's Palsy (Mononeuropathy - CN VII)
- Unilateral complete hemiface weakness including frontalis, eye closure, and mouth
- LMN CN VII lesion (entire hemiface involved, unlike UMN which spares forehead)
- Associated with hyperacusis and taste disturbance

### Case 2: Myasthenia Gravis (NMJ)
- **Hallmark: fatiguable weakness** - provoked by exercise, improves with rest
- Autoimmune disease: antibodies against acetylcholine receptor (AChR) in 70-80%, MuSK Ab in ~10%, LRP4 Ab in some, ~10% seronegative
- **EOM involvement:** ptosis and non-anatomic diplopia (~80-90%)
- **Bulbar weakness** (15-20%): nasal speech, masticatory and swallowing difficulty
- **Respiratory involvement:** especially orthopnea; C3,4,5 keeps the diaphragm alive
- Upper and lower limb weakness, proximal predominance
- Sensation and reflexes normal
- **Bimodal:** younger women (20-30s) and older men (60-80s)
- **Diagnosis:** AChR or MuSK antibodies; repetitive nerve stimulation shows >10% decrement
- **Treatment:**
  - Symptomatic: cholinesterase inhibitors (pyridostigmine) - partially effective, inadequate for severe disease
  - Immunologic (for life-threatening disease): PLEX (plasmapheresis), IVIg, steroids
  - Chronic immunosuppressants: azathioprine, mycophenolate, cyclosporine, cyclophosphamide, rituximab, methotrexate
  - Newer FDA-approved biologics (2021-2023): eculizumab (complement inhibitor), efgartigimod (reduces AChR+ Ab via FcRN blockade), rozanolixizumab (monoclonal Ab promoting IgG degradation), ravulizumab (complement C5 inhibitor), zilucoplan (peptide preventing MAC destruction)
  - **Thymectomy** recommended for patients with thymoma and for patients <60 with generalized MG

### Neuroanatomy Review: Extraocular Muscles (CN III, IV, VI)

| CN | Function | Dysfunction |
|----|----------|-------------|
| III | Levator palpebrae + all EOM except superior oblique and lateral rectus; PANS (pupil constriction) superficially on CN III | Ptosis, eye rests down and out, can only abduct and go down/intort; dilated pupil |
| IV | Superior oblique (depression and intorsion; "look at the tip of your nose") | Hypertropic eye; vertical diplopia worse looking toward nose + down; head tilt to compensate |
| VI | Lateral rectus (abduction) | Horizontal diplopia; inability to abduct; eye medially deviated |

- Pupil-sparing CN III palsy can result from ischemia to internal fibers (e.g., diabetic CN III ischemic palsy)

### Case 3: Myopathy (Muscle Disease)
- **Presentation:** progressive weakness, proximal > distal muscles
- Tasks impaired: combing hair, lifting items overhead, climbing stairs
- Sensory system spared
- Reflexes normal or low
- **Serum CK elevated** (distinguishes from neurogenic disease)
- EMG/NCS: small amplitude, polyphasic potentials (myopathic pattern)
- Muscle biopsy may be needed for precise diagnosis

### Acquired Myopathies
- **Inflammatory:** polymyositis, dermatomyositis, inclusion body myositis (IBM), autoimmune necrotizing myopathy
- **Drug/Toxic:** steroid, statin, alcohol
- **Metabolic**
- **Endocrine:** hypothyroidism
- **Vitamin D deficiency**
- **ICU/critical illness myopathy**

### Case 4: Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy (Muscle - Genetic)
- 6-year-old boy with progressive weakness, no sensory deficits
- **Gower's sign:** uses hands/arms to "walk up" body from squatting (proximal lower limb weakness)
- **Pseudohypertrophy of calves:** muscle replaced by fat/connective tissue
- Elevated AST and CK
- X-linked recessive, DMD gene at Xp21.1 (absence of dystrophin)
- **DMD:** onset age 2-6, non-ambulatory by 10-12, severely disabled by 20, death ~18-20 years from respiratory failure
- **BMD (Becker):** milder, highly variable onset and severity
- Treatment: glucocorticoids (prednisone); newer antisense oligonucleotide therapies (eteplirsen, golodirsen) for certain mutations - not curative

### Hereditary Myopathies (Muscular Dystrophies) - Overview
- Muscular Dystrophy (Duchenne/Becker dystrophinopathies)
- Myotonic Dystrophy (DM1 & DM2)
- Facioscapulohumeral Dystrophy
- Limb girdle muscular dystrophy
- Mitochondrial myopathies
- Metabolic myopathies
- Channelopathies
- Congenital myopathies

### Case 5: ALS / Motor Neuron Disease (Anterior Horn Cell)
- **Hallmark: combination of UMN + LMN signs** with pure motor weakness
- Progressive, insidious, often **asymmetric**
- Fasciculations + muscle atrophy (LMN) with hyperreflexia + Babinski (UMN)
- Affects limbs, bulbar muscles (dysarthria, dysphagia), respiratory muscles
- EMG/NCS confirms widespread motor denervation, sensory system spared
- **Median survival ~4 years** from symptom onset
- Lou Gehrig's disease
- **Spectrum of motor neuron diseases:**
  - LMN end: Werdnig-Hoffmann/SMA Type 1 (newborns), Intermediate SMA Type 2 (babies), Kugelberg-Welander SMA Type 3 (kids), Motoneuron disease Type 4 (adults)
  - Middle: ALS (progressive bulbar palsy, progressive limb)
  - UMN end: Primary Lateral Sclerosis
- **SMA:** autosomal recessive, deletion in SMN gene on chromosome 5q13.2
  - SMA Type 1 (Werdnig-Hoffmann): onset <6 months, most common and most severe, was universally fatal by age 2
  - New therapies: nusinersen (intrathecal antisense oligonucleotide), onasemnogene abeparvovec (IV gene replacement via AAV), risdiplam (oral splicing modifier)
- **ALS treatments:** riluzole (modest survival benefit), edaravone (may slow progression), sodium-phenylbutyrate-taurursodiol (2022), tofersen (2023, for SOD1 genetic variants); multidisciplinary supportive care

### Fact Check: Pattern Matching

| Pattern | Diagnosis |
|---------|-----------|
| Chronic distal symmetric stocking-glove | Peripheral polyneuropathy (NOT mononeuritis multiplex) |
| Acute symmetric ascending paralysis, areflexia | Guillain-Barre Syndrome (GBS) |
| Unilateral complete hemiface weakness | Bell's palsy (NOT just "lower face" - that's UMN) |
| Fatiguable pure motor weakness | Myasthenia gravis (NOT just "myopathy") |
| Chronic, progressive asymmetric UMN + LMN signs | ALS |

## Part 2: Peripheral Nerve Neuropathology (Dr. Vogel)

### Types of Peripheral Nerve Disease by Cellular Component

#### A. Demyelinating Neuropathies
- Primary involvement of myelin/Schwann cells
- **Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease (CMT1a):** most common inherited peripheral neuropathy (125,000 affected in U.S.)
  - Autosomal dominant; tandem duplication on chromosome 17p11.2-12 → trisomic expression of PMP-22 (peripheral myelin protein 22)
  - Too much PMP-22 → instability of peripheral myelin with repeated demyelination and remyelination
  - Progressive distal motor and sensory deficit, slowed nerve conduction
  - Pes cavus foot deformity, palpable nerve enlargement
  - Pathology: **"onion bulbs"** - concentric layers of Schwann cells separated by collagen from repeated demyelination/remyelination cycles → thickened nerves
  - Treatment: supportive
- **HNPP (Hereditary neuropathy with liability to pressure palsies):** deletion of PMP-22 → vulnerable to pressure palsies lasting hours-days → permanent nerve injury; **"tomaculous" neuropathy** (sausage-like myelin swellings)
- Compact myelin proteins: MBP (myelin basic protein), MPZ (myelin protein zero), PMP22, periaxin (PRX); mutations in these cause CMT type 1 category

#### B. Infectious Polyneuropathies
- **Leprosy:** granulomatous inflammation in nerves; organisms difficult to find in tuberculoid form
- **Diphtheria:** exotoxin causes demyelination; paresthesias and weakness; early loss of proprioception/vibratory sensation; incomplete blood-nerve barrier in sensory ganglia allows toxin entry
- **Varicella-Zoster:** latent in sensory ganglia of spinal cord and brainstem; reactivation → painful vesicular skin eruption in dermatomal distribution (shingles), most frequently thoracic or trigeminal; ganglia show neuronal destruction/loss with mononuclear infiltrates, regional necrosis, hemorrhage

#### C. Axonal Neuropathies - Two Types

| Feature | Dying Back Axonopathy | Wallerian Degeneration |
|---------|----------------------|----------------------|
| Pattern | **Most common type** of peripheral neuropathy | Focal or multifocal neuropathy |
| Mechanism | Failure of axonal transport; fibers "die back" from periphery | Axonal degeneration distal to injury site |
| Distribution | Symmetric **stocking-glove** pattern | Follows specific nerve territory |
| Recovery | Slow, if any | Better prognosis; regeneration begins immediately (axons grow 1-2 mm/day) |
| Cell body | Intact | Undergoes **central chromatolysis** (enlargement, rounding, peripheral displacement of nucleus, enlargement of nucleolus, dispersion of Nissl substance) |

- **Axonal transport:** anterograde and retrograde transport via microtubules keeps the nerve axon healthy; the volume of a long axon far exceeds that of the neuronal cytoplasm around its nucleus; longest nerves are most vulnerable to transport failure
- **Length-dependent polyneuropathies** (dying back): stocking-glove deficit pattern; denervation atrophy of muscle

#### D. Vascular Neuropathies
- **Mononeuritis multiplex:** classic cause is **necrotizing arteritis (vasculitis)** causing ischemia to a peripheral nerve → Wallerian degeneration
- Classic in **polyarteritis nodosa** (multisystem: nerve, muscle, kidney/hematuria, skin, brain)
- Fibrinoid necrosis of vessel wall → secondary occlusion

### Diabetic Neuropathy
- 4 basic patterns, all related to vascular disease:
  1. **Distal symmetrical polyneuropathy** (small-fiber predominant) - most common
  2. Radiculoplexopathy and radiculopathy
  3. Mononeuropathy and mononeuritis multiplex
  4. Autonomic neuropathy
- Prevalence depends on disease duration: up to 80% of those with DM >15 years

### Metabolic and Nutritional Neuropathies
- **Uremic neuropathy:** distal, symmetric; may be asymptomatic or cause muscle cramps, dysesthesias; axonal with regeneration/recovery after dialysis
- Chronic liver disease, chronic respiratory insufficiency, thyroid dysfunction
- **Vitamin deficiencies:** Thiamine, B12 (cobalamin), B6 (pyridoxine), E (alpha-tocopherol)
- **Alcohol:** often leads to axonal neuropathy with strong contribution of associated dietary deficiency
- **Toxic neuropathies:** industrial/environmental chemicals, biologic toxins, therapeutic drugs, heavy metals (lead, arsenic)
- Many "idiopathic" peripheral neuropathies may have metabolic or toxic underpinnings; complete history and lab workup needed

### Neuropathies Associated with Malignancy
- Direct infiltration/compression of peripheral nerves (mononeuropathy, brachial plexopathy, cranial nerve palsies, polyradiculopathy from meningeal carcinomatosis)
- **Paraneoplastic:** most common type is sensorimotor neuropathy, frequently associated with small-cell carcinoma of the lung
- **Plasma cell neoplasms:** amyloid deposition in peripheral nerves; monoclonal immunoglobulin targeting myelin-associated glycoprotein

## Part 3: Diseases of Muscle - Neuropathology (Dr. Vogel)

### Normal Skeletal Muscle Histology
- Composed of long spindle-shaped cells bundled in fascicles, enveloped by fibrous connective tissue (epimysium → perimysium → endomysium)
- **Type 1 fibers (red/dark):** more mitochondria, myoglobin, lipid; fatigue-resistant; weight-bearing muscles
- **Type 2 fibers (white/light):** less mitochondria; more glycogen; fatigue-sensitive; quick-acting muscles
- Normal **mosaic pattern** of fiber types on cross-section; some diseases selectively affect certain fiber types (diagnostically useful)

### Neurogenic Changes in Muscle
- ~1/3 of muscle biopsies show predominantly neurogenic changes
- Denervated fibers shrink and atrophy but do not die; can be restored with reinnervation
- Patterns: single fiber atrophy → fiber type grouping (reinnervation) → group atrophy (re-denervation)
- Myofiber necrosis does NOT result from denervation, so **serum CK is NOT elevated** in neurogenic disease (key distinguisher from primary myopathy)
- Classic neurogenic disease: **ALS** - spinal cord shows loss of corticospinal motor fibers and atrophic anterior roots; muscle shows group atrophy with compensatory hypertrophy

### Diagnostic Tools Before Muscle Biopsy
1. History and physical examination
2. EMG and nerve conduction studies
3. Serum creatine phosphokinase (CK) - elevated in primary muscle diseases
4. MRI or ultrasound of muscle/spine
5. Cerebrospinal fluid
6. Genetic testing
- Non-invasive techniques increasingly diagnostic; biopsy may be needed to confirm/support diagnosis, justify therapy, provide genetic counseling

### Myopathies - Classification
1. **Dystrophic** (muscular dystrophies)
2. **Inflammatory**
3. **Congenital** (structural)
4. **Metabolic** (including storage, toxic)

---

### Muscular Dystrophies
- **Definition:** genetically transmitted diseases causing progressive muscle weakness through degeneration and loss of myofibers, usually with elevated CK
- Key proteins affected link the contractile apparatus (actin) to the cell membrane and extracellular matrix
- Loss/deficiency → instability under contractile stress → membrane leakage → increased cytoplasmic calcium → cell death

#### Pathology (non-specific findings)
- Fiber degeneration and regeneration
- Marked variation in muscle fiber diameter
- Splitting of enlarged fibers
- Internal nuclei (normally peripheral)
- Endomysial fibrosis
- Replacement of degenerated muscle by fat (→ pseudohypertrophy, as in Duchenne calf hypertrophy)
- Necrosis with macrophage infiltration → elevated serum CK

#### Dystrophinopathies (X-linked recessive)
- **Duchenne MD (DMD):** 30/100,000 live male births; ~1/3 new mutations; **absence of dystrophin**
  - Onset age 2-6, non-ambulatory by 10-12, severely disabled by 20
  - Death ~18-20 years from respiratory failure, heart failure, pneumonia
  - Cardiac and cognitive involvement
  - DMD gene Xp21.1 → premature stop codon → severely truncated/absent protein
- **Becker MD (BMD):** 3/100,000 live male births; ~10% new mutations; **reduced/partially functional dystrophin**
  - Milder, highly variable onset and severity
  - Deletion of portion of central rod domain → shorter but functional protein
- **Dystrophin:** N-terminus links to actin, C-terminus links to sarcolemmal proteins (dystroglycan complex, sarcoglycans) → links contractile apparatus to cell membrane and ECM
- H&E histology completely nonspecific; **immunohistochemistry** for dystrophin domains (R-DYS rod, C-DYS C-terminus, N-DYS N-terminus) is diagnostic

#### Myotonic Dystrophy
- **2nd most common muscular dystrophy**; most common adult-onset form
- Autosomal dominant, linked to 19q13.2
- **CTG trinucleotide repeat expansion** in 3' UTR of DMPK gene (Dystrophia Myotonica Protein Kinase)
- DMPK mutant RNA binds and sequesters transcription factors
- Normal: 4-37 repeats; Affected: 50 to >4000 repeats
- Severity and age of onset correlate with repeat number
- **Anticipation:** amplification across generations → earlier/more severe disease in offspring
- Two genotypes: DM1 and DM2
- Multi-system disease: **cataracts, cardiomyopathy/arrhythmia, cognitive impairment**
- **Myotonia:** delayed relaxation of muscles; demonstrable at bedside with grip myotonia and percussion myotonia
- Classic phenotype: frontal balding, temporalis atrophy, sternocleidomastoid atrophy, ptosis, "fish mouth"/drooping mouth (facial weakness), gynecomastia
- Pathology: fiber atrophy AND hypertrophy, many central nuclei, sarcoplasmic masses, ring fibers
- Biopsies almost never done because clinical presentation is so classic

---

### Congenital (Structural) Myopathies
- Cause of infantile hypotonia (myopathic, neuropathic, or metabolic)
- Not all infants with congenital myopathies are obviously hypotonic
- Some clinical features may suggest specific diagnosis; muscle pathology often necessary

#### Nemaline Myopathy
- Diagnostic finding: **red inclusions (nemaline rods)** on Gomori trichrome stain; dark bodies on electron microscopy
- Patient variably weak and hypotonic; CK NOT elevated

#### Central Core Disease
- Diagnostic finding: **central clearing** (empty spaces in center of fibers) due to lack of mitochondria in fiber center
- **RYR1 (ryanodine receptor 1) gene mutations**
- **Risk of malignant hyperthermia** with inhalational anesthetics → deranged calcium homeostasis in muscle → lethal hyperthermia

---

### Inflammatory Myopathies

#### Dermatomyositis
- Immune disease affecting muscle, skin, and other tissues
- **Diagnostic criteria (3 of 5):** symmetrical proximal weakness progressing over weeks/months; abnormal muscle biopsy; elevated serum muscle enzymes (CK, aldolase, LDH); characteristic EMG abnormalities; characteristic rash
- **Skin findings:** heliotrope rash (periorbital), Gottron's papules (scaly erythematous lesions over knuckles), extensor surface rash, dilated capillaries around nailbeds, periungual hemorrhages
- Childhood form: calcinosis, periorbital edema, erythematous rash, contractures; rare kidney/bowel/retinal infarcts
- Adult form: contractures/effusions, arrhythmias, ILD; **increased malignancy risk** (must screen)
- **Pathology:** **perifascicular atrophy** (outer edges of fascicles selectively affected) - pathognomonic; muscle pathology secondary to capillary disease
- If diagnostic criteria met without biopsy, biopsy not necessary

#### Polymyositis / Immune-Mediated Necrotizing Myopathies
- Direct anti-muscle fiber "attack" (unlike DM which is capillary-mediated)
- **CD8+ cytotoxic T-cell lymphocytes** attacking non-necrotic muscle fibers
- Anti-MHC-1 immunostaining highlights "opsonized" myofibers
- No skin findings (key distinction from DM)
- Anti-synthetase syndrome: Raynaud's, mechanic's hands
- Arrhythmias, ILD; some subtypes have **increased malignancy risk**
- Term "polymyositis" going out of favor → replaced by specific immune-mediated myopathy classifications (IMNM, IMPP, anti-synthetase syndrome)

#### Inclusion Body Myositis (IBM)
- **Most common sporadic myopathy in patients >65 years**
- Slowly progressive weakness in **quadriceps and distal upper extremity** (unlike typical proximal-predominant myopathies)
- Dysphagia from esophageal/pharyngeal involvement
- Modestly elevated CK; most myositis-associated autoantibodies absent
- **Does NOT respond to immunosuppressive treatment** (key distinction)
- Enigmatic disease without known etiology
- **Pathology:** **rimmed vacuoles** - key diagnostic finding

---

### Metabolic and Toxic Myopathies
- **Lipid metabolism defects:** carnitine transport/mitochondrial deficiencies; muscle pain, tightness, myoglobinuria after prolonged exercise/fasting; concomitant cardiomyopathies, fatty liver
- **Glycogen metabolism defects:** McArdle disease (Type V, muscle phosphorylase deficiency); painful cramps with strenuous exercise; myoglobinuria in 50%; muscular exercise fails to raise venous lactate; CK always elevated
- **Mitochondrial myopathies**
- **Toxic myopathies:**
  - **Statins** - most common drug cause
  - Chloroquine/hydroxychloroquine
  - ICU myopathy / myosin deficient myopathy
  - Thyrotoxic myopathy vs. hypothyroidism
  - **Alcohol** - binge drinking → acute rhabdomyolysis → myoglobinuria → renal failure

| PNS Lesion Site | Key Features | Classic Example |
|----------------|-------------|-----------------|
| Peripheral nerve (focal) | Unilateral, follows single nerve distribution | Bell's palsy |
