## 1. Altered Mental Status & Coma

### Definitions
- **Coma** = absence of BOTH arousal AND awareness
- **GCS** = E4 + V5 + M6 (max 15)

### Simon's Rule of 4
First things to do:
1. **ABCs, vitals**
2. **Give thiamine FIRST, then D50, then naloxone** (give thiamine before glucose to prevent Wernicke)
3. Blood & urine tests

Then assess four things:
1. **Pupils** (absent pupillary reflex = brainstem not working)
2. **Doll's eyes / cold calorics** (tests brainstem oculomotor pathways)
3. **Response to pain** - flexor posturing (decorticate) = lesion above midbrain; extensor posturing (decerebrate) = pons/medulla
4. **Meningeal signs**

### Two Localizations of Coma
1. **ARAS** (ascending reticular activating system in upper brainstem - midbrain/pons)
2. **Diffuse bilateral cerebral hemisphere / bilateral thalamus**

### Three Etiologies of Coma
1. **Non-structural (metabolic)** - no lesion; intact cranial nerves, no motor asymmetry (e.g., EtOH intoxication)
2. **Structural** - physical lesion (e.g., stroke, tumor)
3. **Electrical** - seizure/status epilepticus

### Coma Mimics

| Mimic | Definition |
|-------|-----------|
| **Persistent vegetative state** | Seemingly awake (opens eyes, turns head) but unaware of environment. Sleep/wake cycles present. Diffuse cortical dysfunction. |
| **Locked-in state** | Paralyzed but conscious with intact sensation. Bilateral pons lesion. Vertical eye movements spared (midbrain intact). |
| **Complete peripheral nerve paralysis** | Cognitively intact but total nerve paralysis → appears comatose |
| **Post-ictal state** | Minimal responsiveness after a seizure |
| **Abulia / akinetic mutism** | Decreased initiative/executive dysfunction. Large frontal lobe lesion. |
| **Psychogenic unresponsiveness** | e.g., catatonia from psychiatric illness |
| **Minimally conscious state** | Reproducible behavior indicating conscious perception but no reliable communication |
| **Brain death** | Irreversible loss of ALL cortical, subcortical, and brainstem function |

## 3. Movement Disorders

### Pyramidal vs Extrapyramidal

| Pyramidal (voluntary motor) | Extrapyramidal (involuntary motor) |
|---|---|
| Damage → weakness or paralysis | Damage → decreased movement (bradykinesia), excess movement (chorea, ballism), tremor, incoordination |
| Exam: hyperreflexia, spasticity, upgoing Babinski | Exam: non-weakness motor abnormalities, usually preserved strength and reflexes |
| Localization: motor cortex, corticospinal/corticobulbar tracts | Localization: basal ganglia, cerebellum, brainstem nuclei (STN, SN, red nucleus) |

### Key Buzzwords

| Term | Definition | System |
|------|-----------|--------|
| **Spasticity** | Velocity-dependent stiffness ("spastic catch") | Pyramidal |
| **Lead-pipe rigidity** | Stiffness NOT velocity-dependent | Extrapyramidal |
| **Chorea** | Flowing random movements | Extrapyramidal (caudate - think Huntington's) |
| **Hemiballismus** | Fast large-amplitude movements | Extrapyramidal (subthalamic nucleus) |
| **Athetosis** | Slow writhing movements | Extrapyramidal |
| **Ataxia** | Incoordination | Extrapyramidal (cerebellum) |
| **Dysmetria** | Cannot move to target smoothly | Extrapyramidal (cerebellum) |

### High-Yield Movement Disorders
- **Essential tremor:** action/kinetic tremor; treat with beta-blockers (propranolol) or primidone
- **Cerebellar disease:** dysmetria, dysdiadochokinesia, ataxia, dysarthria, truncal titubation
- **Huntington's disease:** autosomal dominant, CAG repeats on chromosome 4; chorea + neuropsychiatric symptoms + cognitive decline

### Tremor Types

| Tremor | When it occurs | Think... |
|--------|---------------|----------|
| **Resting** ("pill rolling") | At rest, improves with movement | Parkinson's disease |
| **Kinetic/Postural** | During movement or sustained posture | Essential tremor |
| **Intention** | Worsens approaching target | Cerebellar disorders |
| **Dystonic** | In a dystonic body part | Dystonia |

All tremors are **extrapyramidal signs**.

## 4. Neurodegenerative Disease & Dementia

### MCI vs Dementia
- **MCI:** cognitive change beyond normal aging but patient **retains independence**
- **Dementia:** cognitive decline + **loss of ability to perform ADLs/iADLs**

### Cognitive Domain Localization

| Domain | Localization |
|--------|-------------|
| Episodic memory | Hippocampus |
| Language production | Left frontal (Broca's area) |
| Language comprehension | Left temporal (Wernicke's area) |
| Visuospatial | Right > left parietal lobes |
| Executive function | Frontal lobes |

### Three Major Dementias

| Feature | Alzheimer's Disease | Lewy Body Dementia | Frontotemporal Dementia |
|---------|---|---|---|
| **Pathology** | Tau tangles + amyloid plaques | Alpha-synuclein (Lewy bodies) | Tau (or TDP-43) |
| **Hallmark** | Loss of short-term recall | Fluctuations, visual hallucinations, REM sleep behavior disorder, parkinsonism | Early executive dysfunction, disinhibition, lack of empathy, compulsions |
| **Imaging** | Hippocampal atrophy | — | Frontal/temporal atrophy |
| **Treatment** | Cholinesterase inhibitors, NMDA blockade (memantine), +/- aducanumab | — | — |

### Prion Disease
- **Rapid** progression over weeks to months
- **Myoclonus**
- MRI: **cortical ribboning**
- CSF: 14-3-3 protein, RT-QuIC

## 5. Parkinson's Disease

### Cardinal Features: **TRAP**
- **T**remor (resting, "pill rolling")
- **R**igidity (lead-pipe; cogwheel = rigidity + tremor)
- **A**kinesia/bradykinesia
- **P**ostural instability

### Parkinsonism vs Parkinson's Disease
- **Parkinsonism** = the clinical syndrome (TRAP features) from any cause
- **Parkinson's disease** = idiopathic parkinsonism due to loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra with Lewy body pathology (alpha-synuclein)

### Treatment (all symptomatic, NOT disease-modifying)

| Drug Class | Examples | MOA | Key Side Effects |
|------------|---------|-----|-----------------|
| **Levodopa** | Carbidopa-levodopa | Increases DA production | Dyskinesias (with chronic use) |
| **DA agonists** | Ropinirole, pramipexole | Directly binds DA receptors | Psychiatric: impulse control, hallucinations, mood |
| **MAO-B inhibitors** | Selegiline, rasagiline | Inhibits DA breakdown | — |
| **COMT inhibitors** | Entacapone, tolcapone | Inhibits DA breakdown | Liver toxicity (tolcapone) |

### Surgical Treatment
- **Deep brain stimulation (DBS)** targeting STN or GPi
- Pallidotomy (rare)
