### 1. Primary Hemostasis
Review the events characterizing primary hemostasis, including endothelial cell injury, platelet adhesion, platelet activation, and platelet aggregation that result in formation of the platelet plug.

### 5. Congenital vs Acquired Factor Deficiency
Outline a diagnostic strategy to distinguish congenital factor deficiency from acquired factor deficiency using history, physical exam, and laboratory evaluation.

## Hemostatic Balance

The hemostatic mechanism is regulated to control bleeding yet avoid thrombosis. This delicate balance exists between:
- **Pro-coagulant forces** that promote clot formation
- **Anti-coagulant forces** that limit clotting

Failure of the hemostatic mechanism leads to pathology:
- **Hemorrhage** (bleeding) when insufficient clotting
- **Thrombosis** (excessive clotting) when regulation fails

---

## Primary Hemostasis

Primary hemostasis involves the formation of a temporary platelet plug at the site of vascular injury. This process occurs in four sequential steps.

### Step 1: Endothelial Cell Injury

**Key Events:**
- Injury to endothelial cells manifests as uncontrolled bleeding (hemorrhage)
- Exposes the **subendothelial matrix** containing:
  - **Collagen**
  - Fibronectin
  - Laminin
- **von Willebrand factor (VWF)** is released from storage in **Weibel-Palade bodies** within endothelial cells

### Step 2: Platelet Adhesion

**Key Events:**
- Inactivated circulating platelets adhere to subendothelial matrix proteins
- **Collagen** binds to **GPIa/IIa (α2β1)** receptors on platelets
- **VWF** binds to **GPIb-IX-V** receptors on platelets
  - High shear forces unravel VWF multimers into a favorable conformation for platelet capture and binding

### Step 3: Platelet Activation

**Key Events:**
- **Outside-in signaling:** Platelets bind collagen via platelet **GPVI** receptor
- **Inside-out signaling:** Platelets bind VWF
- Platelet signaling leads to:
  - Platelet shape change
  - Release of agonists from granules (ADP, TxA2)
  - Recruitment and activation of nearby inactivated platelets in circulation

### Step 4: Platelet Aggregation

**Key Events:**
- Activated platelets bind to other activated platelets through **fibrinogen** bridges
- **Fibrinogen** binds **GPIIb/IIIa (αIIbβ3)** receptors on platelets
- Aggregate of platelets, VWF, collagen, and fibrinogen forms a **platelet plug** (temporary)
- For larger vessels, a stable fibrin clot is needed to permanently stop bleeding → requires **Secondary Hemostasis**

### Summary of Primary Hemostasis

1. **Endothelial Cell Injury** exposes subendothelial collagen and releases VWF
2. **Platelet Adhesion** via collagen-GPIa/IIa and VWF-GPIb-IX-V binding
3. **Platelet Activation** induces shape change, granule release, and recruitment
4. **Platelet Aggregation** through fibrinogen-GPIIb/IIIa bridges forms platelet plug

## Secondary Hemostasis

Secondary hemostasis involves activation of coagulation factors to generate thrombin and form a stable fibrin clot. This process can be understood through the classical cascade model and the cell-based model.

### Classical Coagulation Cascade

The classical cascade divides coagulation into three pathways:

**Contact Activation (Intrinsic) Pathway:**
- XII → XIIa
- XI → XIa
- IX → IXa
- Links to common pathway

**Tissue Factor (Extrinsic) Pathway:**
- Trauma exposes tissue factor (TF)
- VII → VIIa
- Links to common pathway

**Common Pathway:**
- X → Xa
- Prothrombin (II) → Thrombin (IIa)
- Fibrinogen (I) → Fibrin (Ia)
- XIII → XIIIa (cross-links fibrin)

**Important Note:** While the classical cascade outlines the sequence of factor activation, the **cell-based model** more accurately represents cellular hemostasis.

### Cell-Based Model of Hemostasis

The cell-based model emphasizes interactions between cells and coagulation factors through four phases:

#### Phase 1: Initiation (on TF-bearing cells)

**Key Factor: Tissue Factor (TF)**
- Primary physiologic initiator of coagulation
- Integral membrane protein found on:
  - Extravascular cells (smooth muscle in vascular wall)
  - Monocytes/endothelial cells in inflammatory states

**Key Events:**
1. Break in endothelium exposes TF to plasma Factor VII
2. **TF-FVIIa complex** activates FX and FIX
3. FXa activates small amount of FV
4. **FXa + FVa + Ca²⁺** on phospholipid surfaces = **prothrombinase complex**
5. Small amount of thrombin generated

**Important Notes:**
- Coagulation factors exist as zymogens (inactive) until activated
- FIXa moves to platelet surface for later stages
- Source of FVa is likely protein released from platelet alpha granules
- TF-FVIIa complex is eventually inhibited by **tissue factor pathway inhibitor (TFPI)**

#### Phase 2: Amplification (on platelet surface)

**Key Concept:** Platelet-driven thrombin generation

**How Small Amount of Thrombin Amplifies:**
1. Enhances platelet adhesion
2. Fully activates platelets (conformational changes)
3. Activates **FV** (cofactor)
4. Activates **FVIII** (cofactor)
5. Activates **FXI**

**Result:** Platelets localize to damaged endothelium and become primed for procoagulant complex assembly

#### Phase 3: Propagation (on activated platelet surface)

**Key Complexes Assemble:**
- **Intrinsic tenase complex:** FVIIIa/FIXa/FX
- **Prothrombinase complex:** FXa/FVa/FII

**Key Events:**
1. FIXa (formed during initiation) diffuses from TF-expressing cells to activated platelets
2. Large-scale thrombin generation = **thrombin burst**
3. Thrombin (FIIa) converts fibrinogen to fibrin

#### Phase 4: Clot Stabilization

**Key Events:**
1. Thrombin burst leads to cleavage of fibrinopeptides from fibrinogen
2. Fibrin monomers polymerize to form fibrin polymer
3. Thrombin activates **FXIII → FXIIIa**
4. **FXIIIa stabilizes clot** by cross-linking fibrin strands

### Summary of Secondary Hemostasis

**Initiation:**
- Occurs on TF-expressing cells
- TF-FVIIa activates FX and FIX
- Small amount of thrombin generated

**Amplification:**
- Thrombin activates platelets for procoagulant complex assembly
- Activates FV and FVIII (cofactors)

**Propagation:**
- Intrinsic tenase complex and prothrombinase assemble on activated platelet surface
- Large-scale thrombin generation (thrombin burst)
- Fibrinogen → Fibrin

**Stabilization:**
- FXIIIa cross-links fibrin for stable clot

## Checks and Balances of Hemostasis

Multiple mechanisms limit clot formation to the site of injury and prevent pathologic thrombosis.

### 1. Localization of Coagulation Factors

**Principle:** Coagulation factors bound to cell surfaces are protected from plasma protease inhibitors

**Mechanisms:**
- TF-bearing cells vs. platelet surfaces provide spatial organization
- **FXa** produced by TF/FVIIa is quickly inactivated by **TFPI** or **antithrombin (AT)** if it leaves the TF-bearing cell surface
- Factors remain active when bound to appropriate cell surfaces

### 2. Endothelial Cell Containment

**How Intact Endothelial Cells Help:**
- Thrombin that diffuses from injury site is:
  - Inhibited by **antithrombin (AT)**, OR
  - Binds to **thrombomodulin (TM)** on endothelial cell surface

**Thrombin-Thrombomodulin Complex:**
1. Thrombin binds thrombomodulin on endothelial cells
2. Complex activates **Protein C → Activated Protein C (APC)**
3. APC binds cofactor **Protein S**
4. **APC/Protein S complex inactivates FVIIIa and FVa**
5. Result: Slows amplification and propagation of secondary hemostasis

**Natural Anticoagulants:**
- **Protein C**
- **Protein S**
- **Antithrombin (AT)**

### 3. Fibrinolytic Pathway

**Purpose:** Degrades fibrin clot to facilitate tissue repair and prevent excessive thrombosis

**Key Steps:**
1. Damaged endothelial cells release **tissue plasminogen activator (tPA)**
2. Plasminogen binds to fibrin at lysine binding sites
3. tPA, plasminogen, and fibrin form a complex
4. Complex activates plasminogen → **plasmin**
5. **Plasmin degrades fibrin** to fibrin degradation products
6. Final product: **D-dimer** (cross-linked fibrin fragments)

**Regulation:**
- **Plasminogen activator inhibitors (PAI-1 and PAI-2)** regulate tPA activity
- **Plasmin inhibitors (α2-antiplasmin, α2-macroglobulin, C1-esterase inhibitor)** prevent excessive fibrinolysis

## Laboratory Testing in Hemostasis

### Diagnostic Approach to Bleeding

Evaluation of bleeding should include:
1. **Comprehensive history:**
   - Bleeding symptoms (epistaxis, bruising, oral bleeding, menorrhagia, surgical bleeding, hemarthrosis, etc.)
   - Medical, surgical, and family history
   - Medications (NSAIDs, aspirin, anticoagulants, herbal supplements)
   - Birth history (for pediatrics): maternal history, vitamin K administration

2. **Physical examination**

3. **Laboratory studies**

### General Laboratory Tests

**Complete Blood Count (CBC) with Differential:**
- **Platelet count**
- **Mean platelet volume (MPV)** → assesses platelet size

**Peripheral Smear Review:**
- Platelet number
- Platelet morphology and granularity
- Platelet clumping
- Sample integrity (heated sample, artifact)
- Large platelets suggest increased turnover

**Complete Metabolic Panel:**
- High-level assessment for liver or renal function

### Primary Hemostasis Testing

Tests that evaluate platelet function and von Willebrand factor.

#### Platelet Function Analyzer-100 (PFA-100)

**Principle:** Measures ability of platelets to adhere and aggregate to a membrane coated with collagen/epinephrine or collagen/ADP under high shear stress

**Result:** Closure time (time to occlude aperture)
- Normal: 0-300 seconds
- Abnormal: >300 seconds (prolonged closure time)

**Pros:**
- Easy and quick screening test
- Potential screening for platelet function disorders and von Willebrand disease (VWD)

**Cons:**
- Low sensitivity/specificity
- Sensitive to multiple factors:
  - Hematocrit <30%
  - Platelet count <100,000
  - Medications (anticoagulants, NSAIDs/aspirin, fish oil)

#### Platelet Aggregometry

**Gold standard** for testing platelet function

**Light Transmission Aggregometry (LTA):**
- Platelet-rich plasma (PRP) stirred in cuvette at 37°C
- Sits between light source and photocell
- Agonist added → platelets aggregate → absorb less light
- Increased light transmission detected by photocell
- 100% transmission = platelet-poor plasma (PPP)
- 0% transmission = PRP baseline

**Whole Blood Aggregometry (WBA):**
- Measures change in impedance from platelet aggregation
- Same agonists except epinephrine
- Maintains physiologic blood-cell mixture
- Lower blood volume required
- More sensitive to most agonists

**Agonists Tested:**
- ADP
- Collagen
- Ristocetin
- Epinephrine
- Arachidonic acid
- Thrombin

**Pros:**
- Sensitive and specific
- Diagnostic capability
- WBA works on icteric/lipemic samples

**Cons:**
- Time and labor intensive
- Must schedule in advance
- Requires fresh sample at room temperature (patient and control)
- Influenced by multiple medications

#### Von Willebrand Factor Testing (3-test panel)

1. **VWF functional test:**
   - VWF:RCoF (ristocetin cofactor) OR
   - VWF:GPIbM (binding to GPIb)

2. **VWF Antigen (VWF:Ag):**
   - Quantifies VWF protein level

3. **Factor VIII activity (FVIII:C):**
   - VWF carries and stabilizes FVIII
   - Low VWF → low FVIII

**Second-tier test:**
- **VWF multimer analysis** if abnormal profile

### Acquired Factors Affecting Platelet Function

**Medications:**
- **Aspirin:** Irreversibly inhibits COX pathway for 5-7 days
- **NSAIDs:** Reversibly inhibit COX pathway
- Penicillin
- Tricyclic antidepressants (amitriptyline, imipramine)
- SSRIs (unclear association)
- Herbal supplements (fish oil, ginkgo biloba, ginseng)

**Systemic Disorders:**
- Uremia
- Liver disease
- Cardiopulmonary bypass
- Myelodysplastic and myeloproliferative disorders
- Disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC)
- Sepsis

### Secondary Hemostasis Testing

Tests that evaluate coagulation factor function.

#### Prothrombin Time (PT)

**Principle:**
- Citrated patient plasma mixed with **thromboplastin** (tissue factor from rabbit brain or recombinant source)
- Calcium added in molar excess over citrate
- Measures time for plasma to clot

**Normal Range:** 11-15 seconds (varies by institution/reagent)

**Pathway Tested:** Extrinsic and common pathways
- Factors VII, X, V, II, fibrinogen

#### International Normalized Ratio (INR)

**Formula:** INR = (Patient PT / Control PT)^ISI

**ISI:** International Sensitivity Index
- Correction factor for different reagents/instruments globally
- Standardizes PT results worldwide

**Important Notes:**
- INR is a **calculated value**, not measured
- Has no assigned unit
- Used primarily for **coumadin (warfarin) monitoring**
- Coumadin INR goal: 2-3 (most indications)

#### Activated Partial Thromboplastin Time (aPTT)

**Principle:**
- Citrated patient plasma mixed with:
  - **Partial thromboplastin** (phospholipid)
  - **Activator** (usually contact surface like particulate silica)
- Calcium added in molar excess over citrate
- Measures time for plasma to clot

**Normal Range:** 25-35 seconds (varies by institution)

**Pathway Tested:** Intrinsic and common pathways
- Contact system: XII, XI
- Intrinsic: IX, VIII
- Common: X, V, II, fibrinogen

#### Interpretation of PT/aPTT

**Prolonged aPTT Only (Intrinsic pathway):**
- Factors XII, XI, IX, VIII

**Prolonged PT Only (Extrinsic pathway):**
- Factor VII

**Prolonged PT and aPTT (Common pathway):**
- Factors X, V, II, fibrinogen

**Normal PT and aPTT:**
- Factor XIII (stabilizes clot but not measured by these tests)

#### Coagulation Mixing Study

**Purpose:** Distinguish factor deficiency from factor inhibitor

**Procedure:**
1. Mix patient plasma 1:1 with normal pooled plasma
2. Perform immediate PT or aPTT
3. Repeat after 1-2 hours incubation at 37°C

**Interpretation:**

**Corrects to normal:**
- Suggests **factor deficiency**
- Normal plasma provides missing factors
- Examples: Hemophilia A (FVIII deficiency), Hemophilia B (FIX deficiency)

**Partial or no correction:**
- Suggests **inhibitor** present
- Inhibitor inactivates factors even in mixed sample
- Perform immediate and incubated tests

**Types of Inhibitors:**
- **Lupus anticoagulant:** Antibodies against phospholipid-dependent coagulation tests (does NOT cause bleeding)
- **Specific factor inhibitors:** Antibodies against specific coagulation proteins (e.g., acquired hemophilia A with FVIII antibodies - DOES cause bleeding)
- **Nonspecific inhibitors**

#### Fibrinogen Testing

**Fibrinogen Activity:**
- Direct functional test

**Thrombin Time:**
- Another measure of fibrinogen activity
- Prolonged with low fibrinogen or fibrinogen dysfunction

**Reptilase Time (second-tier):**
- Heparin-insensitive alternative
- Useful if heparin contamination suspected

### Acquired Conditions Affecting Coagulation Tests

**Medications and Supplements:**
- Anticoagulants
- Herbal supplements

**Disease States:**
- Autoimmune disorders
- Post-viral illness acquired factor inhibitors (pediatrics)
- **Liver disease** (decreased synthesis of clotting factors)
- Malignancy
- Consumptive coagulopathy (sepsis, DIC)

**Antibodies:**
- **Lupus anticoagulants:** Prolong aPTT but do NOT cause bleeding (thrombotic risk)
- **Specific factor inhibitors:** Antibodies against specific factors (e.g., acquired FVIII inhibitors cause bleeding)

**Vitamin K Deficiency:**
- Affects factors II, VII, IX, X and Protein C, Protein S
- These factors require vitamin K for **γ-carboxylation** of their Gla domain
- Warfarin inhibits vitamin K epoxide reductase → functional vitamin K deficiency
- Monitored by INR

### Developmental Hemostasis

**Definition:** Physiologic maturation of hemostatic system from in utero through neonatal period

**Why Does This Occur?**
- Coagulation proteins do **not cross the placenta**
- Immature liver function at birth
- No endogenous vitamin K production by gut bacteria at birth (intestinal tract sterile)
- **Vitamin K injection at birth is essential**

**Key Points:**
- Most coagulation factors at birth are below adult levels
- **Exceptions:** Fibrinogen, VWF, FV, FVIII, FXIII (normal or elevated)
- VWF can be elevated at birth
- Despite lower levels, **healthy newborns rarely bleed or clot spontaneously**
- Majority of differences **resolve by 6 months of age**

**Clinical Implication:** When interpreting factor levels in infants <6 months, must use age-appropriate reference ranges

### Sample Processing Factors

Lab results can be influenced by sample or processing issues:

**Collection Issues:**
- Improper citrate anticoagulant amount
- Heparin contamination (labs drawn from central/arterial lines)
- Traumatic phlebotomy

**Sample Quality:**
- **Icteric** (high bilirubin)
- **Lipemic** (high lipid)
- **Hemolyzed** (RBC degradation)

**Processing Issues:**
- Improper temperature
- Centrifugation errors
- Freezing/thawing issues

**Clinical Pearl:** If PT/aPTT seems discordant with clinical picture, consider sample processing issues

## Congenital vs Acquired Factor Deficiency

### Distinguishing Features

#### History Clues

**Suggests Congenital:**
- **Lifelong bleeding history** from childhood
- **Family history** of bleeding disorder
- Bleeding disproportionate to degree of trauma
- Specific patterns:
  - **Hemophilia A/B:** Deep muscle hematomas, hemarthrosis, delayed bleeding after trauma
  - **VWD:** Mucosal bleeding (epistaxis, menorrhagia), easy bruising

**Suggests Acquired:**
- **New-onset bleeding** in previously healthy individual
- Recent medication changes (anticoagulants, NSAIDs)
- Underlying systemic disease (liver disease, malignancy, autoimmune disorder)
- Recent viral illness (acquired inhibitors in children)

#### Laboratory Clues

**Congenital:**
- **Isolated** factor deficiency (e.g., low FVIII only)
- **Mixing study corrects** (factor deficiency, not inhibitor)
- Normal liver and kidney function
- Family members may have similar abnormalities

**Acquired:**
- **Multiple** factor deficiencies (e.g., liver disease affects II, VII, IX, X)
- **Mixing study may not correct** if inhibitor present
- Abnormal liver function tests (decreased synthesis)
- Evidence of consumptive coagulopathy (low fibrinogen, elevated D-dimer in DIC)
- Vitamin K deficiency pattern (low II, VII, IX, X)

### Common Congenital Bleeding Disorders

**Hemophilia A (Factor VIII deficiency):**
- X-linked recessive
- Prolonged aPTT, normal PT
- Mixing study corrects

**Hemophilia B (Factor IX deficiency):**
- X-linked recessive
- Prolonged aPTT, normal PT
- Mixing study corrects

**Von Willebrand Disease:**
- Autosomal (most commonly dominant)
- Most common inherited bleeding disorder
- Abnormal VWF testing
- May have prolonged aPTT (low FVIII)

### Common Acquired Coagulopathies

**Liver Disease:**
- Decreased synthesis of most clotting factors
- Prolonged PT and aPTT
- Low fibrinogen in severe disease

**Vitamin K Deficiency:**
- Affects factors II, VII, IX, X (vitamin K-dependent)
- Prolonged PT first (FVII has shortest half-life)
- Eventually prolonged aPTT

**Warfarin (Coumadin) Therapy:**
- Inhibits vitamin K epoxide reductase
- Mimics vitamin K deficiency
- Monitored by INR

**Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC):**
- Consumption of clotting factors and platelets
- Prolonged PT and aPTT
- Low fibrinogen
- Elevated D-dimer
- Thrombocytopenia

**Acquired Hemophilia A:**
- Autoantibodies against FVIII
- Can occur post-partum, with autoimmune disease, or idiopathic
- Prolonged aPTT that does NOT correct with mixing study
- Normal PT

## Key Concepts

### Primary Hemostasis
- Involves endothelial injury, platelet adhesion (via VWF and collagen), platelet activation, and platelet aggregation
- Forms temporary platelet plug
- Requires adequate platelet number and function
- Disorders cause **mucosal bleeding** (epistaxis, menorrhagia, petechiae, easy bruising)

### Secondary Hemostasis
- Involves coagulation factor activation through initiation, amplification, and propagation phases
- Forms stable fibrin clot
- Cell-based model emphasizes role of TF-bearing cells and activated platelets
- Disorders cause **deep tissue bleeding** (hemarthrosis, muscle hematomas, delayed bleeding)

### Natural Anticoagulants
- **Protein C/Protein S:** Inactivate FVIIIa and FVa
- **Antithrombin:** Inhibits thrombin and other factors
- **TFPI:** Inhibits TF-FVIIa complex
- Prevent excessive clotting at injury site

### Fibrinolysis
- Plasminogen → Plasmin (activated by tPA)
- Plasmin degrades fibrin → D-dimer
- Regulated by PAI-1, PAI-2, and plasmin inhibitors
- Allows tissue repair and prevents pathologic thrombosis

### Laboratory Testing Strategy
1. **Start with screening tests:** PT, aPTT, platelet count
2. **Peripheral smear** for morphology
3. **Mixing studies** if PT/aPTT prolonged
4. **Specific factor levels** based on pattern
5. **Platelet function testing** if mucosal bleeding with normal counts
6. **VWF testing** for suspected VWD
7. Consider **acquired causes**: medications, liver disease, vitamin K deficiency, DIC

### Important Clinical Distinctions
- **Hemophilia vs VWD:** Both can prolong aPTT, but VWD has abnormal VWF testing and mucosal bleeding
- **Factor deficiency vs inhibitor:** Mixing study differentiates
- **Lupus anticoagulant vs specific inhibitor:** LAC causes thrombosis, not bleeding
- **Congenital vs acquired:** Age of onset, family history, pattern of factor deficiencies
- **Developmental hemostasis:** Age-appropriate reference ranges essential for infants
