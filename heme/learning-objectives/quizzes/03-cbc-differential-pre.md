## Answer Key

### Q1. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. Complete Blood Chemistry is not a standard medical abbreviation. Electrolyte and metabolic panel values are measured by a basic or comprehensive metabolic panel (BMP/CMP), not a CBC.
- **B)** Correct. CBC stands for Complete Blood Count. It is a screening test that provides information about the cellular components of peripheral blood, including red blood cells (count, hemoglobin, hematocrit, indices), white blood cells (count and differential), and platelets.
- **C)** Incorrect. Blood cultures are separate tests used to identify bacterial or fungal pathogens in the bloodstream. The CBC does not involve culture of organisms; it counts and characterizes blood cells.
- **D)** Incorrect. Bone marrow cellularity and architecture are evaluated by bone marrow biopsy and aspirate, not by a CBC. The CBC examines peripheral blood, not bone marrow tissue.

### Q2. Answer: C

- **A)** Incorrect. The concentration of hemoglobin in grams per deciliter describes the hemoglobin concentration (Hgb), not the hematocrit. These are related but distinct parameters.
- **B)** Incorrect. The total number of red blood cells per microliter describes the red blood cell count (RBC count), not the hematocrit. The hematocrit is a volume-based measurement, not a cell count.
- **C)** Correct. Hematocrit (Hct) is defined as the volume of whole blood occupied by red blood cells, expressed as a percentage. It is measured as packed cell volume after centrifugation. Normal values are approximately 42-52% for adult males and 36-46% for adult females.
- **D)** Incorrect. The average volume of an individual erythrocyte measured in femtoliters describes the mean cell volume (MCV), not the hematocrit. MCV measures individual cell size, while hematocrit measures the aggregate volume of all red cells in a blood sample.

### Q3. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. Anisocytosis refers to variation in red blood cell size, not shape. It is reflected by an elevated red cell distribution width (RDW) on the CBC.
- **B)** Correct. Poikilocytosis is the term for variation in red blood cell shape on a peripheral blood smear. Specific poikilocytes include spherocytes, elliptocytes, sickle cells, schistocytes, acanthocytes, target cells, teardrop cells, bite cells, and others, each with specific clinical associations.
- **C)** Incorrect. Polychromasia refers to a bluish tinge of red blood cells on Wright-Giemsa staining, indicating the presence of residual RNA in reticulocytes. It reflects increased reticulocyte release, not variation in cell shape.
- **D)** Incorrect. Rouleaux formation describes red blood cells stacking like coins, which occurs when plasma protein levels are elevated (such as in multiple myeloma or inflammatory states). It is not the term for variation in cell shape.

### Q4. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. The total volume of red blood cells in one liter of blood relates to hematocrit, not MCV. MCV is an average per-cell measurement, not a total volume measurement.
- **B)** Correct. Mean cell volume (MCV) is the average volume of individual erythrocytes, measured in femtoliters (fL). Normal MCV is 80-100 fL. It is used to classify anemias as microcytic (MCV less than 80 fL), normocytic (80-100 fL), or macrocytic (greater than 100 fL).
- **C)** Incorrect. The average weight of hemoglobin per red blood cell describes the mean cell hemoglobin (MCH), measured in picograms. MCH and MCV are different red cell indices.
- **D)** Incorrect. The coefficient of variation in red blood cell size describes the red cell distribution width (RDW), not MCV. RDW measures variation in cell size, while MCV measures average cell size.

### Q5. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. An MCV of 60-80 fL would include both abnormally low values and the lower end of microcytic range. The standard microcytic threshold is below 80 fL, and values in the 60s are clearly microcytic.
- **B)** Correct. The normal MCV range is 80-100 fL, defining normocytic red blood cells. An MCV below 80 fL is microcytic (suggesting problems with hemoglobin production such as iron deficiency or thalassemia), and an MCV above 100 fL is macrocytic (suggesting megaloblastic anemia, liver disease, or other causes).
- **C)** Incorrect. An MCV of 100-120 fL falls in the macrocytic range. Values above 100 fL indicate larger-than-normal red blood cells, which can be seen in megaloblastic anemia (B12/folate deficiency), liver disease, alcohol use, and other conditions.
- **D)** Incorrect. An MCV of 120-140 fL would represent severely macrocytic red blood cells. Such extreme macrocytosis is uncommon and typically suggests severe megaloblastic anemia from profound B12 or folate deficiency.

### Q6. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. The average diameter of red blood cells is not directly measured by the RDW. Red cell diameter is assessed morphologically on a blood smear (normal is approximately 7.5 micrometers), while RDW is a statistical measure of size variation.
- **B)** Correct. The red cell distribution width (RDW) measures the variation in red blood cell size, also known as anisocytosis. It is expressed as a coefficient of variation of the MCV (RDW-CV), with a normal range of 12-14.6%. An elevated RDW indicates a heterogeneous population of red cells of varying sizes.
- **C)** Incorrect. The ratio of reticulocytes to mature erythrocytes is the reticulocyte percentage, not the RDW. The reticulocyte count assesses bone marrow erythroid response, while RDW measures size variation among all circulating red cells.
- **D)** Incorrect. The concentration of hemoglobin per unit volume of red blood cells describes the mean cell hemoglobin concentration (MCHC), not the RDW. MCHC measures hemoglobin density, while RDW measures size variation.

### Q7. Answer: C

- **A)** Incorrect. Lymphocytes are the second most abundant white blood cell in healthy adults (normal absolute count: 1.0-4.8 x 10^3/uL). However, lymphocytes are the most numerous leukocyte in young children.
- **B)** Incorrect. Monocytes are the third most abundant circulating leukocyte (normal absolute count: 0.1-0.8 x 10^3/uL). They differentiate into macrophages and dendritic cells upon entering tissues.
- **C)** Correct. Neutrophils are the most abundant white blood cell in the peripheral blood of healthy adults, with a normal absolute count of 1.5-5.0 x 10^3/uL. They are innate immune cells that serve as first responders to bacterial infections through phagocytosis.
- **D)** Incorrect. Eosinophils are relatively rare in peripheral blood (normal absolute count: 0-0.4 x 10^3/uL). They are involved in parasitic infections and allergic responses.

### Q8. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. The primary phagocytes that respond first to bacterial infections are neutrophils, not eosinophils. Neutrophils are the most abundant granulocyte and serve as the first line of innate immune defense against bacteria.
- **B)** Correct. Eosinophils are granulocytes involved in responses to parasitic infections, allergic reactions, and inflammatory states. They are regulated by IL-5 and contain granules with enzymes (including aryl sulfatase) that can combat parasites and modulate allergic responses.
- **C)** Incorrect. Antibody production is the function of plasma cells (differentiated B-lymphocytes), not eosinophils. Eosinophils are innate immune cells of the granulocyte lineage.
- **D)** Incorrect. Release of histamine and heparin in immediate hypersensitivity reactions is the function of basophils and mast cells, not eosinophils. While eosinophils are involved in allergic responses, their role is distinct from that of basophils.

### Q9. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. Automated instruments readily calculate both percentages and absolute counts. The preference for absolute counts is based on clinical accuracy, not on difficulty of calculation.
- **B)** Correct. Absolute counts (calculated as WBC count multiplied by the percentage of each cell type) provide the true number of each leukocyte subtype per unit volume. Percentages can be misleading because an increase in one cell type will cause the relative percentage of other types to decrease, even if their absolute numbers are unchanged. For example, a patient with lymphocytosis may appear to have a low neutrophil percentage when the absolute neutrophil count is actually normal.
- **C)** Incorrect. Both manual and automated differentials generate percentages, and both can be converted to absolute counts. Percentages are standard output from both methods; the clinical recommendation is to convert to absolute counts regardless of the method used.
- **D)** Incorrect. The preference for absolute counts is clinically driven, not merely a regulatory requirement. Absolute counts provide meaningful clinical information that percentages alone cannot, such as correctly identifying neutropenia or lymphocytosis.

### Q10. Answer: C

- **A)** Incorrect. Thrombocytosis refers to an abnormally high platelet count (greater than 400 x 10^9/L), which is the opposite of thrombocytopenia. It can be reactive (from inflammation) or neoplastic.
- **B)** Incorrect. Leukopenia refers to an abnormally low white blood cell count, not a low platelet count. Leuko- refers to white blood cells, while thrombo- refers to platelets.
- **C)** Correct. Thrombocytopenia is the term for an abnormally low platelet count, defined as below 150 x 10^9/L. It can result from decreased production, increased destruction, or sequestration (such as in the spleen).
- **D)** Incorrect. Polycythemia refers to an abnormally high red blood cell count, hemoglobin, and/or hematocrit. It is a disorder of the erythroid lineage, not the platelet lineage.
