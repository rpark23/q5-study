## Answer Key

### Q1. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. A chronic proliferation of mature lymphocytes describes chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL), not AML. AML involves myeloid precursor cells (blasts), not mature lymphocytes, and is an acute rather than chronic process.
- **B)** Correct. AML is defined as a clonal proliferation of myeloid precursor cells (blasts) that have a reduced capacity to differentiate into mature cells. The WHO defines AML by the presence of 20% or more blasts in the bone marrow or blood, or by the presence of certain characteristic chromosomal abnormalities such as t(8;21), inv(16), or t(15;17) regardless of blast percentage.
- **C)** Incorrect. A reactive increase in mature neutrophils describes neutrophilia or a leukemoid reaction, which are non-neoplastic processes. AML is a malignant condition characterized by immature blasts, not mature neutrophils.
- **D)** Incorrect. A disorder of red blood cell production causing isolated anemia without blast increase could describe pure red cell aplasia or certain anemias, but not AML. AML involves myeloid blast proliferation and typically causes pancytopenia, not isolated anemia.

### Q2. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. A malignant neoplasm of mature B-lymphocytes with lymphocytosis and hypogammaglobulinemia describes chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL), not MDS. MDS is a myeloid disorder, not a lymphoid one.
- **B)** Correct. MDS is an acquired clonal stem cell disorder resulting in bone marrow failure characterized by ineffective hematopoiesis (the bone marrow produces cells that are morphologically abnormal and undergo premature apoptosis). This leads to cytopenias despite a typically hypercellular marrow. MDS carries an overall approximately 30% risk of progression to AML.
- **C)** Incorrect. A myeloproliferative disorder with effective hematopoiesis and elevated blood counts describes myeloproliferative neoplasms (MPNs) such as polycythemia vera or essential thrombocythemia. MDS is characterized by ineffective (not effective) hematopoiesis, leading to low (not high) blood counts.
- **D)** Incorrect. A congenital disorder of neutrophil function describes conditions such as chronic granulomatous disease or leukocyte adhesion deficiency. MDS is an acquired (not congenital) disorder of hematopoietic stem cells, not a functional defect of mature neutrophils.

### Q3. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. "Chronic Hematologic Immunodeficiency Pathway" is not a recognized term. CHIP describes a pre-malignant clonal state in hematopoietic cells, not an immunodeficiency pathway.
- **B)** Correct. CHIP stands for Clonal Hematopoiesis of Indeterminate Potential. It refers to the detection of somatic mutations in hematopoietic cells (such as DNMT3A, TET2, or ASXL1) in individuals who have no cytopenias, dysplasia, or hematologic malignancy. CHIP represents the earliest detectable step in the progression toward MDS and AML.
- **C)** Incorrect. "Complete Hematologic Induction Protocol" is not a standard abbreviation. CHIP refers to a pre-malignant clonal state, not a treatment protocol.
- **D)** Incorrect. "Cytogenetic Hyperdiploid Intermediate Prognosis" is not a recognized abbreviation. Hyperdiploidy is a cytogenetic finding in ALL, and CHIP is a distinct concept related to clonal evolution in myeloid disease.

### Q4. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. Acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL) is the most common malignancy in children, not in adults. In adults, AML is far more common than ALL.
- **B)** Correct. AML is the most common acute leukemia in adults, with approximately 25,000 new cases annually in the United States. Its incidence increases dramatically with age, with a median age at presentation of approximately 70 years.
- **C)** Incorrect. Chronic myeloid leukemia (CML) is a chronic (not acute) leukemia. While it is a myeloid malignancy, it is characterized by a slow accumulation of mature myeloid cells rather than an acute blast proliferation.
- **D)** Incorrect. Chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL) is the most common leukemia overall in the Western world, but it is a chronic lymphoid malignancy, not an acute leukemia. When the question specifies "acute leukemia," AML is the most common in adults.

### Q5. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. A progression rate of less than 5% would significantly underestimate the risk. MDS has a substantial risk of transformation to AML, making it a clinically important consideration in management decisions.
- **B)** Correct. Approximately 30% of MDS cases progress to AML over time. This progression occurs through the stepwise accumulation of additional somatic mutations that shift the balance from ineffective hematopoiesis toward uncontrolled blast proliferation. The risk varies based on the MDS subtype and risk score (R-IPSS).
- **C)** Incorrect. A 70% progression rate overestimates the overall risk. While higher-risk MDS categories have greater transformation rates, the overall rate across all MDS subtypes is approximately 30%.
- **D)** Incorrect. A progression rate greater than 90% greatly overestimates the risk. Many patients with lower-risk MDS live for years without transforming to AML, and some die of other causes before transformation occurs.

### Q6. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. While alkylating agents do suppress the immune system, the mechanism linking them to AML is direct DNA damage in hematopoietic stem cells, not viral reactivation. Therapy-related AML is a well-characterized consequence of DNA-damaging chemotherapy agents.
- **B)** Correct. Alkylating agents cause direct DNA damage (crosslinks, strand breaks, and base modifications) in hematopoietic stem cells. This DNA damage can lead to secondary mutations, chromosomal deletions (particularly of chromosomes 5 and 7), and complex karyotypes that drive clonal evolution toward MDS and AML. Therapy-related AML typically develops 5-7 years after alkylating agent exposure.
- **C)** Incorrect. Iron depletion and compensatory marrow hyperplasia is not the mechanism of therapy-related AML. Alkylating agents cause direct genotoxic damage, not iron depletion, and the leukemogenic mechanism is clonal mutation accumulation rather than compensatory proliferation.
- **D)** Incorrect. Alkylating agents do not cause splenomegaly or splenic trapping of hematopoietic cells as a mechanism of leukemogenesis. The risk of AML from alkylating agents stems from their ability to directly damage the DNA of hematopoietic stem cells.

### Q7. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. Simultaneous elevation of all three blood cell lines describes polycythemia vera or other myeloproliferative conditions with panmyelosis. Pancytopenia is the opposite: it describes simultaneous reduction of all cell lines.
- **B)** Correct. Pancytopenia refers to the simultaneous reduction in red blood cells (anemia), white blood cells (leukopenia), and platelets (thrombocytopenia). In AML and MDS, pancytopenia results from bone marrow failure caused by the crowding out of normal hematopoietic cells by malignant blasts (AML) or by ineffective hematopoiesis with premature apoptosis of dysplastic precursors (MDS).
- **C)** Incorrect. A selective reduction in only the platelet count is called isolated thrombocytopenia. Pancytopenia specifically requires all three cell lines to be decreased, not just one.
- **D)** Incorrect. Elevation of one cell line with reduction of the others does not describe pancytopenia. Pancytopenia requires all three lineages (red cells, white cells, and platelets) to be reduced simultaneously.

### Q8. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. Constitutive tyrosine kinase activation describes the BCR::ABL1 fusion protein in CML, not PML-RARalpha. The PML-RARalpha fusion acts primarily as a transcriptional repressor that blocks differentiation, not as an activating kinase.
- **B)** Correct. The PML-RARalpha fusion protein, produced by the t(15;17) translocation, acts as a dominant-negative repressor of retinoic acid signaling. It blocks the normal retinoic acid-induced transcriptional program required for myeloid differentiation, arresting cells at the promyelocyte stage. This is why pharmacologic doses of all-trans retinoic acid (ATRA) can overcome this block and force differentiation of the leukemic promyelocytes.
- **C)** Incorrect. The PML-RARalpha fusion does not inhibit BCL-2. In fact, it promotes survival of the abnormal promyelocytes rather than causing cell death. BCL-2 inhibition is the mechanism of venetoclax, a drug used in AML treatment.
- **D)** Incorrect. JAK-STAT pathway activation is the mechanism underlying myeloproliferative neoplasms (driven by JAK2 V617F or CALR mutations), not APL. The PML-RARalpha fusion acts through aberrant transcriptional repression of differentiation genes.

### Q9. Answer: C

- **A)** Incorrect. A complex karyotype (three or more cytogenetic abnormalities) is classified as adverse (poor) risk in AML. Patients with complex karyotypes have significantly worse outcomes and often require allogeneic stem cell transplant.
- **B)** Incorrect. Deletions of chromosomes 5 and 7 are classified as adverse (poor) risk, particularly when seen in therapy-related AML. These abnormalities are associated with lower remission rates and shorter overall survival.
- **C)** Correct. Core-binding factor (CBF) translocations, including t(8;21) and inv(16)/t(16;16), are classified as favorable risk in AML. Patients with these translocations have higher rates of complete remission and better overall survival. They often respond well to high-dose cytarabine consolidation and generally do not require allogeneic transplant in first remission.
- **D)** Incorrect. 11q23 (MLL/KMT2A) rearrangements are generally classified as intermediate to adverse risk in AML, depending on the specific fusion partner. They are not considered favorable risk and often portend a more aggressive clinical course.

### Q10. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. The arrest of leukemia cell growth following chemotherapy describes the therapeutic goal of induction, not leukostasis. Leukostasis is a complication of the disease itself, occurring when blast counts are extremely high.
- **B)** Correct. Leukostasis is a medical emergency in which large, sticky leukemic blasts accumulate in the microvasculature, causing sludging and impaired blood flow. This leads to end-organ damage, particularly in the lungs (dyspnea, hypoxia, pulmonary infiltrates) and brain (headaches, confusion, vision changes). It is most common in AML when WBC exceeds 50,000-100,000/uL and carries a 20-40% seven-day mortality.
- **C)** Incorrect. Trapping of normal leukocytes in the spleen due to splenomegaly describes hypersplenism or sequestration, which is a different pathologic process. Leukostasis involves the sludging of malignant blasts in the microvasculature, not splenic trapping of normal cells.
- **D)** Incorrect. Adhesion of leukemic cells to bone marrow stroma is a feature of leukemic cell biology but is not what leukostasis refers to. Leukostasis specifically describes microvascular sludging in peripheral organs like the lungs and brain.
