## Answer Key

### Q1. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. While CMV can cause a mononucleosis-like syndrome, classical infectious mononucleosis is caused by EBV. Additionally, EBV infects B-lymphocytes (not T-lymphocytes), and CMV infects monocytes and other cell types.
- **B)** Correct. Infectious mononucleosis is caused by primary infection with Epstein-Barr virus (EBV), a herpesvirus that infects B-lymphocytes. EBV infects over 90% of adults worldwide, and the virus persists for life in resting memory B-cells. Primary infection in adolescents and young adults commonly presents as the clinical syndrome of infectious mononucleosis.
- **C)** Incorrect. Staphylococcus aureus can cause pharyngitis and tonsillitis, but bacterial pharyngitis (more commonly caused by Group A Streptococcus) is a distinct entity from infectious mononucleosis. Mono is caused by a virus (EBV), not a bacterium.
- **D)** Incorrect. Herpes simplex virus reactivation causes cold sores or genital herpes, not infectious mononucleosis. While HSV and EBV are both herpesviruses, they cause different clinical syndromes through different mechanisms.

### Q2. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. Rash, arthralgia, and hepatomegaly are not the classic triad of mono. While rash can occur in mononucleosis (particularly if amoxicillin is given), it is not part of the defining triad. This combination is more suggestive of systemic lupus erythematosus or other autoimmune conditions.
- **B)** Correct. The classic clinical triad of infectious mononucleosis is fever, pharyngitis (often with white tonsillar exudate), and cervical lymphadenopathy. Additional findings include fatigue, myalgia, splenomegaly (50-75% of patients), and elevated liver enzymes (ALT elevated in approximately 75%).
- **C)** Incorrect. Cough, night sweats, and weight loss are classic "B symptoms" associated with lymphoma or tuberculosis, not the typical presentation of infectious mononucleosis. While patients with mono may have fatigue, the triad is fever, pharyngitis, and cervical adenopathy.
- **D)** Incorrect. Headache, neck stiffness, and photophobia describe the classic triad of meningitis, not infectious mononucleosis. Although EBV can rarely cause meningitis, this is not the typical clinical presentation.

### Q3. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. ALL is diagnosed through bone marrow biopsy, flow cytometry, and cytogenetics, not a heterophile antibody test. The monospot test detects antibodies associated with EBV infection, which is a reactive (not malignant) condition.
- **B)** Correct. The heterophile antibody test (monospot) detects heterophile antibodies that agglutinate animal red blood cells and is approximately 90% sensitive and specific for infectious mononucleosis caused by EBV. It is a rapid, point-of-care test commonly used in the clinical setting for initial diagnosis.
- **C)** Incorrect. CLL is diagnosed by flow cytometry showing a clonal population of mature B-cells expressing CD19, CD20, CD23, and CD5. The monospot test has no role in CLL diagnosis.
- **D)** Incorrect. CMV infection is diagnosed through CMV-specific serology (IgM/IgG), PCR for CMV DNA, or culture. The monospot test is specific for EBV-associated mononucleosis and is negative in CMV infection.

### Q4. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. An indolent malignancy of mature B-lymphocytes describes chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL), not ALL. ALL is an acute, rapidly progressive malignancy of immature lymphoid precursors that is fatal within weeks to months without treatment.
- **B)** Correct. Acute lymphoblastic leukemia is a malignant neoplasm of B-cell or T-cell lineage progenitor cells (lymphoblasts) that rapidly proliferates in the bone marrow, crowding out normal hematopoiesis. It is the most common malignancy of children and is rapidly fatal without immediate treatment. With modern multi-agent chemotherapy, approximately 90% of children and 30-60% of adults can be cured.
- **C)** Incorrect. A chronic myeloid disorder driven by BCR::ABL1 describes chronic myeloid leukemia (CML), which is a myeloproliferative neoplasm of the myeloid lineage. ALL can be Philadelphia chromosome-positive but is fundamentally a lymphoid (not myeloid) malignancy.
- **D)** Incorrect. A reactive expansion of normal lymphocytes describes reactive lymphocytosis (as seen in infectious mononucleosis), not ALL. In ALL, the proliferating cells are malignant, immature lymphoblasts, not reactive mature lymphocytes.

### Q5. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. ALL is rare in newborns. While infant ALL (under 1 year) does occur and carries a particularly poor prognosis, the peak incidence of ALL is in children ages 1-5 years, not in the neonatal period.
- **B)** Correct. ALL has a peak incidence between ages 1 and 5 years, with a median age at diagnosis of 17 years. It is the most common malignancy of childhood. Importantly, while ALL can occur in adults, it is less common and carries a worse prognosis in older age groups.
- **C)** Incorrect. While ALL can occur in adults ages 40-60, this is not the peak incidence age group. AML becomes more common than ALL in adults, and the incidence of ALL is highest in early childhood.
- **D)** Incorrect. ALL in elderly adults over 75 is uncommon and carries the worst prognosis of any age group. The peak incidence is in young children, and adults over 65 have the highest mortality when ALL does occur.

### Q6. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. TdT is not expressed by mature B-lymphocytes. Mature B-cells have completed their development and no longer express this enzyme. TdT expression is restricted to immature lymphoid precursors during early development.
- **B)** Correct. TdT (terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase) is a DNA polymerase expressed in immature lymphoid progenitor cells during early B-cell and T-cell development. It is characteristically positive in acute lymphoblastic leukemia (both B-ALL and T-ALL) and is a key marker used in flow cytometry to identify lymphoblasts and distinguish ALL from mature lymphoid malignancies like CLL or Burkitt lymphoma, which are TdT-negative.
- **C)** Incorrect. TdT is not specific to myeloid blasts. Myeloid blasts are identified by markers such as CD13, CD33, CD117, and myeloperoxidase (MPO). TdT is a lymphoid precursor marker, though rare cases of AML can show aberrant TdT expression.
- **D)** Incorrect. TdT is not a marker of activated T-cells. Reactive T-cells (such as the atypical lymphocytes in mononucleosis) are mature cells that do not express TdT. TdT positivity indicates immaturity, which is a feature of malignant blasts, not reactive lymphocytes.

### Q7. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. Chronic wasting from bone marrow failure describes the general course of untreated leukemia, not tumor lysis syndrome specifically. Tumor lysis syndrome is an acute metabolic emergency, not a chronic process.
- **B)** Correct. Tumor lysis syndrome (TLS) occurs when rapid destruction of tumor cells (either spontaneously or following chemotherapy) releases intracellular contents into the bloodstream, causing hyperuricemia (from nucleic acid breakdown), hyperkalemia (from cellular potassium release), hyperphosphatemia (from intracellular phosphate release), and secondary hypocalcemia (from calcium-phosphate precipitation). This can lead to acute renal failure, cardiac arrhythmias, seizures, and sudden death.
- **C)** Incorrect. An autoimmune condition attacking both tumor and normal tissues does not describe tumor lysis syndrome. While autoimmune phenomena can occur in certain hematologic malignancies (e.g., autoimmune hemolytic anemia in CLL), TLS is a metabolic emergency from cell breakdown, not an autoimmune process.
- **D)** Incorrect. Localized tissue necrosis at a solid tumor site describes tumor necrosis, not tumor lysis syndrome. TLS is a systemic metabolic disturbance caused by the release of intracellular contents from dying tumor cells into the bloodstream, not a localized phenomenon.

### Q8. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. A malignant neoplasm of immature lymphoid progenitor cells that rapidly replaces the bone marrow describes acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL), not CLL. CLL involves mature (not immature) B-lymphocytes and has an indolent (not rapidly progressive) course.
- **B)** Correct. CLL is a malignant neoplasm of mature B-lymphocytes that accumulate in the blood, bone marrow, lymph nodes, and spleen. The diagnosis requires the presence of 5,000/uL or more clonal B-lymphocytes in the peripheral blood. It is the most common leukemia in the Western world and is generally incurable, though many patients have an indolent course with median 5-year survival of 88.5%.
- **C)** Incorrect. A clonal myeloid disorder with constitutive tyrosine kinase activation from the Philadelphia chromosome describes chronic myeloid leukemia (CML), not CLL. CLL is a lymphoid disorder without the BCR::ABL1 fusion.
- **D)** Incorrect. CLL is a clonal neoplasm, not a reactive lymphocytosis. While chronic viral infections can cause reactive lymphocytosis, CLL is a malignant condition with clonal B-cells that cannot be cured with antiviral therapy.

### Q9. Answer: D

- **A)** Incorrect. AML is the most common acute leukemia in adults, but it is not the most common leukemia overall. CLL is more common when both acute and chronic leukemias are considered together.
- **B)** Incorrect. ALL is the most common malignancy in children but is relatively uncommon in adults. It is not the most common adult leukemia.
- **C)** Incorrect. CML is a chronic myeloid malignancy with an incidence of approximately 1-2 per 100,000, which is less common than CLL in the Western world.
- **D)** Correct. Chronic lymphocytic leukemia is the most common leukemia in the Western world, accounting for approximately 30% of adult leukemias and 25% of all leukemias in North America. It has a median age at diagnosis of 71.5 years and an incidence of approximately 5 per 100,000.

### Q10. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. TdT is a marker of immature lymphoid precursors and is positive in ALL blasts, not in CLL. CLL cells are mature B-lymphocytes that do not express TdT. If TdT were positive, the diagnosis would be reconsidered in favor of ALL.
- **B)** Correct. CD5 is normally expressed by T-lymphocytes, but it is aberrantly co-expressed by CLL B-cells along with B-cell markers (CD19, CD20, CD23). This unusual CD5-positive B-cell phenotype is a hallmark of CLL and is critical for distinguishing it from other B-cell neoplasms that are typically CD5-negative (such as follicular lymphoma or marginal zone lymphoma).
- **C)** Incorrect. Myeloperoxidase (MPO) is a marker of the myeloid lineage and is used to identify AML blasts. CLL is a B-cell lymphoid malignancy and does not express MPO.
- **D)** Incorrect. CD34 is a marker of hematopoietic stem cells and immature progenitors. CLL cells are mature B-lymphocytes and do not express CD34. CD34 positivity would suggest a more immature process such as AML or ALL.
