## Answer Key

### Q1. Answer: A

- **A)** Correct. This is a fundamental conceptual distinction in hematology. MPNs produce too many functional (though clonal) blood cells -- effective hematopoiesis leads to elevated counts (erythrocytosis in PV, thrombocytosis in ET, leukocytosis in CML) with complications like thrombosis, hemorrhage, and hyperviscosity. MDS produces dysfunctional cells that undergo premature apoptosis in the marrow (ineffective hematopoiesis), leading to cytopenias despite a cellular or hypercellular marrow. Both are clonal stem cell disorders, and both can transform to AML.
- **B)** Incorrect. MPNs do not arise from lymphoid progenitors. Both MPNs and MDS are clonal disorders arising from hematopoietic stem cells of the myeloid lineage. The driver mutations (JAK2, CALR, MPL in MPNs; SF3B1, TET2, DNMT3A, etc. in MDS) affect multipotent hematopoietic stem cells, not lineage-committed lymphoid progenitors.
- **C)** Incorrect. Both MPNs and MDS are clonal disorders. MPNs are driven by specific somatic driver mutations (JAK2 V617F, CALR, MPL, BCR::ABL1) that cause clonal expansion of hematopoietic stem cells. They are not reactive proliferations. The clonal nature of MPNs is fundamental to their identity as neoplasms.
- **D)** Incorrect. MPNs can transform to acute leukemia, and this is a major concern in their management. PV and ET have a small but real risk of transformation to AML (especially post-MPN myelofibrosis), PMF has a 10-20% risk of leukemic transformation, and CML progresses to blast crisis if untreated. Both MPNs and MDS carry leukemic transformation risk.

### Q2. Answer: C

- **A)** Incorrect. BCR::ABL1 is the defining fusion of chronic myelogenous leukemia (CML), not a driver of ET or PMF. In fact, the diagnosis of ET or PMF requires that BCR::ABL1 be excluded, as CML can sometimes mimic these diseases. BCR::ABL1 testing is a mandatory part of the MPN diagnostic workup.
- **B)** Incorrect. FLT3-ITD is a mutation primarily associated with acute myeloid leukemia (AML), not with myeloproliferative neoplasms. It is one of the most common mutations in AML and is important for AML risk stratification and treatment, but it does not serve as a driver in ET or PMF.
- **C)** Correct. CALR (calreticulin) mutations are the second most common driver mutation in ET (~25% of cases) and PMF (~25-30% of cases), found in a large proportion of JAK2-negative cases. MPL (thrombopoietin receptor) mutations account for a smaller additional fraction (~5%). The three main MPN drivers are JAK2 V617F, CALR, and MPL -- and rare "triple-negative" cases exist that lack all three.
- **D)** Incorrect. NPM1 (nucleophosmin 1) mutations are among the most common mutations in AML, where they serve as a favorable prognostic marker. NPM1 is not a driver mutation in myeloproliferative neoplasms and plays no role in the pathogenesis of ET or PMF.

### Q3. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. CALR is not a kinase and does not directly phosphorylate JAK2. The mechanism involves CALR acting as a chaperone protein that, when mutated, gains an abnormal ability to bind and activate the MPL receptor on the cell surface, leading to downstream JAK-STAT activation.
- **B)** Correct. Mutant calreticulin acquires a novel C-terminus (due to frameshift mutations in exon 9) that allows it to bind and constitutively activate the MPL (thrombopoietin) receptor on the cell surface. This leads to ligand-independent (TPO-independent) activation of JAK-STAT signaling, driving megakaryocyte proliferation and thrombocytosis. This explains why CALR-mutant ET closely phenocopies JAK2-mutant ET -- both converge on the same JAK-STAT signaling pathway.
- **C)** Incorrect. While wild-type calreticulin does play a role in calcium homeostasis in the endoplasmic reticulum, the pathogenic mechanism of CALR mutations in MPNs is not related to calcium signaling. The disease-driving mechanism is the abnormal binding and activation of the MPL receptor by the mutant protein's novel C-terminus.
- **D)** Incorrect. CALR mutations do not affect platelet clearance in the spleen. The thrombocytosis in CALR-mutant ET is caused by increased platelet production due to constitutive activation of MPL-JAK-STAT signaling in megakaryocytes, not by decreased platelet destruction or clearance.

### Q4. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. The PML-RARalpha fusion protein is produced by the t(15;17) translocation, which is the defining molecular lesion of acute promyelocytic leukemia (APL), not CML. PML-RARalpha blocks differentiation at the promyelocyte stage, whereas CML is characterized by overproduction of maturing myeloid cells.
- **B)** Correct. The Philadelphia chromosome t(9;22) creates the BCR::ABL1 fusion gene, which encodes a constitutively active tyrosine kinase. This drives unregulated myeloid proliferation and is the defining molecular lesion of CML. The discovery of BCR::ABL1 led to the development of imatinib and other tyrosine kinase inhibitors, which have transformed CML from a fatal disease into a chronic, manageable condition.
- **C)** Incorrect. CALR mutations are not created by a translocation. CALR mutations are insertions/deletions in exon 9 of the calreticulin gene and are driver mutations in JAK2-negative essential thrombocythemia and primary myelofibrosis. They are mechanistically and genetically distinct from the BCR::ABL1 fusion.
- **D)** Incorrect. The t(9;22) does not result in loss of JAK2. Rather, it fuses the BCR gene on chromosome 22 with the ABL1 gene on chromosome 9, creating a novel fusion kinase. JAK2 is located on chromosome 9p24 and is not affected by this translocation. Furthermore, JAK2 is not a negative regulator of erythropoiesis -- it is a positive signal transducer.

### Q5. Answer: C

- **A)** Incorrect. A patient with 35% blasts, new cytogenetic abnormalities, and worsening cytopenias is not in chronic phase. Chronic phase CML is characterized by a controlled disease with <10% blasts, stable blood counts (albeit elevated), and response to TKI therapy. This presentation represents a dramatic and dangerous transformation.
- **B)** Incorrect. While the accelerated phase of CML does represent disease progression, it is defined by 10-19% blasts, increasing basophilia, or additional cytogenetic abnormalities. With 35% blasts, this patient has progressed beyond the accelerated phase into blast crisis, which has a much worse prognosis and different management implications.
- **C)** Correct. Blast crisis is defined by >=20% blasts (by WHO criteria) or >=30% blasts (by older ELN criteria) in the blood or bone marrow, and represents transformation of CML into an acute leukemia (which can be myeloid or lymphoid in phenotype). It carries a very poor prognosis with median survival measured in months, even with aggressive therapy including TKIs and chemotherapy. The new cytogenetic abnormalities (clonal evolution) further confirm disease progression.
- **D)** Incorrect. Myelofibrotic transformation can occur in CML but would present with increasing marrow fibrosis, teardrop cells, and a leukoerythroblastic picture, not with 35% blasts. The high blast percentage definitively places this in blast crisis. Ruxolitinib targets JAK2, not BCR::ABL1, and would not be appropriate primary therapy for CML.

### Q6. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. The JAK2 V617F mutation does not directly affect EPO gene transcription in the kidney. JAK2 is a cytoplasmic tyrosine kinase that signals downstream of the EPO receptor on erythroid progenitors -- it does not act in renal peritubular cells where EPO is produced.
- **B)** Correct. In polycythemia vera, the JAK2 V617F mutation causes the EPO receptor to signal constitutively, driving red cell production independent of EPO. The resulting elevated red cell mass increases oxygen delivery to the kidneys, which suppresses EPO production through normal physiologic negative feedback (the kidney senses adequate oxygen and downregulates EPO). This is why a low EPO level is a major diagnostic clue for PV.
- **C)** Incorrect. PV does not destroy the EPO-producing renal peritubular interstitial cells. The kidneys are structurally normal in PV. The low EPO is a physiologic suppression response to the elevated red cell mass and improved tissue oxygenation, not a result of renal parenchymal damage.
- **D)** Incorrect. Platelets do not bind or sequester circulating EPO. EPO is a glycoprotein hormone that acts on EPO receptors on erythroid progenitors in the bone marrow. The thrombocytosis in PV is a separate consequence of the clonal proliferation driven by JAK2 V617F.

### Q7. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. CML is defined by the BCR::ABL1 fusion from t(9;22), and this patient is JAK2 V617F positive -- these are mutually exclusive driver mutations. CML can cause splenomegaly and occasionally mild fibrosis, but the presentation with prominent constitutional symptoms, teardrop cells, leukoerythroblastic picture, and extensive reticulin fibrosis in a JAK2-positive patient is classic for primary myelofibrosis.
- **B)** Correct. Primary myelofibrosis is characterized by progressive bone marrow fibrosis (reticulin and collagen deposition by reactive stromal cells stimulated by megakaryocyte-derived cytokines like TGF-beta and PDGF). As the fibrotic marrow can no longer support hematopoiesis, compensatory extramedullary hematopoiesis develops primarily in the spleen and liver, causing massive splenomegaly. Teardrop cells (dacrocytes) form as red cells are squeezed through the fibrotic marrow, and the leukoerythroblastic picture (nucleated RBCs and immature granulocytes) reflects release of immature cells from stressed extramedullary sites.
- **C)** Incorrect. Essential thrombocythemia primarily presents with isolated thrombocytosis and does not typically cause massive splenomegaly, constitutional symptoms, teardrop cells, or extensive marrow fibrosis. While ET can rarely progress to a secondary myelofibrosis (post-ET myelofibrosis), the primary presentation described here is classic for primary myelofibrosis.
- **D)** Incorrect. Polycythemia vera is characterized primarily by elevated red cell mass (high hemoglobin and hematocrit), not by marrow fibrosis and teardrop cells. While PV can evolve into a "spent phase" with secondary myelofibrosis (post-PV myelofibrosis), the initial presentation described here -- with extensive fibrosis, constitutional symptoms, and leukoerythroblastic picture -- is classic for primary myelofibrosis.

### Q8. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. CML does not inevitably relapse when TKI therapy is discontinued. Multiple clinical trials (STIM, EURO-SKI, and others) have demonstrated that a significant proportion of patients with sustained deep molecular responses can successfully discontinue therapy and remain in treatment-free remission.
- **B)** Correct. Approximately 40-50% of CML patients who have achieved a sustained deep molecular response (BCR::ABL1 undetectable by sensitive PCR) for an extended period can successfully stop TKI therapy without molecular relapse. However, close molecular monitoring is essential -- typically monthly for the first year, then quarterly -- because approximately half of those who attempt TFR will have molecular recurrence and need to restart their TKI. Virtually all who relapse respond again upon TKI reinitiation.
- **C)** Incorrect. TFR is achievable with TKI therapy alone and does not require allogeneic stem cell transplant. While transplant was historically the only curative option for CML, TKIs have largely replaced it for most patients, and TFR represents a functional "cure" achieved entirely through oral medication.
- **D)** Incorrect. Molecular monitoring is critically important after TKI discontinuation -- it is arguably more important than ever. Patients must be monitored frequently (monthly initially, then quarterly) with quantitative PCR for BCR::ABL1 to detect any molecular recurrence early, so TKI therapy can be promptly restarted if needed.

### Q9. Answer: C

- **A)** Incorrect. Imatinib, even at higher doses, cannot overcome the T315I mutation. The T315I mutation is a "gatekeeper" mutation that introduces a bulky isoleucine residue in the ABL kinase domain, sterically preventing imatinib (and other first/second-generation TKIs) from binding to the ATP-binding pocket. Simply increasing the dose does not overcome this structural barrier.
- **B)** Incorrect. Nilotinib is a second-generation TKI that, like imatinib, binds the ATP-binding pocket of ABL1. It is more potent than imatinib against many resistance mutations but is ineffective against T315I for the same steric reason -- the bulky isoleucine residue blocks binding of all first- and second-generation TKIs.
- **C)** Correct. Ponatinib is a third-generation TKI specifically designed to overcome the T315I gatekeeper mutation. Its chemical structure includes a carbon-carbon triple bond linker that allows it to accommodate the bulky isoleucine residue at position 315. Asciminib (a STAMP inhibitor) is also effective against T315I because it binds a completely different site (the myristoyl pocket) rather than competing at the ATP-binding pocket.
- **D)** Incorrect. Hydroxyurea is a non-specific cytoreductive agent that inhibits ribonucleotide reductase and reduces white blood cell counts. While it can provide temporary disease control in CML, it does not target BCR::ABL1, does not induce molecular responses, and is not a definitive treatment for TKI-resistant CML.

### Q10. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. Asciminib does not bind the ATP-binding pocket of BCR::ABL1. Binding the ATP-binding pocket is the mechanism of all traditional TKIs (imatinib, dasatinib, nilotinib, bosutinib, ponatinib). Asciminib was specifically designed to work through a completely different mechanism to overcome resistance mutations that affect the ATP-binding site.
- **B)** Correct. Asciminib is a first-in-class STAMP (Specifically Targeting the ABL Myristoyl Pocket) inhibitor. Rather than competing at the ATP-binding site, it binds the myristoyl pocket of ABL1, allosterically locking the kinase in an inactive conformation. This distinct mechanism allows it to work against many ATP-site resistant mutations (including T315I when combined with other agents) and can be used in combination with ATP-site TKIs for synergistic inhibition.
- **C)** Incorrect. Asciminib is a small-molecule inhibitor, not a monoclonal antibody. It does not recruit the immune system to target BCR::ABL1-expressing cells. Its mechanism is direct allosteric inhibition of the ABL1 kinase through the myristoyl pocket.
- **D)** Incorrect. Asciminib directly targets the ABL1 kinase itself, not JAK2 signaling. JAK2 and BCR::ABL1 are distinct kinases involved in different diseases (JAK2 in MPNs like PV, ET, and PMF; BCR::ABL1 in CML). Asciminib has no activity against JAK2.

### Q11. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. While phlebotomy does cause iron deficiency over time (and this is actually considered a beneficial side effect, as it helps limit red cell production), the target hematocrit is not set at <50% and the rationale is not to prevent iron deficiency. The target is more stringent, based on thrombosis reduction data.
- **B)** Correct. The landmark CYTO-PV trial demonstrated that maintaining hematocrit <45% significantly reduced the rate of cardiovascular death and major thrombotic events compared to a less stringent target of 45-50%. This target of <45% applies to both men and women with PV. Phlebotomy combined with low-dose aspirin forms the foundation of PV management.
- **C)** Incorrect. While the normal female hematocrit reference range is approximately 36-44%, the phlebotomy target in PV is <45% for both sexes based on the CYTO-PV trial data, not on matching normal reference ranges. Targeting <40% would be unnecessarily aggressive and could cause symptomatic anemia.
- **D)** Incorrect. Symptoms and thrombotic complications of PV can develop well below a hematocrit of 55%. The CYTO-PV trial showed that even hematocrits in the 45-50% range conferred significantly increased thrombotic risk compared to <45%. A target of <55% would be dangerously permissive.

### Q12. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. The dose of ruxolitinib is not the explanation. Ruxolitinib effectively inhibits JAK-STAT signaling (as evidenced by the clinical improvement in symptoms and spleen size), but its mechanism of action addresses the downstream consequences of the malignant clone rather than the clone itself. Higher doses would not change this fundamental limitation.
- **B)** Correct. JAK inhibitors (ruxolitinib, fedratinib, pacritinib, momelotinib) effectively reduce cytokine-driven constitutional symptoms and splenomegaly by inhibiting JAK-STAT signaling. However, they do not eliminate the malignant clone and generally do not reverse established marrow fibrosis in most patients. The clinical benefit is real but largely palliative/symptomatic. Allogeneic stem cell transplant remains the only potentially curative therapy for myelofibrosis.
- **C)** Incorrect. Persistent marrow fibrosis after 6 months of ruxolitinib is expected and does not indicate AML transformation. AML transformation would be indicated by the presence of >=20% blasts in the blood or marrow, not by persistent fibrosis. Fibrosis reversal is rare with JAK inhibitors and takes years if it occurs at all.
- **D)** Incorrect. Ruxolitinib is effective regardless of the specific driver mutation (JAK2, CALR, or MPL) because it inhibits JAK1/JAK2 signaling, which is the common downstream pathway activated by all three driver mutations. The clinical response (symptom improvement and spleen reduction) confirms the drug is working; the persistent fibrosis is a known limitation.
