## Answer Key

### Q1. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. Phagocytosis of bacterial pathogens is primarily performed by neutrophils and macrophages, not erythrocytes. Red blood cells lack the organelles and surface receptors necessary for phagocytic activity.
- **B)** Correct. Erythrocytes are predominantly composed of hemoglobin, which binds oxygen in the lungs and delivers it to peripheral tissues. They also transport carbon dioxide back to the lungs for exhalation. This gas exchange function is the defining role of red blood cells.
- **C)** Incorrect. Antibody production is the function of plasma cells, which are terminally differentiated B-lymphocytes. Erythrocytes do not participate in adaptive immunity.
- **D)** Incorrect. Formation of the platelet plug (primary hemostasis) is the function of platelets (thrombocytes), not erythrocytes. Platelets are small anuclear cell fragments derived from megakaryocytes.

### Q2. Answer: C

- **A)** Incorrect. A lifespan of 7-10 days describes platelets (thrombocytes), not erythrocytes. Platelets are much shorter-lived than red blood cells.
- **B)** Incorrect. A time frame of 6-10 hours describes the approximate time neutrophils spend in the peripheral blood circulation. Erythrocytes circulate for a much longer period.
- **C)** Correct. Mature erythrocytes have a lifespan of approximately 120 days in the circulation. After this period, aged red blood cells are removed primarily by splenic macrophages. This lifespan is important for calculating red cell production rates and understanding anemia dynamics.
- **D)** Incorrect. A lifespan of 1-5 days describes the approximate time neutrophils spend in tissues after migrating out of the bloodstream. Erythrocytes remain in circulation for 120 days.

### Q3. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. Apoptosis of mature blood cells at the end of their lifespan is part of normal cell turnover, but this describes cell death and removal, not the generation of new blood cells. Hematopoiesis specifically refers to blood cell production.
- **B)** Correct. Hematopoiesis is defined as the generation of blood cells from precursor cells through the coordinated action of growth factors and a specialized bone marrow microenvironment. It encompasses the entire process from hematopoietic stem cells to mature circulating blood cells.
- **C)** Incorrect. Filtration of aged erythrocytes is a function of the spleen (specifically the red pulp). While this process is related to blood cell homeostasis, it describes cell removal rather than cell production.
- **D)** Incorrect. The clotting cascade is the process of secondary hemostasis involving coagulation factors. It is a function of plasma proteins and platelets, not a process of blood cell generation.

### Q4. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. This reverses the relationship. HSCs are multipotent with the ability to generate all blood cell lineages, while progenitor cells are progressively committed to specific lineages as they differentiate.
- **B)** Correct. The defining feature of hematopoietic stem cells is their capacity for self-renewal, meaning they can divide to produce identical copies of themselves while also generating differentiated progeny. Progenitor cells, although they can proliferate and differentiate, lack this self-renewal capability.
- **C)** Incorrect. HSCs reside primarily in the bone marrow, not the thymus. The thymus is the site of T-cell maturation, and while it receives precursor T-cells from the marrow, it does not house HSCs.
- **D)** Incorrect. HSCs are actually the smallest hematopoietic compartment, comprising approximately 20,000 cells. They are mostly quiescent, and the larger compartments consist of progenitor and maturing precursor cells.

### Q5. Answer: C

- **A)** Incorrect. The fetal liver is the chief site of hematopoiesis during fetal development (from about 5 weeks through birth), but it is not the primary site in a healthy adult. After birth, the bone marrow takes over this role.
- **B)** Incorrect. The yolk sac is the site of primitive hematopoiesis very early in embryonic development (approximately 18 days post-fertilization). It has no role in adult hematopoiesis.
- **C)** Correct. In healthy adults, the bone marrow is the exclusive site of hematopoiesis under normal conditions. With age, active hematopoiesis shifts predominantly to the axial skeleton (vertebrae, sternum, ribs, skull, pelvis).
- **D)** Incorrect. The spleen is not a primary site of hematopoiesis in healthy adults. However, in stress conditions, extramedullary hematopoiesis can occur in the spleen and liver as a compensatory mechanism.

### Q6. Answer: D

- **A)** Incorrect. Thrombopoietin (TPO) is the primary growth factor for megakaryopoiesis and platelet production, not erythropoiesis. TPO acts through the MPL receptor on megakaryocyte progenitors.
- **B)** Incorrect. G-CSF is the primary growth factor for granulopoiesis, specifically promoting neutrophil production and mobilization. It is used clinically to treat neutropenia, not anemia.
- **C)** Incorrect. IL-5 is the specific differentiation factor for eosinophils. It does not play a significant role in erythrocyte production.
- **D)** Correct. Erythropoietin (EPO) is the essential growth factor for erythrocyte production. It is produced primarily by the renal cortex in response to low oxygen tension and acts on erythroid progenitors via the EPO receptor and JAK2 signaling pathway.

### Q7. Answer: C

- **A)** Incorrect. Decreased temperature shifts the curve to the left, increasing oxygen affinity and reducing oxygen delivery to tissues. Increased temperature (such as during fever or exercise) causes a right shift.
- **B)** Incorrect. Increased pH (alkalosis) shifts the curve to the left, increasing oxygen affinity. Decreased pH (acidosis) causes a right shift, promoting oxygen release to metabolically active tissues (the Bohr effect).
- **C)** Correct. Increased 2,3-BPG binds to deoxyhemoglobin and stabilizes the T-state, reducing hemoglobin's oxygen affinity and promoting oxygen release to tissues. This is an important adaptive mechanism at high altitude, where increased 2,3-BPG production improves oxygen delivery despite lower atmospheric oxygen.
- **D)** Incorrect. Fetal hemoglobin (HbF) shifts the curve to the left because its gamma chains have reduced affinity for 2,3-BPG, causing HbF to hold onto oxygen more tightly. This facilitates oxygen transfer from maternal to fetal blood.

### Q8. Answer: C

- **A)** Incorrect. ALA dehydrase catalyzes the conversion of ALA to porphobilinogen in the cytoplasm. While it is an important enzyme in heme biosynthesis (and is inhibited by lead), it is not the rate-limiting step.
- **B)** Incorrect. Ferrochelatase is the final enzyme in heme biosynthesis, inserting iron into protoporphyrin IX to form heme. It is also inhibited by lead but is not the rate-limiting enzyme.
- **C)** Correct. Delta-ALA synthase catalyzes the first and rate-limiting step of heme biosynthesis, converting glycine and succinyl-CoA to 5-aminolevulinic acid (ALA). It requires pyridoxal phosphate (vitamin B6) as a cofactor and is regulated by EPO and iron availability.
- **D)** Incorrect. Uroporphyrinogen decarboxylase is an intermediate enzyme in heme biosynthesis. Its deficiency causes porphyria cutanea tarda, but it is not the rate-limiting step.

### Q9. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. A fully mature erythrocyte that has been circulating for over 60 days is simply an aging red blood cell, not a reticulocyte. Reticulocytes are younger than mature erythrocytes and represent recently produced cells.
- **B)** Correct. A reticulocyte is an enucleated immature red blood cell that still contains residual RNA, giving it a slightly bluish tinge on standard staining (polychromasia). The residual RNA is visible with supravital stains such as new methylene blue. Reticulocytes complete the final 20% of hemoglobin synthesis before becoming fully mature erythrocytes.
- **C)** Incorrect. A nucleated erythroid precursor that has not yet begun hemoglobin synthesis more closely describes a pro-erythroblast or basophilic erythroblast. Reticulocytes have already lost their nucleus and have synthesized most of their hemoglobin.
- **D)** Incorrect. Platelet precursors derived from megakaryocytes are proplatelets, not reticulocytes. Reticulocytes are strictly in the erythroid lineage.

### Q10. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. The production of neutrophils from myeloid progenitors is primarily driven by G-CSF and GM-CSF, not thrombopoietin. TPO acts specifically on the megakaryocyte lineage.
- **B)** Correct. Thrombopoietin (TPO) is the primary growth factor for megakaryopoiesis. It acts through the MPL receptor to promote megakaryocyte proliferation, differentiation, and ultimately platelet production. TPO is regulated by a negative feedback loop in which circulating platelets bind and degrade TPO.
- **C)** Incorrect. B-cell and T-cell differentiation from lymphoid progenitors is driven primarily by IL-7, not thrombopoietin. TPO has no significant role in lymphoid development.
- **D)** Incorrect. The destruction of aged red blood cells is performed by splenic macrophages through mechanical filtration and phagocytosis. This process is not regulated by thrombopoietin.
