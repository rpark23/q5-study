## Answer Key

### Part 1: Lymphocyte Disorders

### Q1. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. The EBV-infected B-lymphocytes are actually the targets of the immune response, not the atypical cells seen on smear. EBV infects B-cells but rarely causes their malignant transformation in immunocompetent hosts; the atypical cells on smear are the immune response to those infected B-cells.
- **B)** Correct. In infectious mononucleosis, the atypical lymphocytes (Downey cells) seen on peripheral smear are reactive CD8+ cytotoxic T-cells that are responding to EBV-infected B-cells. Their large size and abundant cytoplasm reflect immune activation, not malignancy.
- **C)** Incorrect. Natural killer cells do play a role in early antiviral defense, but the characteristic atypical lymphocytes of mono are CD8+ T-cells, not NK cells. NK cells are not the dominant population producing the classic peripheral smear findings.
- **D)** Incorrect. Immature lymphoblasts would suggest acute lymphoblastic leukemia, not infectious mononucleosis. The atypical lymphocytes in mono have mature chromatin and are activated (not immature) T-cells, distinguishing them from the blastic chromatin seen in ALL.

### Q2. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. While tonsillar hypertrophy can occur in mononucleosis and rarely causes airway compromise, this is not the primary reason for avoiding contact sports. Airway obstruction is managed with corticosteroids, not activity restriction.
- **B)** Correct. Splenomegaly occurs in approximately 50% of patients with infectious mononucleosis, and contact sports create a risk of traumatic splenic rupture, which is a potentially life-threatening complication requiring emergent surgical intervention.
- **C)** Incorrect. Myocarditis is an uncommon complication of EBV infection and is not specifically prevented by avoiding contact sports. Activity restriction in the setting of myocarditis would be for a different reason (reducing cardiac workload), not the primary concern in routine mono management.
- **D)** Incorrect. Meningitis is a rare neurologic complication of EBV but is not prevented by avoiding physical activity. The contact sports restriction is specifically aimed at preventing splenic trauma, not CNS complications.

### Q3. Answer: C

- **A)** Incorrect. Acute myeloid leukemia would show myeloid markers (such as CD13, CD33, and myeloperoxidase) rather than the B-cell markers CD19 and CD22. The presence of B-lineage markers and TdT points to a lymphoid, not myeloid, neoplasm.
- **B)** Incorrect. Chronic lymphocytic leukemia is a disease of mature-appearing small lymphocytes, not blasts, and occurs almost exclusively in older adults. CLL would not present in a 4-year-old with >90% blasts in the marrow.
- **C)** Correct. CD19 and CD22 are B-cell lineage markers, and TdT (terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase) is a marker of immature lymphoid progenitors. Greater than 90% blasts in the bone marrow of a young child with these markers is diagnostic of B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia, the most common childhood malignancy.
- **D)** Incorrect. Burkitt lymphoma is a mature B-cell neoplasm that is TdT-negative and typically presents as an extranodal mass (jaw or abdomen) rather than a marrow-predominant process. The TdT positivity here indicates an immature precursor, not a mature B-cell lymphoma.

### Q4. Answer: C

- **A)** Incorrect. While age at diagnosis is a recognized prognostic factor in ALL (ages 1-10 are favorable), it has been surpassed by MRD status as the single most important prognostic indicator after induction therapy.
- **B)** Incorrect. Initial white blood cell count is an important pre-treatment risk factor (higher counts indicate worse prognosis), but once treatment begins, the response to therapy as measured by MRD is more predictive of long-term outcome.
- **C)** Correct. Measurable residual disease (MRD) status after induction chemotherapy is the single most important prognostic factor in ALL. Patients who achieve MRD-negativity have significantly better event-free survival, and MRD is now used to guide treatment intensification decisions.
- **D)** Incorrect. CNS involvement at diagnosis is a high-risk feature that affects treatment (requiring intensified intrathecal therapy), but post-induction MRD status remains the stronger predictor of overall outcome and is used to stratify ongoing therapy.

### Q5. Answer: C

- **A)** Incorrect. While tyrosine kinase inhibitors (e.g., imatinib, dasatinib) are added to chemotherapy regimens in Ph+ ALL and have improved outcomes, they are not curative as monotherapy. Ph+ ALL still carries a poor prognosis compared to standard-risk ALL, even with TKI addition.
- **B)** Incorrect. The Philadelphia chromosome is a strong independent adverse prognostic factor in ALL that confers high-risk status regardless of the patient's age. It is one of the most important cytogenetic abnormalities to identify in ALL.
- **C)** Correct. BCR-ABL positivity (Philadelphia chromosome) in ALL is associated with a poor prognosis and inferior outcomes compared to standard-risk ALL. Unlike in CML, where TKIs produce durable remissions, Ph+ ALL remains a high-risk disease that often requires more intensive treatment including consideration of stem cell transplant.
- **D)** Incorrect. Ph+ ALL is associated with an overall poor prognosis, not specifically a higher rate of CNS relapse. The Philadelphia chromosome affects systemic disease control and overall survival, not just CNS involvement.

### Q6. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. Renal tubular acidosis would primarily cause a metabolic acidosis with electrolyte disturbances different from the classic tumor lysis pattern. The combination of hyperuricemia, hyperkalemia, hyperphosphatemia, and hypocalcemia is not consistent with RTA.
- **B)** Correct. Tumor lysis syndrome results from the rapid destruction of leukemic cells, releasing intracellular contents into the bloodstream. This produces the classic tetrad: hyperuricemia (from nucleic acid breakdown), hyperkalemia (from cell lysis), hyperphosphatemia (from intracellular phosphate release), and secondary hypocalcemia (from calcium-phosphate precipitation).
- **C)** Incorrect. Adrenal insufficiency from steroid withdrawal would present with hypotension, hyponatremia, and hyperkalemia, but would not produce hyperuricemia or hyperphosphatemia. The full constellation of lab findings here is specific to tumor lysis syndrome.
- **D)** Incorrect. Sepsis-related acute kidney injury could cause electrolyte abnormalities, but the specific pattern of markedly elevated uric acid with hyperphosphatemia and reciprocal hypocalcemia is characteristic of tumor lysis syndrome, not sepsis-induced AKI.

### Q7. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. BTK inhibitors (e.g., ibrutinib) are effective in CLL but are reserved for patients who develop indications for treatment (symptomatic disease, progressive cytopenias, or massive lymphadenopathy). Starting therapy in an asymptomatic patient with early-stage CLL does not improve survival.
- **B)** Correct. Asymptomatic, early-stage CLL with no indications for treatment is managed with observation and regular monitoring (watch-and-wait). Multiple studies have shown that early treatment of asymptomatic CLL does not improve overall survival, so therapy is deferred until disease progression or symptom development.
- **C)** Incorrect. FCR (fludarabine, cyclophosphamide, rituximab) is an intensive chemoimmunotherapy regimen used for fit patients with CLL who require treatment. It would not be appropriate for an asymptomatic patient who has no indication for therapy.
- **D)** Incorrect. Allogeneic stem cell transplant is considered only for young patients with very high-risk or refractory CLL. It carries significant morbidity and mortality and would never be the next step for an asymptomatic, newly diagnosed patient.

### Q8. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. Schistocytes are fragmented red blood cells seen in microangiopathic hemolytic anemias (such as TTP/HUS or DIC) and mechanical hemolysis. They are not a feature of CLL.
- **B)** Correct. Smudge cells (basket cells) are the hallmark peripheral smear finding in CLL. They represent fragile CLL lymphocytes that rupture during the mechanical process of preparing the blood smear, leaving behind bare nuclei or disrupted nuclear material.
- **C)** Incorrect. Auer rods are crystallized granules found in the cytoplasm of myeloid blasts and are diagnostic of acute myeloid leukemia, not CLL. They are never seen in lymphoid malignancies.
- **D)** Incorrect. Teardrop cells (dacrocytes) are associated with myelofibrosis and other conditions causing bone marrow infiltration (myelophthisic processes). They reflect distortion of red cells as they squeeze through a fibrotic marrow, not a lymphoproliferative disorder.

### Q9. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. While bone marrow infiltration by CLL can eventually cause neutropenia, this patient's presentation with sinopulmonary infections and low immunoglobulin levels specifically points to humoral immune dysfunction, not neutropenia. Neutropenia would more typically predispose to bacterial and fungal infections without the characteristic immunoglobulin deficiency.
- **B)** Correct. CLL causes a profound dysfunction of humoral immunity, resulting in hypogammaglobulinemia. The malignant B-cells are dysfunctional and cannot produce effective antibodies, while also suppressing normal B-cell function. This leads to recurrent sinopulmonary infections, particularly from encapsulated organisms.
- **C)** Incorrect. T-cell depletion can occur with certain CLL treatments (e.g., fludarabine, alemtuzumab), but the question describes a patient with low immunoglobulin levels, pointing to a B-cell/humoral defect rather than a T-cell problem. The hypogammaglobulinemia is an intrinsic feature of CLL itself.
- **D)** Incorrect. The spleen does not sequester opsonizing antibodies. The spleen can sequester cellular elements (platelets, red cells), but the hypogammaglobulinemia in CLL is due to dysfunctional B-cell immunity, not splenic trapping of immunoglobulins.

### Q10. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. Deletion of 17p causes loss of TP53, which is essential for the DNA damage response that mediates the cytotoxic effect of chemotherapy. Patients with del(17p) are characteristically resistant to conventional chemotherapy, not responsive to it.
- **B)** Correct. Del(17p) results in loss of the TP53 tumor suppressor gene, causing a defective DNA damage response pathway. This renders CLL cells resistant to standard chemotherapy (which relies on inducing DNA damage to trigger apoptosis) and confers the worst prognosis of all cytogenetic abnormalities in CLL. These patients are managed with novel agents such as BTK inhibitors or BCL2 inhibitors.
- **C)** Incorrect. CLL can undergo transformation to an aggressive large-cell lymphoma (Richter transformation), but the specific transformation to monitor for is diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, not Hodgkin lymphoma. Del(17p) predicts chemoresistance and poor overall prognosis, not a specific type of transformation.
- **D)** Incorrect. Del(17p) carries the worst prognosis of all CLL cytogenetic abnormalities, not an excellent one. The favorable end of the cytogenetic spectrum in CLL is del(13q) as a sole abnormality, which is associated with the best prognosis.

### Q11. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. Large cell size with abundant basophilic cytoplasm and a prominent nucleolus describes a reactive immunoblast, not a lymphoblast. Immunoblasts are activated mature lymphocytes seen in reactive processes and should not be confused with malignant blasts.
- **B)** Correct. Lymphoblasts are characterized by fine, dispersed ("blastic") chromatin, scant cytoplasm, and inconspicuous or absent nucleoli. This immature chromatin pattern reflects active DNA replication in a malignant progenitor cell and is the key morphologic feature distinguishing blasts from reactive cells.
- **C)** Incorrect. Mature, clumped chromatin with moderate cytoplasm describes a small mature lymphocyte, such as those seen in CLL or normal circulating lymphocytes. This pattern indicates a fully differentiated cell, not a blast.
- **D)** Incorrect. A bilobed nucleus with eosinophilic granules describes an eosinophil, which is a granulocyte of the myeloid lineage. This has no relation to lymphoblast morphology.

### Q12. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. "Salt-and-pepper" chromatin is classically associated with neuroendocrine tumors (such as carcinoid tumors or small cell carcinoma), not CLL lymphocytes.
- **B)** Correct. CLL cells classically display "soccer ball" chromatin, characterized by round, clumped, and mature-appearing chromatin that gives the nucleus a patterned appearance resembling a soccer ball. This is a high-yield morphologic descriptor for CLL on peripheral smear.
- **C)** Incorrect. "Clock-face" (or "cartwheel") chromatin is the classic descriptor for plasma cells, in which heterochromatin is arranged in a radial pattern around the nucleus resembling the numbers on a clock face.
- **D)** Incorrect. "Owl's eye" appearance refers to the binucleated Reed-Sternberg cells of classical Hodgkin lymphoma, where two mirror-image nuclei with prominent eosinophilic nucleoli create the appearance of owl's eyes. It is not used for CLL morphology.

### Part 2: Malignant Lymphoma

### Q1. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma would show sheets of large atypical B-cells that are CD20-positive. The cells in this biopsy are CD20-negative with a characteristic inflammatory background, which is inconsistent with DLBCL.
- **B)** Correct. The binucleated cells with prominent nucleoli are Reed-Sternberg cells (classic "owl's eye" appearance). The immunophenotype CD30+/CD15+/CD20- with a mixed inflammatory background of lymphocytes, eosinophils, and histiocytes is the hallmark of classical Hodgkin lymphoma. The anterior mediastinal location is also typical for the nodular sclerosis subtype in young adults.
- **C)** Incorrect. T-cell lymphoblastic lymphoma can present as an anterior mediastinal mass in young men, but it shows sheets of immature T-cell blasts (TdT-positive), not scattered large binucleated cells with an inflammatory background. The CD30+/CD15+ immunophenotype is specific for Hodgkin lymphoma.
- **D)** Incorrect. Thymoma is an anterior mediastinal tumor but consists of neoplastic thymic epithelial cells mixed with mature T-lymphocytes. It would not show the Reed-Sternberg morphology or the CD30+/CD15+ immunophenotype seen here.

### Q2. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. Stage IA would mean a single lymph node region with no B symptoms. This patient has two nodal regions (cervical and mediastinal) and has B symptoms (night sweats, weight loss), so Stage IA underestimates both the anatomic extent and the symptomatic burden.
- **B)** Correct. Two lymph node regions on the same side of the diaphragm defines Stage II in the Ann Arbor staging system. The "B" suffix denotes the presence of B symptoms: drenching night sweats, unexplained weight loss >10% of body weight over 6 months, or unexplained fever >38 degrees C. This patient has Stage IIB.
- **C)** Incorrect. Stage III requires lymph node involvement on both sides of the diaphragm. This patient's disease is confined to cervical and mediastinal nodes, which are both above the diaphragm. Additionally, the "A" designation would mean no B symptoms, but she clearly has B symptoms.
- **D)** Incorrect. Stage IV requires extranodal (organ) involvement (e.g., bone marrow, liver, lung parenchyma). There is no mention of extranodal disease in this patient, and her involvement is limited to two nodal regions on the same side of the diaphragm.

### Q3. Answer: D

- **A)** Incorrect. Bleomycin is associated with pulmonary toxicity (pulmonary fibrosis), not cardiotoxicity. A patient presenting with dyspnea after bleomycin exposure would more likely show restrictive lung disease on pulmonary function testing, not a reduced ejection fraction.
- **B)** Incorrect. Vinblastine is a vinca alkaloid whose major toxicities include myelosuppression and peripheral neuropathy. It is not a known cause of late-onset cardiomyopathy.
- **C)** Incorrect. Dacarbazine is an alkylating agent with primary side effects of nausea and myelosuppression. It is not associated with dose-dependent cardiotoxicity or cardiomyopathy.
- **D)** Correct. Adriamycin (doxorubicin) is an anthracycline that causes dose-dependent cardiotoxicity through free radical damage to cardiomyocytes. This can manifest as dilated cardiomyopathy with reduced ejection fraction years to decades after treatment completion, making long-term cardiac monitoring essential for Hodgkin lymphoma survivors.

### Q4. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. MYC activation is the hallmark of Burkitt lymphoma, driven by t(8;14). Follicular lymphoma involves t(14;18), which does not affect MYC but instead dysregulates BCL2, an anti-apoptotic protein.
- **B)** Correct. The t(14;18) translocation places the BCL2 gene under the control of the immunoglobulin heavy chain (IgH) promoter, leading to constitutive overexpression of the BCL2 anti-apoptotic protein. This prevents normal programmed cell death of germinal center B-cells, allowing them to accumulate and drive lymphomagenesis.
- **C)** Incorrect. Constitutive activation of Cyclin D1 is the mechanism of mantle cell lymphoma, driven by the t(11;14) translocation. Follicular lymphoma involves BCL2 overexpression (anti-apoptosis), not Cyclin D1 (cell cycle progression).
- **D)** Incorrect. A fusion tyrosine kinase (such as BCR-ABL) is characteristic of CML and Ph+ ALL, not follicular lymphoma. The t(14;18) translocation does not create a kinase fusion protein; it causes overexpression of the intact BCL2 protein.

### Q5. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. This is the opposite of the expected pattern. Normal germinal center B-cells physiologically downregulate BCL2 to allow apoptosis of low-affinity clones during affinity maturation. Follicular lymphoma is BCL2-positive due to the t(14;18) translocation.
- **B)** Correct. Normal germinal center B-cells are BCL2-negative because they downregulate BCL2 expression to permit apoptosis of B-cells with low-affinity antibodies during somatic hypermutation. Follicular lymphoma cells aberrantly express BCL2 due to t(14;18), preventing this normal apoptotic process. This BCL2 staining pattern is a key diagnostic tool for distinguishing neoplastic follicles from reactive germinal centers.
- **C)** Incorrect. Normal germinal centers are BCL2-negative, which is actually a critical part of normal B-cell selection. If both were BCL2-positive, BCL2 staining would have no diagnostic utility in distinguishing reactive from neoplastic follicles.
- **D)** Incorrect. While normal germinal centers are indeed BCL2-negative, follicular lymphoma cells are BCL2-positive due to the translocation. If both were negative, the t(14;18) translocation would have no functional significance.

### Q6. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. Follicular lymphoma is an indolent (slow-growing) lymphoma, not aggressive. While R-CHOP may be used when treatment is indicated, asymptomatic patients with low tumor burden can often be observed initially without compromising survival.
- **B)** Correct. Follicular lymphoma is the prototypical indolent B-cell lymphoma. It is generally considered incurable with standard therapy (it tends to relapse after treatment), but its slow natural history results in a median overall survival approaching 20 years. Asymptomatic patients with low tumor burden may be observed with a watch-and-wait approach.
- **C)** Incorrect. While follicular lymphoma is indolent, it is generally not curable with standard chemotherapy. Most patients will eventually relapse after treatment, which is why observation is a reasonable approach for asymptomatic patients -- early treatment does not prevent future relapses.
- **D)** Incorrect. A median survival of less than 2 years describes highly aggressive lymphomas such as refractory Burkitt or certain T-cell lymphomas. Follicular lymphoma has an excellent long-term survival measured in decades, not months to years.

### Q7. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. DLBCL is an aggressive lymphoma that is rapidly fatal if untreated and requires prompt initiation of therapy. Observation alone, which is appropriate for indolent lymphomas like follicular lymphoma, would lead to rapid disease progression and death in DLBCL.
- **B)** Correct. Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma is the most common non-Hodgkin lymphoma and is classified as aggressive. However, it is potentially curable with R-CHOP (rituximab, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, prednisone) chemotherapy, with cure rates of approximately 50-60%. The key teaching point is that "aggressive" lymphomas are paradoxically more curable than "indolent" ones.
- **C)** Incorrect. While radiation can play a role in limited-stage DLBCL as consolidation, DLBCL is primarily treated with systemic chemoimmunotherapy (R-CHOP). Radiation alone would be insufficient for this systemic disease.
- **D)** Incorrect. Autologous stem cell transplant is not first-line therapy for DLBCL. It is reserved for patients who relapse after initial R-CHOP therapy and achieve a response to salvage chemotherapy. Most patients are cured with initial R-CHOP alone.

### Q8. Answer: C

- **A)** Incorrect. BCL2 is the gene dysregulated in follicular lymphoma via t(14;18). Burkitt lymphoma is driven by t(8;14), which involves a different oncogene -- MYC -- that drives cell proliferation rather than preventing apoptosis.
- **B)** Incorrect. Cyclin D1 overexpression is the hallmark of mantle cell lymphoma, caused by t(11;14). Burkitt lymphoma's t(8;14) does not involve Cyclin D1 but rather MYC, explaining the extremely high proliferation rate (approaching 100% Ki-67 index).
- **C)** Correct. The t(8;14) translocation juxtaposes the MYC oncogene with the immunoglobulin heavy chain (IgH) locus, causing constitutive MYC overexpression. MYC is a master transcription factor driving cell proliferation, which explains the extremely high proliferative rate and "starry sky" pattern (rapidly dividing cells dying and being phagocytosed by macrophages) characteristic of Burkitt lymphoma.
- **D)** Incorrect. BCL6 is a transcription factor important in germinal center biology and can be rearranged in certain cases of DLBCL, but it is not the gene dysregulated by t(8;14) in Burkitt lymphoma. The defining genetic event in Burkitt is MYC translocation.

### Q9. Answer: C

- **A)** Incorrect. Double-hit lymphomas are among the most chemoresistant B-cell lymphomas, not more chemosensitive. The concurrent activation of both proliferative (MYC) and anti-apoptotic (BCL2) pathways creates a tumor that grows rapidly and resists apoptosis induced by chemotherapy.
- **B)** Incorrect. The presence of concurrent MYC and BCL2 translocations has a very significant negative prognostic impact. Double-hit lymphomas are now classified as a separate entity (high-grade B-cell lymphoma with MYC and BCL2 rearrangements) because of their distinct and poor biology.
- **C)** Correct. Double-hit lymphomas (concurrent MYC and BCL2 translocations) have a significantly worse prognosis compared to standard DLBCL. The combination of MYC-driven proliferation and BCL2-mediated apoptosis resistance creates a highly aggressive disease that responds poorly to standard R-CHOP, often requiring more intensive regimens (e.g., DA-EPOCH-R).
