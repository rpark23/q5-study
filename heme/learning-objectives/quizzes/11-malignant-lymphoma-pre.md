## Answer Key

### Q1. Answer: C

- **A)** Incorrect. Metastatic solid tumors can cause lymphadenopathy, but they typically cause localized regional lymphadenopathy rather than generalized lymphadenopathy. Infection is the most common cause overall.
- **B)** Incorrect. Lymphoma is an important cause of lymphadenopathy and must be considered in the differential diagnosis, but it is far less common than infectious causes of generalized lymphadenopathy.
- **C)** Correct. Infection is the most common cause of lymph node enlargement. Generalized lymphadenopathy can result from infectious, neoplastic, medication-related, autoimmune, and endocrine causes, but infectious etiologies are the most frequent.
- **D)** Incorrect. Autoimmune conditions (immune-mediated, non-infectious causes) can produce generalized lymphadenopathy, but they are less common than infectious causes as the etiology of lymph node enlargement.

### Q2. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. Mantle zone B-cells are the cell of origin for mantle cell lymphoma, not Hodgkin lymphoma. Mantle cell lymphoma is characterized by t(11;14) and Cyclin D1 overexpression.
- **B)** Correct. Classical Hodgkin lymphoma is a neoplasm derived from germinal center B-cells. The neoplastic Reed-Sternberg cells originate from germinal center B-cells but have an impaired B-cell program, losing most B-cell markers (e.g., CD20) while retaining weak PAX5 positivity.
- **C)** Incorrect. Post-germinal center memory B-cells give rise to marginal zone lymphomas and some subtypes of DLBCL, not classical Hodgkin lymphoma.
- **D)** Incorrect. Precursor T-cells give rise to T-cell lymphoblastic lymphoma/leukemia, not Hodgkin lymphoma. Hodgkin lymphoma is a B-cell derived neoplasm.

### Q3. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. This phenotype (CD20+, CD45+, CD30-) would be more consistent with a mature B-cell lymphoma such as DLBCL. Reed-Sternberg cells are characteristically CD20-negative and CD45-negative.
- **B)** Correct. Reed-Sternberg cells in classical Hodgkin lymphoma express CD30 and CD15, but are typically negative for CD20 and CD45 (common leukocyte antigen). They are weakly PAX5 positive and may be EBV positive.
- **C)** Incorrect. CD10+, CD20+, BCL6+ is the immunophenotype of germinal center B-cells and is characteristic of follicular lymphoma or the germinal center B-cell subtype of DLBCL, not Hodgkin lymphoma.
- **D)** Incorrect. CD5+, CD23+, CD20+ is the classic immunophenotype of chronic lymphocytic leukemia/small lymphocytic lymphoma (CLL/SLL), not Hodgkin lymphoma.

### Q4. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. Bone pain, back pain, and bilateral lymphadenopathy are not the definition of B symptoms. B symptoms are a specific set of systemic constitutional symptoms used in lymphoma staging.
- **B)** Correct. B symptoms are defined as fever greater than 100.4 degrees F, drenching night sweats, and unexplained weight loss of 10% or more of body weight over 6 months. The presence of B symptoms is denoted by the "B" suffix in Ann Arbor staging and indicates more aggressive disease.
- **C)** Incorrect. Bleeding, bruising, and bone marrow infiltration are not B symptoms. These may be clinical findings in lymphoma but are not part of the formal staging designation.
- **D)** Incorrect. Breathlessness, pleural effusions, and bulky disease are not B symptoms. While bulky disease is accounted for in the Lugano modification of Ann Arbor staging, it is a separate designation from B symptoms.

### Q5. Answer: C

- **A)** Incorrect. Hodgkin lymphoma is not exclusive to older patients. While there is a second peak in patients over 50, a large proportion of cases occur in young adults aged 15-35.
- **B)** Incorrect. Hodgkin lymphoma does not peak in children under 5. The first peak occurs in young adults aged 15-35 years old.
- **C)** Correct. Hodgkin lymphoma has a characteristic bimodal age distribution with the first peak occurring between ages 15 and 35, and a second peak occurring in patients over 50 years of age.
- **D)** Incorrect. The incidence of Hodgkin lymphoma is not uniform across age groups. It has a well-established bimodal distribution, which is a frequently tested and clinically important epidemiologic feature.

### Q6. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. Adriamycin (doxorubicin) is an anthracycline associated with long-term cardiac toxicity (cardiomyopathy with reduced ejection fraction), not pulmonary toxicity. Cardiac monitoring is essential for survivors treated with this agent.
- **B)** Correct. Bleomycin is the ABVD component most associated with pulmonary toxicity, specifically pulmonary fibrosis. Patients who develop progressive dyspnea after bleomycin exposure should be evaluated for restrictive lung disease.
- **C)** Incorrect. Vinblastine is a vinca alkaloid whose major toxicities include myelosuppression and peripheral neuropathy, not pulmonary toxicity.
- **D)** Incorrect. Dacarbazine is an alkylating agent whose primary side effects include nausea and myelosuppression. It is not a known cause of pulmonary toxicity.

### Q7. Answer: C

- **A)** Incorrect. Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma is the most common non-Hodgkin lymphoma overall, but it is classified as aggressive, not indolent. It accounts for approximately 30% of all NHL.
- **B)** Incorrect. Burkitt lymphoma is a highly aggressive lymphoma characterized by t(8;14) and MYC dysregulation with a near 100% proliferation rate. It is not indolent.
- **C)** Correct. Follicular lymphoma is the most common indolent lymphoma, accounting for approximately 60% of low-grade lymphomas and 25% of all non-Hodgkin lymphomas. It is characterized by the t(14;18) translocation and BCL2 overexpression.
- **D)** Incorrect. Mantle cell lymphoma is generally considered an aggressive lymphoma characterized by t(11;14) and Cyclin D1 overexpression. It is not classified as indolent.

### Q8. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. DLBCL is not indolent. It is classified as an aggressive lymphoma that is rapidly fatal if untreated. The description "indolent and incurable" better fits follicular lymphoma.
- **B)** Correct. DLBCL is an aggressive lymphoma but is potentially curable with R-CHOP chemotherapy, with cure rates of approximately 50-60%. This is a key teaching point: aggressive lymphomas are paradoxically more curable than indolent ones because their rapid proliferation makes them more susceptible to chemotherapy.
- **C)** Incorrect. DLBCL is not indolent and cannot be cured with radiation alone. It requires systemic chemoimmunotherapy (R-CHOP) as primary treatment.
- **D)** Incorrect. While DLBCL is fatal if untreated, it is not universally fatal with treatment. Approximately 50-60% of patients are cured with standard R-CHOP chemotherapy.

### Q9. Answer: C

- **A)** Incorrect. The t(14;18) translocation involving BCL2 is characteristic of follicular lymphoma, not Burkitt lymphoma. BCL2 is an anti-apoptotic protein, whereas Burkitt lymphoma is driven by a proliferation-driving oncogene.
- **B)** Incorrect. The t(11;14) translocation involving Cyclin D1 is characteristic of mantle cell lymphoma, not Burkitt lymphoma. Cyclin D1 drives cell cycle progression in mantle cell lymphoma.
- **C)** Correct. Burkitt lymphoma is defined by the t(8;14) translocation, which juxtaposes the MYC oncogene with the immunoglobulin heavy chain locus, leading to constitutive MYC overexpression and extremely rapid cellular proliferation (near 100% Ki-67 index).
- **D)** Incorrect. The t(9;22) translocation producing the BCR-ABL fusion protein is characteristic of chronic myeloid leukemia and Philadelphia chromosome-positive ALL, not Burkitt lymphoma.

### Q10. Answer: C

- **A)** Incorrect. Fifty percent significantly underestimates the proportion of B-cell lymphomas among non-Hodgkin lymphomas. B-cell lymphomas are the predominant subtype.
- **B)** Incorrect. Sixty-five percent underestimates the true proportion. B-cell lymphomas make up a much larger share of non-Hodgkin lymphomas.
- **C)** Correct. B-cell lymphomas account for approximately 85% of all non-Hodgkin lymphomas. T-cell lymphomas make up the remaining 10-15% and generally carry a worse prognosis with fewer effective treatment options.
- **D)** Incorrect. Ninety-five percent overestimates the proportion. T-cell lymphomas, while less common, still account for 10-15% of non-Hodgkin lymphomas.
