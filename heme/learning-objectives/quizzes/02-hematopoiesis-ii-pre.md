## Answer Key

### Q1. Answer: C

- **A)** Incorrect. The thymus is the site of T-cell maturation, not B-cell development. T-cell precursors migrate from the bone marrow to the thymus for positive and negative selection.
- **B)** Incorrect. The spleen is a secondary lymphoid organ where mature B-cells can encounter antigens and mount immune responses. It is not the site of initial B-cell development.
- **C)** Correct. B-cells develop in the bone marrow (B = Bone marrow). The antigen-independent phase includes heavy chain rearrangement, pairing with surrogate light chain, and light chain rearrangement. Immature B-cells exit the marrow expressing surface IgM.
- **D)** Incorrect. Lymph node germinal centers are sites where mature B-cells undergo somatic hypermutation and class switching after encountering antigen. This is the antigen-dependent phase, not the initial developmental phase.

### Q2. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. CD34 is a marker of immaturity, not maturity. B-cells that have completed class switching are mature effector cells that no longer express CD34. CD34 expression would be expected on early precursors, not on class-switched cells.
- **B)** Correct. CD34 is expressed on very immature hematopoietic cells, including early B-cell precursors in the bone marrow. As B-cells mature, they lose CD34 expression. Persistent CD34 expression on B-lineage cells, especially outside the bone marrow, may suggest acute B-lymphoblastic leukemia (B-ALL).
- **C)** Incorrect. Memory B-cells are mature post-germinal center cells that express high-affinity immunoglobulins. They do not express CD34, which is a marker of immaturity lost early in B-cell development.
- **D)** Incorrect. Somatic hypermutation occurs in the germinal center in B-cells that have already matured well past the CD34-positive stage. CD34 is lost early in development and is not a marker of germinal center activity.

### Q3. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. Positive and negative selection of T-cells occurs in the thymus (cortex and medulla, respectively), not in lymph node germinal centers. Germinal centers are B-cell structures.
- **B)** Correct. The germinal center is a specialized immune zone within lymph node follicles where activated B-cells undergo somatic hypermutation of immunoglobulin genes to increase antigen affinity, and heavy chain class switching (IgM to IgG, IgA, or IgE). B-cells that fail to improve affinity undergo apoptosis.
- **C)** Incorrect. Hematopoietic stem cell differentiation into lymphoid progenitors occurs in the bone marrow, not in germinal centers. The common lymphoid progenitor (CLP) is generated in the marrow before migrating to lymphoid organs.
- **D)** Incorrect. Plasma cells generated from germinal center reactions typically migrate to the bone marrow or mucosal tissues to secrete antibodies. The germinal center itself is not a storage area for plasma cells; it is a dynamic site of B-cell selection and maturation.

### Q4. Answer: C

- **A)** Incorrect. The follicles are the B-cell rich zones of the lymph node cortex. They contain primary follicles (without germinal centers) and secondary follicles (with germinal centers). They are not the T-cell zone.
- **B)** Incorrect. The medullary sinuses are channels that drain lymph toward the efferent lymphatic vessel. They contain macrophages and some plasma cells but are not the primary T-cell zone of the lymph node.
- **C)** Correct. The paracortex is the T-cell rich zone of the lymph node, located between the cortical follicles and the medulla. It contains high endothelial venules (HEVs) through which naive lymphocytes enter the lymph node from the bloodstream. T-cells interact with dendritic cells presenting antigen in this zone.
- **D)** Incorrect. The subcapsular sinus is the area just beneath the lymph node capsule where afferent lymph first enters the node carrying antigens. It contains macrophages but is not the T-cell zone.

### Q5. Answer: C

- **A)** Incorrect. The bone marrow is where T-cell precursors originate, but T-cell maturation (including TCR rearrangement, positive selection, and negative selection) occurs in the thymus after precursors migrate there.
- **B)** Incorrect. The spleen is a secondary lymphoid organ that filters blood and supports immune responses. It does not perform T-cell maturation or thymic education.
- **C)** Correct. The thymus is the primary organ for T-cell maturation. Precursor T-cells from the bone marrow enter the thymic cortex, undergo TCR rearrangement, pass through positive selection (cortex) and negative selection (cortex/medulla), and exit as mature naive CD4+ or CD8+ T-cells.
- **D)** Incorrect. The liver plays a minor role in hematopoiesis during fetal development and produces some erythropoietin in adults, but it is not involved in T-cell maturation.

### Q6. Answer: C

- **A)** Incorrect. Germinal centers with dark and light zones are found in lymph node follicles and splenic white pulp follicles, not in the thymic medulla. The thymus does not have germinal centers under normal conditions.
- **B)** Incorrect. High endothelial venules (HEVs) are characteristic of the lymph node paracortex, where they facilitate naive lymphocyte entry. They are not a defining feature of the thymic medulla.
- **C)** Correct. Hassall corpuscles are concentric whorls of epithelial cells found in the thymic medulla. They are a histologic hallmark of the thymus and are thought to contribute to regulatory T-cell development through TSLP production. The medulla is where mature single-positive T-cells reside before exiting to the circulation.
- **D)** Incorrect. Marginal zone B-cells are found at the border of the white pulp and red pulp in the spleen. They are not found in the thymic medulla, which is a T-cell maturation site.

### Q7. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. Adaptive immune responses against blood-borne pathogens are primarily mediated by the white pulp of the spleen, which contains organized B-cell follicles and T-cell zones (PALS). The red pulp serves a filtration function.
- **B)** Correct. The splenic red pulp filters aged or abnormal erythrocytes through its cords of Billroth and sinusoidal slits. Macrophages in the red pulp phagocytose (cull) aged red cells or remove membrane inclusions (pit). The hypoxic, acidic environment of the cords is especially toxic to aged or abnormal erythrocytes.
- **C)** Incorrect. Thrombopoietin is produced primarily by the liver, not the splenic red pulp. The spleen does sequester approximately one-third of circulating platelets, but it does not produce TPO.
- **D)** Incorrect. Naive B-cells and T-cells are found in the white pulp of the spleen (follicles and PALS, respectively), not in the red pulp. The red pulp is primarily a filtration zone for erythrocytes.

### Q8. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. The PALS is the T-cell rich zone, not a B-cell zone. It surrounds the central artery and contains T-cells that interact with dendritic cells and B-cells. The B-cell rich zones in the spleen are the follicles and the marginal zone.
- **B)** Correct. The periarteriolar lymphoid sheath (PALS) is a T-cell rich zone in the splenic white pulp that surrounds the central artery. It is analogous to the paracortex of the lymph node and is where T-cells interact with antigen-presenting cells and provide help to B-cells.
- **C)** Incorrect. The splenic cords (cords of Billroth) are part of the red pulp, not the white pulp. They are reticular tissue structures lined by macrophages that filter and phagocytose aged or abnormal red blood cells.
- **D)** Incorrect. Sinusoidal endothelium in the red pulp (lined by littoral cells) is involved in filtering red blood cells, not in adaptive immune function. This is a red pulp structure, whereas the PALS is in the white pulp.

### Q9. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. While asplenic patients may have some increased risk from intraerythrocytic parasites like Babesia (since the spleen helps clear parasitized red cells), the classic and most dangerous susceptibility is to encapsulated bacteria, not intracellular parasites.
- **B)** Correct. Asplenic patients are uniquely susceptible to overwhelming post-splenectomy infection (OPSI) by encapsulated bacteria, especially Streptococcus pneumoniae, Haemophilus influenzae, and Neisseria meningitidis. The spleen's marginal zone is critical for opsonization and clearance of these organisms, which is why vaccination is required before or after splenectomy.
- **C)** Incorrect. Anaerobic bacteria are not typically associated with increased risk in asplenic patients. Defense against anaerobes depends on other immune mechanisms, not splenic function.
- **D)** Incorrect. Opportunistic fungal infections are more commonly associated with severe neutropenia or T-cell immunodeficiency, not with asplenia. The spleen is not the primary defense against fungal pathogens.

### Q10. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. Autoantibody-mediated destruction of blood cells describes autoimmune cytopenias (such as autoimmune hemolytic anemia or immune thrombocytopenic purpura), which have different pathophysiologic mechanisms from hypersplenism.
- **B)** Correct. Hypersplenism is defined as enlargement of the spleen with increased phagocytosis and sequestration of blood cells, resulting in cytopenias (anemia, leukopenia, and/or thrombocytopenia, with thrombocytopenia being the most common). The bone marrow is typically hyperplastic as a compensatory response.
- **C)** Incorrect. This describes hyposplenism or asplenia, which is the opposite of hypersplenism. Hyposplenism leads to impaired filtration and increased susceptibility to infections by encapsulated organisms.
- **D)** Incorrect. Neoplastic infiltration of the spleen can be a cause of splenomegaly and may contribute to hypersplenism, but it is not the definition of hypersplenism itself. Hypersplenism refers to the functional consequence (increased sequestration) of an enlarged spleen.
