## Answer Key

### Q1. Answer: C

- **A)** Incorrect. Anemia caused by nutritional deficiency (iron, B12, folate) is classified as hypoproliferative anemia, not aplastic anemia. In these conditions, the bone marrow is typically cellular (or even hypercellular in megaloblastic anemias) with identifiable defects in maturation, rather than the hypocellular, fat-replaced marrow seen in aplastic anemia.
- **B)** Incorrect. Accelerated destruction of red blood cells in the spleen describes extravascular hemolysis, a feature of hemolytic anemias. In hemolytic anemias, the bone marrow is hypercellular with increased erythropoiesis, which is the opposite of the hypocellular marrow in aplastic anemia.
- **C)** Correct. Aplastic anemia is defined by pancytopenia (low red cells, white cells, and platelets) resulting from failure or destruction of hematopoietic stem cells. The bone marrow biopsy characteristically shows a markedly hypocellular marrow with replacement of hematopoietic tissue by fat. This distinguishes it from other causes of pancytopenia such as leukemia or myelofibrosis.
- **D)** Incorrect. Clonal expansion of malignant blast cells crowding out normal marrow describes acute leukemia or other marrow-infiltrating malignancies. In these conditions, the marrow is typically hypercellular with blasts, not hypocellular with fat replacement as in aplastic anemia.

### Q2. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. While certain viral infections (such as hepatitis) are associated with aplastic anemia, the mechanism is not direct viral lysis of stem cells. Instead, viral infection is thought to trigger an aberrant autoimmune T-cell response against hematopoietic stem cells. The immune-mediated destruction is the primary mechanism.
- **B)** Correct. Most cases of acquired aplastic anemia are caused by autoreactive cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CD8+ T cells) that attack and destroy hematopoietic stem cells through interferon and TNF-induced apoptosis. This was established historically when treatment with antithymocyte globulin (ATG, an anti-T-cell therapy) led to marrow recovery, confirming the immune-mediated mechanism.
- **C)** Incorrect. Nutritional deficiency causes ineffective hematopoiesis with a hypercellular marrow (e.g., megaloblastic changes in B12/folate deficiency), not the hypocellular, fat-replaced marrow characteristic of aplastic anemia.
- **D)** Incorrect. Fibrosis replacing normal bone marrow tissue describes myelofibrosis, which is a distinct myeloproliferative disorder. Aplastic anemia features fat replacement of marrow, not fibrotic replacement.

### Q3. Answer: C

- **A)** Incorrect. High-dose corticosteroids are the first-line treatment for Diamond-Blackfan anemia, not acquired aplastic anemia. Corticosteroids are not effective as primary therapy for acquired aplastic anemia because the mechanism is T-cell-mediated stem cell destruction, not a steroid-responsive process.
- **B)** Incorrect. ATG plus cyclosporine is the standard immunosuppressive regimen for patients with acquired aplastic anemia who lack a matched sibling donor or who are older (typically over 40 years). When a matched sibling donor is available for a young patient, transplantation is preferred because it offers a definitive cure with 90-95% long-term survival.
- **C)** Correct. Allogeneic bone marrow transplantation from an HLA-matched sibling donor is the treatment of choice for young patients with severe acquired aplastic anemia, offering long-term survival rates of 90-95%. It replaces the destroyed hematopoietic stem cell compartment with donor-derived stem cells, providing a definitive cure rather than immunosuppression alone.
- **D)** Incorrect. Eltrombopag is a thrombopoietin receptor agonist that has shown promise as an adjunct to immunosuppressive therapy in aplastic anemia by stimulating residual stem cells. However, it is not used as monotherapy and does not offer the curative potential of a matched sibling transplant.

### Q4. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. Acquired autoimmune pure red cell aplasia is a different entity that occurs in adults and can be associated with thymomas or autoimmune diseases. Fanconi anemia is an inherited (autosomal recessive) syndrome, not acquired.
- **B)** Correct. Fanconi anemia is an autosomal recessive inherited bone marrow failure syndrome caused by defects in DNA interstrand crosslink repair (involving 23 identified genes in the FA/BRCA pathway). It is characterized by progressive bone marrow failure, congenital anomalies (short stature, thumb abnormalities, skin hyperpigmentation, cafe-au-lait spots), and a greater than 15% lifetime risk of developing malignancies including AML and squamous cell carcinomas.
- **C)** Incorrect. The triad of dystrophic nails, reticular skin pigmentation, and oral leukoplakia describes dyskeratosis congenita, which is a telomere biology disorder, not a DNA repair defect. Although both are inherited bone marrow failure syndromes, they have distinct molecular mechanisms and clinical features.
- **D)** Incorrect. An inherited ribosomal protein disorder causing isolated erythroid failure in infancy describes Diamond-Blackfan anemia, not Fanconi anemia. Fanconi anemia causes pancytopenia (all lineages affected), not isolated red cell aplasia.

### Q5. Answer: C

- **A)** Incorrect. Telomere length analysis is the diagnostic test for dyskeratosis congenita, not Fanconi anemia. Very short telomeres across multiple cell lineages confirm telomere biology disorders, which have a distinct molecular basis from Fanconi anemia.
- **B)** Incorrect. Elevated erythrocyte adenosine deaminase (eADA) activity is a supportive laboratory finding for Diamond-Blackfan anemia, not Fanconi anemia. eADA is elevated in DBA as part of the fetal red cell characteristics seen in that condition.
- **C)** Correct. The diagnostic test for Fanconi anemia is the diepoxybutane (DEB) chromosomal breakage test. Lymphocytes from the patient are exposed to DEB (a DNA crosslinking agent), and the cells are examined for chromosomal breaks and radial figures. Markedly increased chromosomal breakage confirms the underlying DNA repair defect characteristic of Fanconi anemia.
- **D)** Incorrect. Bone marrow biopsy showing megaloblastic changes would suggest B12 or folate deficiency, not Fanconi anemia. While Fanconi anemia does show a hypocellular marrow, the definitive diagnosis requires the DEB chromosomal breakage test, not marrow morphology alone.

### Q6. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. Short stature, absent thumbs, and cafe-au-lait spots are features of Fanconi anemia, not dyskeratosis congenita. While both syndromes cause bone marrow failure, their physical examination findings and molecular mechanisms are distinct.
- **B)** Correct. The classic clinical triad of dyskeratosis congenita consists of dystrophic nails, reticular (lacy, net-like) skin pigmentation, and oral leukoplakia (white patches on the oral mucosa). This triad, combined with progressive bone marrow failure and very short telomeres, is pathognomonic for dyskeratosis congenita.
- **C)** Incorrect. Pancreatic insufficiency, neutropenia, and skeletal abnormalities describe Shwachman-Diamond syndrome, which is caused by mutations in genes involved in ribosome biogenesis (most commonly SBDS). This is a different inherited bone marrow failure syndrome with distinct features.
- **D)** Incorrect. Macrocytic anemia, reticulocytopenia, and thumb anomalies describe Diamond-Blackfan anemia, a congenital pure red cell aplasia caused by ribosomal protein gene mutations. While thumb anomalies can be seen in both DBA and Fanconi anemia, the mucocutaneous triad is specific to dyskeratosis congenita.

### Q7. Answer: C

- **A)** Incorrect. Defective DNA interstrand crosslink repair is the molecular basis of Fanconi anemia, not dyskeratosis congenita. The FA/BRCA pathway handles DNA crosslink repair, which is unrelated to the telomere maintenance pathway affected in dyskeratosis congenita.
- **B)** Incorrect. Mutations in ribosomal protein genes causing haploinsufficiency describe the molecular basis of Diamond-Blackfan anemia, not dyskeratosis congenita. DBA involves impaired ribosome formation leading to p53 activation and erythroid progenitor apoptosis.
- **C)** Correct. Dyskeratosis congenita is a telomere biology disorder caused by mutations in genes involved in telomere maintenance, including DKC1 (most common, X-linked), TINF2, TERC, and TERT. These mutations lead to impaired telomerase function and progressive telomere shortening, resulting in critically short telomeres that can be measured to confirm the diagnosis.
- **D)** Incorrect. Pyruvate kinase deficiency is a red blood cell enzymopathy that causes chronic hemolytic anemia, not bone marrow failure. It has no connection to the telomere biology pathway disrupted in dyskeratosis congenita.

### Q8. Answer: D

- **A)** Incorrect. Fanconi anemia presents with pancytopenia and congenital anomalies (radial ray defects, cafe-au-lait spots, short stature) but does not characteristically feature exocrine pancreatic insufficiency. Fanconi anemia is a DNA repair disorder, not associated with pancreatic dysfunction.
- **B)** Incorrect. Diamond-Blackfan anemia is a pure red cell aplasia affecting only the erythroid lineage. It does not cause neutropenia or pancreatic dysfunction. DBA is caused by ribosomal protein gene mutations.
- **C)** Incorrect. Dyskeratosis congenita is a telomere biology disorder that presents with the mucocutaneous triad and progressive bone marrow failure but does not cause exocrine pancreatic insufficiency.
- **D)** Correct. Shwachman-Diamond syndrome is uniquely characterized by the combination of neutropenia (predominantly affecting the myeloid lineage, though other lineages may also be affected) and exocrine pancreatic insufficiency causing fat malabsorption. It is caused by mutations in the SBDS gene involved in ribosome biogenesis. This distinctive pairing of hematologic and pancreatic dysfunction is not seen in other inherited bone marrow failure syndromes.

### Q9. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. Pancytopenia affecting all three cell lineages is characteristic of aplastic anemia (acquired or inherited forms like Fanconi anemia), not Diamond-Blackfan anemia. DBA specifically affects only the erythroid lineage.
- **B)** Correct. Diamond-Blackfan anemia is a congenital pure red cell aplasia, meaning it selectively affects erythroid progenitors while sparing myeloid and megakaryocytic lineages. Patients present with severe macrocytic anemia and reticulocytopenia but maintain normal white blood cell counts and platelet counts. This single-lineage failure distinguishes it from other inherited bone marrow failure syndromes that cause pancytopenia.
- **C)** Incorrect. Isolated severe neutropenia is more characteristic of severe congenital neutropenia (Kostmann syndrome) or the early phases of Shwachman-Diamond syndrome. Diamond-Blackfan anemia affects the erythroid lineage, not the myeloid lineage.
- **D)** Incorrect. Isolated thrombocytopenia with normal other lineages would suggest conditions such as congenital amegakaryocytic thrombocytopenia or thrombocytopenia-absent radius syndrome. This is not the pattern seen in Diamond-Blackfan anemia.

### Q10. Answer: C

- **A)** Incorrect. Epstein-Barr virus can cause various hematologic complications, including hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis, but it does not have specific tropism for erythroid progenitors and is not the classic cause of transient aplastic crisis in chronic hemolytic anemias.
- **B)** Incorrect. Cytomegalovirus can cause bone marrow suppression in immunocompromised patients but is not the agent responsible for the characteristic transient aplastic crisis seen in sickle cell disease and other chronic hemolytic anemias.
- **C)** Correct. Parvovirus B19 has specific tropism for erythroid progenitor cells, entering via the P antigen (globoside) receptor. It is directly cytotoxic to these precursors, causing a transient halt in red cell production. In patients with chronic hemolytic anemias who depend on high rates of erythropoiesis to compensate for ongoing destruction, this brief arrest causes a precipitous drop in hemoglobin (transient aplastic crisis).
- **D)** Incorrect. Streptococcus pneumoniae is an important pathogen in sickle cell disease due to functional asplenia, but it causes sepsis and pneumonia, not aplastic crisis. Pneumococcal infection does not specifically target erythroid progenitors.
