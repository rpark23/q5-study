**Q1.** A 25-year-old previously healthy woman presents with pancytopenia. Bone marrow biopsy shows a markedly hypocellular marrow with fat replacing hematopoietic tissue. There are no dysplastic features or blast cells. Which of the following is the most likely pathophysiologic mechanism?

- A) Clonal expansion of malignant hematopoietic cells crowding out normal precursors
- B) Autoimmune T-cell mediated destruction of hematopoietic stem cells
- C) Nutritional deficiency causing ineffective hematopoiesis
- D) Fibrosis of the marrow stroma from chronic inflammation

**Q2.** A 20-year-old with acquired aplastic anemia has a fully HLA-matched sibling donor. Which of the following treatment approaches offers the best long-term survival?

- C) Allogeneic bone marrow transplantation from the matched sibling

**Q10.** Pure red cell aplasia (PRCA) in adults can be associated with thymomas. A patient with a thymoma and isolated severe anemia (normal WBC and platelets) undergoes thymectomy with subsequent improvement in anemia. Which mechanism best explains the association between thymomas and PRCA?

- A) The thymoma produces erythropoietin antibodies that neutralize the hormone
- B) The thymoma generates abnormal T cells that specifically suppress erythroid progenitor differentiation
- C) Thymic epithelial cells compete with erythroid precursors for essential growth factors
- D) The thymoma directly invades the bone marrow and physically displaces erythroid precursors

## Answer Key

### Q1. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. Clonal expansion of malignant cells crowding out normal precursors describes the mechanism of leukemia or other marrow-infiltrating malignancies. In those conditions, the marrow would be hypercellular with blasts or abnormal clonal cells, not hypocellular with fat replacement as described in this case.
- **B)** Correct. Acquired aplastic anemia is most commonly an immune-mediated disease in which autoreactive cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CD8+ T cells) attack and destroy hematopoietic stem cells. This leads to progressive loss of marrow cellularity with replacement by fat, resulting in pancytopenia. The absence of dysplastic features or blast cells helps exclude myelodysplastic syndrome or leukemia, pointing to aplastic anemia.
- **C)** Incorrect. Nutritional deficiency (such as B12 or folate deficiency) causes ineffective erythropoiesis with a hypercellular marrow showing megaloblastic changes, not a hypocellular marrow replaced by fat. The marrow in nutritional deficiency is actively producing cells, but they are dying prematurely before release.
- **D)** Incorrect. Fibrosis of the marrow stroma describes myelofibrosis, which would show a fibrotic marrow with reticulin or collagen deposition on biopsy, often with extramedullary hematopoiesis (hepatosplenomegaly). This is distinctly different from the fat-replaced hypocellular marrow described here.

### Q2. Answer: C

- **A)** Incorrect. High-dose cyclophosphamide alone has been studied as a treatment for aplastic anemia, but it carries significant toxicity and is not considered the standard of care. It does not offer the same long-term cure rate as allogeneic transplant from a matched sibling donor in young patients.
- **B)** Incorrect. ATG plus cyclosporine is the standard immunosuppressive regimen for patients who lack a matched sibling donor or who are older (typically >40 years). It achieves a 70-80% response rate but is associated with risks of relapse and clonal evolution (MDS/AML), making it inferior to matched sibling transplant in young, eligible patients.
- **C)** Correct. Allogeneic bone marrow transplantation from an HLA-matched sibling donor is the treatment of choice for young patients (generally under 40) with severe aplastic anemia, offering long-term survival rates of 90-95%. It replaces the destroyed hematopoietic stem cell compartment with donor-derived stem cells, providing a definitive cure.
- **D)** Incorrect. Eltrombopag (a thrombopoietin receptor agonist) has shown promise as an adjunct to immunosuppressive therapy in aplastic anemia, stimulating residual stem cells to proliferate. However, it is not used as monotherapy and does not offer the curative potential of a matched sibling bone marrow transplant.

### Q3. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. ATG is not a myeloid growth factor; it is a polyclonal antibody preparation that depletes T lymphocytes. Cyclosporine does not stimulate erythropoiesis; it suppresses T-cell activation via calcineurin inhibition. The rationale for both agents is immunosuppression, not growth factor support.
- **B)** Correct. Acquired aplastic anemia is driven by autoreactive cytotoxic T cells that attack and destroy hematopoietic stem cells. ATG (antithymocyte globulin) is a polyclonal antibody that depletes T lymphocytes, directly removing the effector cells causing marrow destruction. Cyclosporine is a calcineurin inhibitor that suppresses T-cell activation and IL-2 production, providing ongoing immunosuppression to prevent the autoreactive T-cell response from recurring after ATG wears off. Together they achieve a 70-80% response rate.
- **C)** Incorrect. ATG targets T cells, not B cells. Aplastic anemia is primarily a T-cell mediated disease, not an antibody-mediated disease. Anti-stem cell antibodies are not the major pathogenic mechanism, and cyclosporine does not function by preventing antibody class switching.
- **D)** Incorrect. ATG does not directly stimulate stem cell proliferation; it works by removing the autoreactive T cells that are destroying the stem cells, allowing remaining stem cells to recover and repopulate the marrow. Cyclosporine does not prevent graft-versus-host-like reactions in this context; it suppresses the patient's own autoreactive T cells.

### Q4. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. Progressive renal failure is not a characteristic feature of Fanconi anemia. While renal anomalies (such as horseshoe kidney or ectopic kidney) can occur as part of the congenital malformation spectrum, progressive renal failure requiring transplantation is not the major long-term concern. Cancer risk is the most important discussion topic.
- **B)** Correct. Fanconi anemia (confirmed by the positive DEB chromosomal breakage test) carries a greater than 15% lifetime risk of developing malignancies, particularly acute myeloid leukemia (AML) and squamous cell carcinomas of the head, neck, and anogenital region. The underlying DNA crosslink repair defect predisposes to genomic instability and malignant transformation. Lifelong cancer surveillance is essential and must be discussed with the family.
- **C)** Incorrect. While many patients with Fanconi anemia do develop progressive bone marrow failure requiring transfusion support, this is not inevitable in all patients, and the timeline varies. More importantly, the cancer risk is the most critical long-term concern to discuss because of its life-threatening implications and the need for proactive surveillance.
- **D)** Incorrect. Autoimmune hemolytic anemia is not a recognized complication of Fanconi anemia. The hematologic complications of Fanconi anemia center on bone marrow failure (aplastic anemia) and malignant transformation (MDS/AML), not autoimmune cytopenias.

### Q5. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. Fanconi anemia involves defective DNA interstrand crosslink repair and is diagnosed by the DEB (diepoxybutane) chromosomal breakage test, not by telomere length analysis. Fanconi anemia presents with pancytopenia, short stature, and skeletal/organ anomalies (radial ray defects, cafe-au-lait spots) but does not feature the nail-skin-mucosal triad described here.
- **B)** Correct. The classic triad of dystrophic nails, reticular skin pigmentation, and oral leukoplakia is pathognomonic for dyskeratosis congenita. The underlying molecular defect is in telomere maintenance (a telomere biology disorder), leading to critically short telomeres that can be measured to confirm the diagnosis. Mutations in DKC1, TERC, TERT, and other telomere-related genes have been identified.
- **C)** Incorrect. Shwachman-Diamond syndrome involves defective ribosome biogenesis (usually SBDS gene mutations) and characteristically presents with neutropenia and exocrine pancreatic insufficiency. It does not produce the nail-skin-mucosal triad or short telomeres described in this case.
- **D)** Incorrect. Diamond-Blackfan anemia involves defective ribosomal protein production and presents as a pure red cell aplasia in infancy, not with the mucocutaneous triad and short telomeres described here. DBA is characterized by selective absence of erythroid precursors, elevated eADA, and elevated HbF.

### Q6. Answer: C

- **A)** Incorrect. Fanconi anemia presents with pancytopenia (all lineages affected), congenital anomalies (radial ray defects, cafe-au-lait spots, short stature), and is diagnosed by the DEB chromosomal breakage test. It does not characteristically feature exocrine pancreatic insufficiency, which is the key distinguishing feature in this case.
- **B)** Incorrect. Diamond-Blackfan anemia is a pure red cell aplasia, selectively affecting the erythroid lineage while sparing myeloid and megakaryocytic lineages. It would not present with neutropenia as the primary marrow finding and does not cause pancreatic dysfunction.
- **C)** Correct. Shwachman-Diamond syndrome is uniquely characterized by the combination of bone marrow failure (predominantly neutropenia, though other lineages can be affected) and exocrine pancreatic insufficiency causing fat malabsorption (steatorrhea). It is caused by mutations in genes involved in ribosome biogenesis (most commonly SBDS). This distinctive combination of neutropenia plus pancreatic dysfunction is not seen in other inherited bone marrow failure syndromes.
- **D)** Incorrect. Dyskeratosis congenita is a telomere biology disorder that presents with the classic mucocutaneous triad (dystrophic nails, reticular skin pigmentation, oral leukoplakia) and progressive bone marrow failure. It does not cause exocrine pancreatic insufficiency, which is the hallmark of Shwachman-Diamond syndrome.

### Q7. Answer: C

- **A)** Incorrect. Erythropoietin injections are not effective in Diamond-Blackfan anemia. The defect is intrinsic to the erythroid progenitors (ribosomal protein mutations), not a deficiency of erythropoietin. The remaining erythroid precursors are fundamentally unable to proliferate and differentiate normally, so providing more growth signal does not correct the problem.
- **B)** Incorrect. ATG plus cyclosporine is the standard immunosuppressive regimen for acquired aplastic anemia, which is an immune-mediated destruction of hematopoietic stem cells. Diamond-Blackfan anemia is an inherited ribosomal disorder, not an immune-mediated disease, so immunosuppression targeting T cells is not the appropriate therapy.
- **C)** Correct. Diamond-Blackfan anemia (DBA) is a congenital pure red cell aplasia caused by mutations in ribosomal protein genes (RPS19 being the most common). The clinical features in this case -- early infancy presentation, severe macrocytic anemia, reticulocytopenia, absent erythroid precursors with preserved other lineages, elevated eADA, elevated HbF, and thumb anomalies -- are classic for DBA. Approximately 60-80% of patients respond to corticosteroid therapy, which is the first-line treatment.
- **D)** Incorrect. Iron supplementation is not appropriate here. The anemia is not due to iron deficiency but to a fundamental defect in erythroid progenitor cell function from ribosomal protein mutations. In fact, patients with DBA who require chronic transfusions often develop iron overload and need iron chelation, not supplementation.

### Q8. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. This answer incorrectly swaps the diagnostic tests for Fanconi anemia and dyskeratosis congenita. Fanconi anemia is diagnosed by the DEB test (chromosomal breakage), not short telomeres. Dyskeratosis congenita is diagnosed by short telomeres, not the DEB test. Mixing up these associations could lead to missed or incorrect diagnoses.
- **B)** Correct. Fanconi anemia is diagnosed by the DEB (diepoxybutane) test, which shows increased chromosomal breakage reflecting the underlying DNA interstrand crosslink repair defect. Dyskeratosis congenita is diagnosed by demonstrating very short telomeres, consistent with the telomere biology disorder. Diamond-Blackfan anemia is supported by elevated erythrocyte adenosine deaminase (eADA) activity, along with elevated HbF and characteristic clinical features.
- **C)** Incorrect. This answer incorrectly assigns elevated HbF as the defining test for Fanconi anemia, elevated eADA for dyskeratosis congenita, and the DEB test for Diamond-Blackfan anemia. None of these pairings are correct. While HbF can be elevated in Fanconi anemia, it is not the defining diagnostic test; the DEB chromosomal breakage test is.
- **D)** Incorrect. This answer correctly pairs Fanconi anemia with the DEB test but incorrectly assigns elevated eADA to dyskeratosis congenita and short telomeres to Diamond-Blackfan anemia. Elevated eADA is characteristic of Diamond-Blackfan anemia, and short telomeres are the hallmark of dyskeratosis congenita.

### Q9. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. While patients with sickle cell disease are at increased risk for Streptococcus pneumoniae infections (due to functional asplenia), pneumococcal sepsis would not typically cause isolated severe anemia with reticulocytopenia. Sepsis-associated marrow suppression is usually less dramatic, and the markedly low reticulocyte count (0.1%) with the absence of bleeding or sequestration points to a specific erythroid progenitor toxin.
- **B)** Correct. Parvovirus B19 has specific tropism for erythroid progenitor cells, entering via the P antigen (globoside) receptor on their surface. It is directly cytotoxic to these precursors, causing a transient arrest of red cell production (transient aplastic crisis). In healthy individuals, the brief pause in erythropoiesis goes largely unnoticed, but in patients with chronic hemolytic anemias like sickle cell disease -- who depend on very high rates of red cell production to compensate for ongoing hemolysis -- the abrupt halt causes a precipitous drop in hemoglobin with absent reticulocytes.
- **C)** Incorrect. Epstein-Barr virus can cause various hematologic complications, but it does not typically cause isolated severe anemia with reticulocytopenia in sickle cell patients. EBV-associated hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis can suppress all lineages, but that is a different clinical picture from the selective red cell aplasia described here.
- **D)** Incorrect. Mycoplasma pneumoniae can cause cold agglutinin-mediated hemolytic anemia, but this would present with evidence of active hemolysis (elevated reticulocyte count, elevated LDH, spherocytes) rather than reticulocytopenia. The hallmark of the case -- a nearly absent reticulocyte count -- indicates a production problem, not accelerated destruction.

### Q10. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. Erythropoietin antibodies are associated with a rare form of PRCA that can occur after treatment with exogenous erythropoietin (anti-EPO antibody-mediated PRCA), but this is not the mechanism in thymoma-associated PRCA. Thymomas generate abnormal T cells, not anti-erythropoietin antibodies.
- **B)** Correct. Thymomas can generate aberrant T-cell populations that escape normal thymic selection. In thymoma-associated PRCA, these abnormal T cells specifically suppress erythroid progenitor differentiation in the bone marrow while sparing myeloid and megakaryocytic lineages (explaining the isolated anemia with normal WBC and platelets). Thymectomy removes the source of these pathologic T cells, allowing erythropoiesis to recover.
- **C)** Incorrect. Thymic epithelial cells do not compete with erythroid precursors for growth factors. The thymus and the bone marrow occupy different anatomic and physiologic niches. The pathology is mediated by aberrant T cells generated within the thymoma, not by competition for resources.
- **D)** Incorrect. Thymomas are mediastinal tumors that do not invade the bone marrow. The mechanism of PRCA in thymoma is immune-mediated (aberrant T-cell suppression of erythropoiesis), not physical displacement of marrow elements by tumor infiltration.
