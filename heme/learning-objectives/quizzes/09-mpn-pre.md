## Answer Key

### Q1. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. Malignant disorders of mature lymphocytes causing lymphadenopathy and lymphocytosis describe lymphoproliferative disorders such as CLL or lymphoma, not myeloproliferative neoplasms. MPNs involve myeloid lineage cells.
- **B)** Correct. Myeloproliferative neoplasms are clonal disorders arising from hematopoietic stem cells that are characterized by overproduction of one or more mature myeloid cell lineages. Unlike MDS, the hematopoiesis in MPNs is effective, meaning the cells are produced and released into the blood, leading to elevated counts (erythrocytosis, thrombocytosis, or leukocytosis) rather than cytopenias.
- **C)** Incorrect. Bone marrow failure syndromes with ineffective hematopoiesis and cytopenias describe myelodysplastic syndromes (MDS), not MPNs. The key distinction is that MPNs have effective hematopoiesis with elevated counts, while MDS has ineffective hematopoiesis with low counts.
- **D)** Incorrect. Congenital disorders of neutrophil function describe conditions such as chronic granulomatous disease or Chediak-Higashi syndrome. MPNs are acquired neoplastic disorders, not congenital functional defects.

### Q2. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. A translocation creating a fusion tyrosine kinase (BCR::ABL1) defines CML, but the JAK2 V617F mutation is not a translocation. It is a point mutation (valine to phenylalanine at position 617) in the JAK2 gene, and it is most characteristic of PV, not CML.
- **B)** Correct. JAK2 V617F is an acquired somatic point mutation that substitutes phenylalanine for valine at position 617 in the pseudokinase domain of JAK2. This causes constitutive activation of the JAK-STAT signaling pathway, driving cytokine-independent proliferation of myeloid cells. It is found in 95-98% of polycythemia vera, making it nearly diagnostic, and in 50-60% of essential thrombocythemia and primary myelofibrosis.
- **C)** Incorrect. Frameshift mutations in calreticulin (CALR) are the second most common driver mutations in ET and PMF, but the question asks about JAK2 V617F, which is a different mutation. CALR mutations are found in JAK2-negative cases of ET and PMF.
- **D)** Incorrect. Activating mutations in the thrombopoietin receptor (MPL) account for approximately 5% of ET and PMF cases. JAK2 V617F is a mutation in the JAK2 kinase itself, not in the MPL receptor, and is most frequently found in PV.

### Q3. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. Deletion of the long arm of chromosome 5, del(5q), is a cytogenetic abnormality associated with MDS (particularly a subtype responsive to lenalidomide) and adverse-risk AML. It is not the Philadelphia chromosome.
- **B)** Correct. The Philadelphia chromosome results from a reciprocal translocation between chromosomes 9 and 22, designated t(9;22)(q34;q11.2). This translocation fuses the BCR gene on chromosome 22 with the ABL1 gene on chromosome 9, creating the BCR::ABL1 fusion gene. The resulting fusion protein is a constitutively active tyrosine kinase that drives CML.
- **C)** Incorrect. The t(15;17) translocation produces the PML-RARalpha fusion protein and defines acute promyelocytic leukemia (APL), not CML. APL is a subtype of AML, and its translocation involves different chromosomes than the Philadelphia chromosome.
- **D)** Incorrect. Inversion of chromosome 16, inv(16), is a core-binding factor abnormality associated with favorable-risk AML. It is not the Philadelphia chromosome, which specifically refers to t(9;22).

### Q4. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. Induction, consolidation, and maintenance are phases of chemotherapy treatment for acute leukemia (such as AML or ALL), not phases of CML disease progression. The question asks about the natural history of untreated CML.
- **B)** Correct. The natural history of CML progresses through three phases: chronic phase (lasting 5-6 years, with <15% blasts and relatively stable disease), accelerated phase (lasting 6-9 months, with 15-30% blasts and signs of disease progression), and blast crisis (with >30% blasts, behaving like an acute leukemia with a median survival of only 3-6 months). TKI therapy has dramatically altered this natural history for the majority of patients.
- **C)** Incorrect. Pre-fibrotic phase, overt fibrosis phase, and leukemic transformation phase describe the progression of primary myelofibrosis, not CML. While CML and myelofibrosis are both MPNs, their disease progression patterns differ.
- **D)** Incorrect. Low-risk, intermediate-risk, and high-risk are categories used in prognostic scoring systems (such as the DIPSS for myelofibrosis or the R-IPSS for MDS), not the phases of CML natural history.

### Q5. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. Iron overload and hemochromatosis are not primary consequences of erythrocytosis in PV. In fact, PV patients undergoing therapeutic phlebotomy often develop iron deficiency, which is considered a beneficial effect that helps limit further red cell production.
- **B)** Correct. The primary clinical concern in PV is the risk of arterial and venous thrombosis caused by increased blood viscosity from the elevated red cell mass. Thrombotic events (stroke, MI, DVT, portal vein thrombosis, Budd-Chiari syndrome) are a major cause of morbidity and mortality. The risk of thrombosis increases exponentially when the hematocrit exceeds 55%.
- **C)** Incorrect. PV does not cause severe immunosuppression or opportunistic infections. The clonal myeloid cells in PV are functional, and the disease does not typically impair immune defense in the way that MDS or leukemia does.
- **D)** Incorrect. PV does not transform to ALL. If transformation occurs, it is to myelofibrosis (post-PV myelofibrosis) or AML, and the overall risk is relatively low (<5% at 10-15 years). Transformation to a lymphoid malignancy is not a feature of PV.

### Q6. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. A reactive elevation of platelets driven by iron deficiency, chronic inflammation, or infection describes secondary (reactive) thrombocytosis, not essential thrombocythemia. ET is a primary clonal neoplasm, and the diagnosis requires exclusion of reactive causes.
- **B)** Correct. Essential thrombocythemia is a clonal myeloproliferative neoplasm characterized by sustained thrombocytosis (typically platelets >450,000/uL) due to megakaryocytic hyperplasia in the bone marrow. Driver mutations include JAK2 V617F (50-60%), CALR (~25%), and MPL (~5%). Clinical complications include both thrombosis and hemorrhage (especially with extreme thrombocytosis >1,500,000/uL due to acquired von Willebrand syndrome).
- **C)** Incorrect. Immune-mediated platelet destruction causing thrombocytopenia describes immune thrombocytopenic purpura (ITP), which involves low (not high) platelet counts and is mediated by autoantibodies, not T-lymphocyte malignancy. ET is the opposite: it causes elevated platelet counts.
- **D)** Incorrect. Post-splenectomy thrombocytosis is a reactive phenomenon that occurs because the spleen normally sequesters approximately one-third of circulating platelets. While post-splenectomy thrombocytosis is usually transient, ET is a persistent clonal condition that does not resolve on its own.

### Q7. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. Marked lymphocytosis with smudge cells is the hallmark peripheral blood finding of chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL), not myelofibrosis. CLL involves mature B-lymphocytes, whereas myelofibrosis is a myeloid disorder.
- **B)** Correct. Primary myelofibrosis is characterized by a leukoerythroblastic blood picture, meaning both immature myeloid cells (myelocytes, metamyelocytes) and nucleated red blood cells are found in the peripheral blood. Teardrop cells (dacrocytes) are the signature morphologic finding, formed as red cells are distorted while squeezing through the fibrotic bone marrow. These findings reflect the displacement of hematopoiesis from the fibrotic marrow to extramedullary sites.
- **C)** Incorrect. Isolated thrombocytosis with giant platelets and no other abnormalities better describes essential thrombocythemia (ET), not myelofibrosis. Myelofibrosis has a much more complex blood picture involving multiple cell lineage abnormalities.
- **D)** Incorrect. Pancytopenia with circulating blasts exceeding 20% describes acute leukemia (AML), not myelofibrosis. While myelofibrosis can transform to AML, the primary blood picture of myelofibrosis shows the characteristic leukoerythroblastic pattern rather than a blast-predominant picture.

### Q8. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. A monoclonal antibody targeting CD20 describes rituximab, which is used in B-cell lymphomas and CLL. Imatinib is a small-molecule kinase inhibitor, not a monoclonal antibody, and it targets BCR::ABL1, not CD20.
- **B)** Correct. Imatinib is an ATP-mimetic small-molecule inhibitor that binds to the ATP-binding pocket of the ABL kinase domain within the BCR::ABL1 fusion protein. By occupying this pocket, it prevents ATP binding and substrate phosphorylation, effectively shutting down the constitutive kinase activity that drives CML. It was the first targeted kinase inhibitor approved for cancer therapy (FDA approved 2001) and revolutionized CML treatment.
- **C)** Incorrect. Hypomethylating agents (azacitidine, decitabine) are used in MDS and AML, not CML. Imatinib does not affect DNA methylation; it directly inhibits the BCR::ABL1 tyrosine kinase.
- **D)** Incorrect. JAK2 inhibitors (such as ruxolitinib) are used in PV and myelofibrosis, not CML. Imatinib targets BCR::ABL1, which is a distinct kinase from JAK2. While both are tyrosine kinases, they are involved in different diseases.

### Q9. Answer: B

- **A)** Incorrect. TFR does not refer to non-pharmacologic remission using radiation. In CML, TFR specifically means stopping oral TKI medication after achieving a sustained deep molecular response. Radiation therapy is not a primary treatment modality for CML.
- **B)** Correct. Treatment-free remission refers to the ability of select CML patients who have achieved a sustained deep molecular response (BCR::ABL1 undetectable by sensitive PCR for an extended period) to discontinue their TKI therapy and remain in molecular remission without any ongoing treatment. Clinical trials (STIM1, EURO-SKI) have shown that approximately 40-50% of carefully selected patients can maintain TFR, with close molecular monitoring to detect any recurrence.
- **C)** Incorrect. A period of observation before starting treatment describes a "watch-and-wait" approach, which is used in CLL and some indolent lymphomas. CML requires treatment initiation upon diagnosis; TFR refers to stopping treatment after achieving deep remission, not delaying initial treatment.
- **D)** Incorrect. Drug holidays between chemotherapy cycles are not what TFR describes. TFR in CML is the sustained discontinuation of all TKI therapy in patients who have achieved and maintained deep molecular responses, with the goal of long-term medication-free remission.

### Q10. Answer: C

- **A)** Incorrect. Ruxolitinib is a JAK1/JAK2 inhibitor that effectively reduces splenomegaly and constitutional symptoms in myelofibrosis, but it does not eliminate the malignant clone or cure the disease. It provides important symptomatic benefit but its effects are largely palliative.
- **B)** Incorrect. Hydroxyurea is a cytoreductive agent that can reduce elevated blood counts in MPNs, but it does not cure myelofibrosis. It may be used for temporary control of leukocytosis or thrombocytosis but has no disease-modifying effect on the underlying fibrosis.
- **C)** Correct. Allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplant is the only therapy with curative potential for primary myelofibrosis. By replacing the patient's diseased hematopoietic system with a donor's healthy stem cells, transplant can eliminate the malignant clone and reverse marrow fibrosis. However, it carries significant morbidity and mortality and is reserved for intermediate-2 and high-risk patients who are fit enough for the procedure.
- **D)** Incorrect. Interferon-alpha can be used in some MPNs (particularly PV and ET) and may reduce the JAK2 V617F allele burden, but it is not curative for myelofibrosis and does not have the same efficacy as stem cell transplant in this disease.
