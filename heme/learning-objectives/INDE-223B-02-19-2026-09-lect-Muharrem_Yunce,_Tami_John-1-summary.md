## Immunohematology

- The study of antigens and antibodies associated with blood transfusion
- An **antigen** elicits formation of an **antibody** (water-soluble plasma globular protein)
- Safe transfusion of blood products is the main focus
- **RBC antigen and plasma antibody** interaction results in coating and destruction of RBCs (hemolysis) which may be:
  - Mild to severe
  - Extravascular or intravascular
- Rapid RBC destruction releases free hemoglobin, potentially causing renal failure, DIC, and death

## RBC Surface Antigens

RBC antigens are arranged in **45+ systems** and fall into two categories:

### 1. Carbohydrate Antigens
- **ABO** (most important)

### 2. Protein Antigens
- **Rh** (D, C, c, E, e)
- Duffy (Fy^a, Fy^b)
- MNSs
- Kell (K, k)
- Kidd (Jk^a, Jk^b)
- Several others

## ABO Blood Group System

The most important blood group system in transfusion practice.

### Landsteiner's Rule
"Under normal circumstances, an antigen and its corresponding antibody do not coexist."
- Type A: has A antigen, makes anti-B antibody
- Type B: has B antigen, makes anti-A antibody
- Type O: has neither antigen, makes both anti-A and anti-B → **Universal RBC donor**
- Type AB: has both antigens, makes neither antibody → **Universal plasma donor**

### ABO Typing
- **Forward grouping:** Using known antisera (antibodies) to detect antigens on patient's red cells
- **Reverse grouping:** Using reagent cells with known ABO antigens to test patient's serum for ABO antibodies
- Forward and reverse typing must agree

## Pre-Transfusion Testing (Compatibility Testing & Unit Selection)

Steps include:
1. Review patient's past transfusion history (antibodies, transfusion problems, recent transfusion/pregnancy, medications, diagnosis, history of HDFN)
2. ABO and Rh typing of recipient and donor
3. Antibody screen of the patient's serum/plasma
4. Product selection
5. RBC crossmatch

## Indirect Antiglobulin Test (IAT)

- Used for ABO/Rh grouping, antibody screen & identification, crossmatch, and antigen typing (minor antigens)
- Detects antigen-antibody reactions occurring **in vitro**
- Requires incubation period (15-30 min) for antibody binding
- Requires use of **Anti-Human Globulin (AHG, or Coombs) reagent**

### IAT in Crossmatching
1. Recipient's serum (containing antibodies) is obtained
2. Donor's blood sample is added to the tube with serum
3. Recipient's Ig's that target donor's RBCs form antibody-antigen complexes
4. Anti-human Ig's (Coombs antibodies) are added
5. Agglutination of RBCs occurs if human Ig's are attached → **positive test result**

---

## Antibody Screening

- Patient serum/plasma tested against **three standard type O red cells** of known antigenic phenotype
- **No agglutination** → no detectable alloantibody
- **Agglutination or hemolysis** → presence of antibody
- Detection of antibodies outside of the ABO system
- Not expected unless patient has history of transfusion, transplant, or pregnancy
- >95% of patients have a negative antibody screen
- Must avoid transfusing RBCs with antigen(s) corresponding to the patient's antibody

### Antibody Identification
- Uses an extended panel of typed RBCs to identify specific antibody based on reactivity patterns

---

## Direct Antiglobulin Test (DAT) - Differentiate from IAT

- **DAT (Direct Coombs test):** Detects antibodies already bound to patient's RBCs **in vivo**
  1. Blood sample from patient with immune-mediated hemolytic anemia (antibodies already on RBC surface)
  2. Patient's washed RBCs are incubated with anti-human antibodies (Coombs reagent)
  3. RBCs agglutinate if antibodies are present on the surface
- **IAT** = in vitro; **DAT** = in vivo

### Product Selection & Crossmatch
- Historically blood products were serologically crossmatched with patient sample
- Now reserved for patients with alloantibodies (<5% of patients)
- All other products go through an **electronic crossmatch**

## Blood Components for Transfusion

Four main components: **Red Blood Cells, Plasma, Platelets, Cryoprecipitate**

### Red Blood Cells
- 42-day shelf life
- Hematocrit of 60-70%
- Stored at cold temperatures (1-6°C)
- Transfused to support oxygen delivery during acute bleeding and chronic anemia
- **Transfusion trigger:** Hgb <7 g/dL (commonly used, but individual evaluation recommended)
  - Hgb <7: Risk of inadequate O₂ delivery to critical tissues
  - Expected increment: ~1 g/dL per unit

### Plasma (Fresh Frozen Plasma - FFP)
- Frozen at -18°C for up to 1 year
- Available in liquid form for trauma and massive surgical bleeds (heat labile factors V, VIII decrease)
- Supports **secondary hemostasis** in bleeding patients
- Should NOT be used to correct lab coagulation abnormalities in a non-bleeding patient
- Transfusing FFP to correct INR <2 is usually ineffective and counterproductive
- Recombinant factor replacements exist for most single factor deficiencies

### Platelets
- Shelf life of **5 days**
- Stored at near room temperature with constant agitation
- Historically highest rate of bacterial contamination (now pathogen reduced at Stanford)
- Supports **primary hemostasis**
- **Transfusion thresholds:**
  - <10 K/uL in stable non-bleeding patients
  - <50 K/uL in bleeding patients or pre-procedure
- Expected increment: 30-50 K/uL

### Cryoprecipitate
- Prepared by thawing FFP at 1-4°C → high molecular weight proteins precipitate → centrifuge and discard supernatant
- Stored at -18°C, thawed on demand
- **Major components:** Fibrinogen, Factor VIII, von Willebrand Factor, Factor XIII
- Primary use: **replete fibrinogen**
- Transfuse for fibrinogen <100 mg/dL
- Expected increment: 50-100 mg/dL

### Summary of Transfusion Guidelines

| Component | Threshold | Expected Increment |
|-----------|-----------|-------------------|
| RBC | Hgb <7 g/dL | 1 g/dL |
| Platelets | Plt <10 K/uL (stable); <50 K/uL (bleeding/procedure) | 30-50 K/uL |
| FFP | Bleeding patient | — |
| Cryo | Fibrinogen <100 mg/dL | 50-100 mg/dL |

## Transfusion Reactions

### When to Suspect
- Any new symptom or significant change in vital signs during or within 24 hours after a transfusion

### What to Do
1. **STOP THE TRANSFUSION** immediately
2. Assess symptoms, vitals, brief exam
3. If ONLY symptoms are hives/pruritis/flushing → ok to resume after medicating and resolution
4. ANY other situation → send a transfusion reaction evaluation
5. If suspect AHTR, TRALI, septic reaction → page Transfusion MD ASAP

### Common Reactions

| Reaction | Manifestations | Pathophysiology | Severity |
|----------|---------------|-----------------|----------|
| **TACO** | Hypoxemia & increased BP | Volume overload | Mild to fatal |
| **Non-severe allergic** | Hives, pruritis | Reaction to donor plasma proteins | Mild |
| **Febrile non-hemolytic** | Fever, chills, rigors | Cytokine release from donor WBCs | Mild/moderate |

### Rare Reactions

| Reaction | Manifestations | Pathophysiology | Severity |
|----------|---------------|-----------------|----------|
| **Acute hemolytic** | Fever, hypotension | ABO incompatibility | Mild to fatal |
