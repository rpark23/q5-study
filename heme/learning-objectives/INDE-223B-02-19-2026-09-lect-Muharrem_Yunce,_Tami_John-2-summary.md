## Learning Objectives

### 1. Biological Basis of HCT
Understand the biological basis of hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HCT), including the history of bone marrow transplantation and the role of pluripotent hematopoietic stem cells.

### 2. Applications and Diseases Treated by HCT
Know the various applications of HCT and diseases for which it is effective, including malignant (leukemias, lymphomas, MDS) and non-malignant (inherited disorders, aplastic anemia) indications.

### 3. Donor Selection and Stem Cell Sources
Understand donor selection and hematopoietic stem cell (HSC) sources, including HLA typing/matching, donor hierarchy, and the three stem cell sources (bone marrow, mobilized peripheral blood, umbilical cord blood).

### 4. Benefits and Challenges of Allogeneic HCT
Understand the major benefits and challenges of allogeneic HCT, including stages of transplant, infection risk, and graft-versus-host disease (GVHD).

### 5. Gene Therapies
Recognize major categories and applications of gene therapies, including in vivo gene transfer and ex vivo gene modification (CAR-T, HSC-based gene therapy).

### 6. Limitations of Gene Therapies
Know the limitations of gene therapies currently in common practice, including manufacturing challenges, antigen escape, and toxicities (CRS, ICANS).

## History of Bone Marrow Transplantation

- **1940s:** Initial observations that radiation results in toxicity and death
- **1942:** Shielding spleen resulted in radioprotection
- **1949:** Transfer of bone marrow cells conferred radioprotection
- **1950s:** BMT recipients able to accept skin graft from donor
- **1956:** First successful BMT by Dr. E. Donnall Thomas
- **1958:** Discovery of human leukocyte antigens (HLA) by Dr. Jean Dausset
- **1968:** First BMT with a matched RELATED donor (University of Minnesota) for SCID
- **1970s:** Autologous transplant developed
- **1976:** First BMT with an UNRELATED donor (Memorial Sloan Kettering)

## Biological Basis

### Hematopoietic Stem Cells (HSC)
- Pluripotent cells that repopulate all blood elements
- Bone marrow niche houses long-term HSCs that mature to:
  - **Common Myeloid Progenitor (CMP):** matures to RBCs, platelets, granulocytes/neutrophils, monocytes
  - **Common Lymphoid Progenitor (CLP):** matures to B and T lymphocytes

### Types of Transplant
- **Autologous:** Stem cells obtained from SELF
  - Uses: Hematopoietic rescue after high-dose chemotherapy; hematopoietic target for gene therapies
- **Allogeneic:** Stem cells from a NON-SELF donor
  - Uses: Replacement of diseased marrow for hematologic malignancies and inherited conditions affecting bone marrow/blood cells

## Diseases Treated with HCT

### Allogeneic Transplant - Malignant
- Acute leukemias (ALL, AML)
- Chronic leukemias (CML, CLL)
- Myelodysplastic syndromes
- High-risk lymphomas

### Allogeneic Transplant - Non-Malignant
- Inherited disorders affecting bone marrow:
  - Inborn errors of immunity (SCID, CGD, WAS, LAD)
  - Inborn errors of metabolism (Hurler, leukodystrophies, cALD)
  - Red cell disorders (sickle cell disease, thalassemia)
  - Inherited bone marrow failure syndromes (Fanconi Anemia, DBA, DKC, congenital neutropenia, SDS)
- Idiopathic severe aplastic anemia
- Hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis

### Autologous Transplant
- Multiple myeloma (most common adult indication)
- Non-Hodgkin and Hodgkin's lymphoma
- Solid tumors (neuroblastoma, brain tumors, refractory germ cell tumors)
- HSC-based gene therapies (growing number of diseases)

### Most Common Indications
- **Adults:** Auto = myeloma; Allo = acute leukemias/MDS
- **Pediatrics:** Auto = neuroblastoma/solid tumors; Allo = acute leukemias, inherited diseases; Gene therapy = SCD, thalassemia, PID, metabolic

## Donor Selection

### HLA Typing and Matching
- **HLA region** located on Chromosome 6, encodes Class I, II, III glycoproteins
  - **Class I MHC:** expressed on nearly all nucleated cells + platelets; present endogenous peptide antigens to CD8+ T cells
  - **Class II MHC:** expressed on APCs (macrophages, dendritic cells, B cells); present exogenous antigenic peptides to CD4+ T cells
  - **Class III MHC:** complement system components and inflammatory molecules

### Donor Hierarchy
1. **Matched sibling donor (MSD)** - GOLD STANDARD
2. HLA Matched unrelated (MUD), Mismatched unrelated (MMUD)
3. Mismatched related/Haploidentical (Haplo)
4. Unrelated Umbilical Cord Blood (UCB)

### Other Considerations
1. Younger > older
2. Male > Female (if female, lower or no parity preferred)
3. ABO matched > minor mismatched > major mismatched
4. CMV immune matched status

### HLA Inheritance
- Full chromosome inherited as one haplotype from each parent
- Each sibling: HLA matched 25%, haplotype matched 50%, disparate 25%

### Thymic Education
- Thymocytes educated via positive and negative selection
- **Positive selection:** Low-affinity to thymic cortical epithelial cells → cells survive → mature into Treg and CD4/CD8 T cells
- **Negative selection:** High-affinity to thymic medullary epithelial and BM-derived cells → cells destroyed

## Stem Cell Sources

### 1. Bone Marrow Harvest
- Donor sedated in OR; bone marrow aspirated from posterior superior iliac crest bilaterally
- ~20 ml/kg of recipient removed; max 20 ml/kg from donor
- Processed: volume reduced, RBC reduced (if ABO mismatched), infection and quality tested

### 2. Mobilized Peripheral Blood
- Donor stimulated with G-CSF +/- plerixafor to mobilize stem cells from marrow to peripheral blood
- PBSCs collected via apheresis
- Can be processed for CD34 selection, T cell depletion, gene therapy manufacturing

### 3. Umbilical Cord Blood
- Collected at child's birth (in utero or ex utero)
- Processed, cryopreserved, and stored in a bank

### Comparison of Stem Cell Sources

| Feature | Autologous | Bone Marrow | Peripheral Blood | Cord Blood |
|---------|-----------|-------------|-----------------|------------|
| GVHD risk | None | ~25% | Highest (chronic) | Lowest; can be more mismatched |
| Non-engraftment risk | Low | Low | Lowest | Highest |
| Treatment limits | No GVL effect | Preferred in peds; priority for non-malignant | Used often in adults | Fixed dose |
| Other | Gene therapies | Consent for sedated harvest | Mobilization + apheresis | One-time donor capacity |

## Stages of Allogeneic Transplantation

### I. Conditioning (Preparation)
- 7-10 days of chemotherapy, radiation, and immune therapy
- Purpose: immune ablation and myelotoxicity

### II. Transplantation
- IV infusion of stem cells

### III. Engraftment
- Time to blood count recovery
- Neutrophil engraftment: 2-4 weeks post-infusion (ANC >500/uL x3)
- Platelet recovery can take weeks to months

### IV. Recovery
- GVHD monitoring and prevention
- Infection monitoring and control
- Hematopoietic recovery and immune reconstitution

## Complications

### Autologous HCT
- Regimen-related toxicity, infection, relapse/progression

### Allogeneic HCT
- Regimen and transplant-related toxicity, infectious complications, relapse, failure to engraft, and **GVHD** (can be fatal)

### Infection Risk Timeline
- **Pre-engraftment/30 days:** Neutropenia → bacterial, fungal (Candida, early Aspergillus), HSV
- **Day 90-100:** Infections, transplant-related factors; CMV, Adenovirus
- **Day 180:** Infections, chemo toxicity; late Aspergillus, VZV
- **1 year:** GVHD, infections; encapsulated bacteria, Pneumocystis
- **Long term:** GVHD, chronic infections if with chronic immune suppression

## Graft-Versus-Host Disease (GVHD)

### Pathophysiology
1. Host tissue injury (chemo/radiation) → inflammatory mediators
2. Host APCs present antigen to donor T cells → T cell activation and expansion
3. Effector cells (T cells, macrophages) cause tissue injury

### Risk Factors
- HLA disparity, sex mismatch, donor parity, older age, high T cell dose in cell product, stem cell source (PB > BM > CB)

### Mortality: 10-25%

### Acute GVHD
- Classically within first 100 days (or later with weaning of immune suppression)
- **Skin:** Maculopapular lesions → erythroderma → skin peeling (resembles TEN)
- **GI tract:** Patchy ulcerations → complete mucosal defacement; secretory diarrhea

### Chronic GVHD
- Classically after 100 days
- Most important cause of morbidity following allogeneic HCT
- Can affect any organ; causes irreversible functional deficits
- Median 2-3 years of significant immune suppression; <80% able to come off completely
- **Phases:** (I) T-cell alloreactivity and aberrant innate immunity → (II) Dysregulated immunity drives chronic inflammation → (III) Fibrosis and aberrant tissue repair
- **Affected organs:** Skin (poikiloderma, lichen planus-like, morphea-like, sclerosis), mouth (lichen-type, hyperkeratotic plaques), eyes, GI tract, lung (bronchiolitis obliterans), muscle/fascia/joints, liver

### GVHD Management
- **Prophylaxis:** Donor and graft selection; 3-12 months of immune suppression (calcineurin inhibitors: tacrolimus/cyclosporine); post-transplant cyclophosphamide increasingly common
- **Treatment:** First-line = high-dose corticosteroids (~50% completely respond); multimodal targeted therapies for steroid-refractory (JAK inhibitors, kinase inhibitors, cytokine blockers, photopheresis, mesenchymal cells, antifibrotics)

## Cellular-Based Therapies

### Gene Therapies
- Medical technique that treats, prevents, or transforms disease via altering genetic material
- Gene editing and integration techniques allow modifying, replacing, or inactivating genes

### Types of Gene Therapies

#### In Vivo Gene Transfer
- Gene packaged in viral vector (e.g., AAV) delivered directly to target tissue via IV infusion/injection
- **Example indications:** Spinal muscular atrophy, retinitis pigmentosa, hemophilia B, Duchenne's muscular dystrophy
- **Limitations:** Limited packaging capacity, immunogenicity, inability to transfect non-dividing cells, target tissue tolerance

#### Ex Vivo Gene Modification
- **CAR-T cells:** Genetically modified to recognize specific tumor antigens → T-cell activation and proliferation → cytotoxic durable destruction of cancer
- **Other cellular therapies:** CAR-NK cells, TCR-transduced T cells, tumor antigen-specific CTLs
- **Autologous HSC gene therapy:** For beta-thalassemia, SCID-X1, ADA-SCID, WAS, MLD, sickle cell disease

### CAR-T Cell Therapy Process
1. Collection of patient's T cells by leukapheresis
2. Enrichment and activation of T cells
3. Transduction with lentiviral CAR construct
4. Expansion of CAR-expressing T cells
5. Isolation of final product
6. Lymphodepleting conditioning chemotherapy → administration of CAR T cells

### Limitations of CAR-T Cell Therapy
1. CAR failure: manufacturing, function, persistence issues
2. Loss of target antigen on tumor cells → antigen-negative relapse
3. **Toxicities:** Cytokine Release Syndrome (CRS), Immune Effector Cell-Associated Neurotoxicity Syndrome (ICANS) - often self-limiting but can be severe
4. Ongoing need for treatments for solid tumors and diseases with less obvious/more toxic targets
